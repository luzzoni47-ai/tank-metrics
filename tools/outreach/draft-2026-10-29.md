Title: How SBI actually calculates your fixed deposit interest

Body:

A bank FD pays compound interest, not simple interest, and that is the part
most people get wrong when they estimate the maturity amount by hand. SBI
compounds quarterly on most fixed deposits. So a rate quoted as "6.8% per
annum" is really 6.8 ÷ 4 = 1.7% applied four times a year, and each quarter's
interest starts earning in the next quarter.

That compounding is why the effective annual yield is higher than the headline
rate. Rs 1,00,000 at 6.8% for one year gives roughly Rs 1,06,98, not Rs 1,06,800
— the extra comes from compounding. Stretch it to five years and the gap
widens, because the effect compounds on itself every quarter.

Two things move the answer: senior citizens get an extra 0.50% on most
tenures, and tax is deducted at source once yearly interest crosses Rs 50,000
(Rs 1,00,000 for seniors). Both change what you actually receive.

— it shows the quarterly steps so you can check the maths by hand.
