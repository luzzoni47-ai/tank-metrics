Title: What is exit load, and how much does it actually cost you if you redeem early?

Body:

Exit load is the fee a mutual fund charges when you redeem units before a set
holding period ends. The usual Indian structure is 1% within 12 months and nil
after, but it is not one rule for everyone: liquid funds use a graded slab
(0.0070% on day 1, zero by day 7) and some equity schemes use 0.5% for 90 days.
Read your scheme's clause rather than assuming the 1%-for-a-year figure.

Exit load is charged on the redemption value, not on your gains. Redeem
Rs 50,000 inside the load period at 1% and Rs 500 is deducted before the money
reaches you — you get Rs 49,500. If a Rs 50,000 investment grew to Rs 55,000 in
eight months, a 1% load takes Rs 550, more than a tenth of the Rs 5,000 gain.

The trap is comparing funds on returns alone. Two schemes can post the same
three-year return and leave you with different amounts if one has a long load
period and you move money often. Hold a long SIP and the load rarely matters;
pull out within a year and it can wipe out most of the return.

shows the steps so you can check the maths by hand.
