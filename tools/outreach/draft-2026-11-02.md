Title: Why "highest RD rate" is the wrong thing to shop for

Body:

A recurring deposit rate is not one number — it moves with the tenure. Most
banks pay roughly 6.5% to 7% for a 12-month RD and step it up for longer
tenures, and the small finance banks (AU, Ujjivan, Equitas, Suryoday) usually
quote 0.5% to 1% above the large private banks across every tenure. So "the
highest RD rate" is only ever a rate for a specific month count.

Two things matter more than the headline. First, the monthly instalment is
compounded quarterly, not monthly — a 24-month RD at 7% on Rs 5,000 a month
matures at about Rs 1,29,000, and the quarterly convention is what sets that
figure. Second, RD interest is taxed at your slab every year, and the bank
deducts TDS once a year's interest crosses Rs 40,000, so your post-tax return
is lower than the number the board shows.

Compare the maturity value for your exact tenure and amount, not the rate.

deposits, maturity value and interest earned side by side.
