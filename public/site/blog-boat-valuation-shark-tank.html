What is boat valuation in shark tank?

boAt's parent company is Imagine Marketing. At its 2025 IPO filing, the company was valued at roughly ₹15,000 crore, on yearly revenue of about ₹3,000 crore. Aman Gupta and Sameer Mehta built it from 2016 into India's largest audio and wearable brand. That is a story of scale, not of a single cheque.

When a Shark Tank India founder asks for ₹10 crore, boAt at ₹15,000 crore is the other end of the scale. This calculator lets you put a revenue figure and a multiple together to see the same kind of valuation the market gives a real Indian company. The point is simple: valuation comes from revenue and profit, not from a story.

The calculator also exposes an uncomfortable truth. A brand with ₹1 crore of revenue at a 5x multiple is worth ₹5 crore, no matter how good the story is. Revenue is the input the market cannot argue with, and it is the one a founder can actually grow.

For a founder the lesson is to grow the input the multiple multiplies. A brand at ₹10 crore of revenue does not need a cleverer story to reach ₹50 crore of value at a 5x multiple; it needs five times the revenue. That is a harder but far more honest path than arguing for a higher multiple.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

In India, hardware and D2C brands usually trade at a lower revenue multiple than US brands, because margins are thinner and competition is fierce. boAt's multiple sits in single digits for exactly that reason, while a US audio brand can command a premium. Copying a US multiple overstates an Indian valuation.

An Indian valuation also has to stand up to a chartered accountant and SEBI rules, and deals are often structured as CCPS. US tools work on 409A and on dollars, which do not translate cleanly. Tank Metrics keeps everything in ₹ Crores so the number is one an Indian investor will recognise.

Indian hardware brands also carry higher working capital and import costs, which pull the multiple down further. A brand that imports components pays customs duty and IGST before it sells a single unit, so the revenue that reaches the multiple is already carrying that drag.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying a US software multiple to an Indian hardware brand, which overstates the valuation badly.
  • Counting gross order value as revenue. Only net revenue, after returns and discounts, belongs in the formula.
  • Using one unusually good month as the yearly revenue figure.
