Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between pre-money and post-money valuation?

Post-money is the company's value after the investment; pre-money is post-money minus the investment. For ₹1 crore at 10%, post-money is ₹10 crore and pre-money is ₹9 crore.

Which valuation do Indian term sheets use?

Indian term sheets are normally written in pre-money valuation, while founders and TV pitches usually quote post-money. Always check which one a number refers to before agreeing.

Is the equity percentage calculated on pre-money or post-money?

Always on post-money. The investor's ₹1 crore is part of the ₹10 crore post-money company, so 10% is 10% of ₹10 crore, not of ₹9 crore.

In Shark Tank India Season 3, a founder said he was valued at ₹4 crore. A shark offered ₹1 crore for 20% and corrected him on camera: the post-money valuation is ₹5 crore, not ₹4 crore. The founder had quoted the pre-money number and forgotten that the cheque gets added on top. It is the single most common valuation mistake Indian founders make on TV - and in term sheets. What is pre money vs post money valuation india?

Pre-money and post-money are the two valuations of the same deal, separated by the investment. Post-money is what the company is worth after the cheque lands; pre-money is post-money minus the cheque. The investor's equity is always a slice of the post-money number, never the pre-money one.

Get the order wrong and the deal is mispriced. Offer ₹1 crore for 10% and the post-money is ₹10 crore; the pre-money is ₹9 crore. A founder who quotes ₹9 crore as the valuation has just handed the investor an extra ₹1 crore of value for free.

Every term sheet in India is written in pre-money, and every shark on TV speaks in post-money, which is exactly why the two get mixed up. This calculator keeps both in front of you at once, in ₹ Crores.

Formula

The formulaPost-Money = (Investment ÷ Equity) × 100; Pre-Money = Post-Money − Investment

The equity percentage is always taken on the post-money valuation. ₹1 crore for 10% gives a ₹10 crore post-money and a ₹9 crore pre-money. Quote the pre-money in the term sheet, but do the division on post-money.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Indian term sheets are written in pre-money valuation, but Indian TV and most founders speak in post-money. The gap between the two is the cheque itself, and quoting the wrong one can cost a founder crores in a single conversation.

In India the equity is usually issued as CCPS (Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares), and the valuation often needs a chartered accountant or a SEBI-registered merchant banker to certify it. That is a different system from the US 409A valuation, and it makes the pre-money / post-money distinction a legal one, not just a maths one.

Indian early rounds are frequently raised from family and friends without a written valuation. When a later angel asks for equity, this calculator is how you turn that informal history into a pre-money and post-money number you can defend in front of your CA.

Common Mistakes

  • Quoting the pre-money valuation as if it were the post-money, which gives the investor more equity than the deal intended.
  • Applying the equity percentage to the pre-money number. It is always applied to post-money.
  • Forgetting the ESOP pool, which is usually created before the round and sits inside the pre-money, quietly diluting founders.
