Frequently asked questions

How much salary should a founder take in India?

Enough to live on, but small enough to leave the business room to grow. A common guide is to keep it under half of annual profit so the company can still reinvest.

Is founder salary tax deductible?

Yes. A salary paid to a founder-director is a business expense that reduces the company's taxable profit, and it is taxed in the founder's own hands as salary income.

Should a founder take salary or dividends?

Salary is deductible for the company and taxed at your slab, while dividends are paid from post-tax profit and taxed again in your hands. A chartered accountant can compare the two for your situation.

Founders on Shark Tank India are asked one personal question almost every time, what salary do you draw? The honest answers are all over the place. Some founders take nothing, others pay themselves a modest ₹50,000 a month, and a few admit to a comfortable figure once the business is profitable. Bamboo India took a deal at ₹50 lakh for 3.5% equity plus ₹30 lakh of debt, and the sharks pressed on whether the founders were drawing a salary from a business of that size. What is founder salary calculator startup india?

A founder salary calculator startup India use turns two numbers into a decision. The first is the monthly take-home you actually need to live on, and the second is the annual profit the company can genuinely spare. Multiply your take-home by twelve to get the salary the company must pay, then compare it with profit. If the salary is a small slice of profit, the business can afford it, but if it eats most of the profit, you are funding your lifestyle from the company's runway.

The calculator also shows the largest salary that still looks healthy, taken as half of annual profit, so the business keeps enough to reinvest. In a young Indian startup this balance matters more than the headline number, because every rupee paid as salary is a rupee not spent on growth or left as a buffer.

Founder salary is also a tax question, not just a cash question. A salary paid to a founder-director is a business expense that reduces the company's taxable profit, and it is taxed in the founder's own hands as salary income, usually with TDS deducted at source. How you split pay between salary and dividends changes the total tax bill.

There is no single correct salary. The right figure is the smallest amount that lets you focus fully on the business without worrying about rent or school fees. A founder who is distracted by money cannot build well, and a founder who overpays weakens the very company that pays them. This tool helps you find that middle ground with two numbers you already know.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

In India the choice between salary and dividends is a real tax decision. Salary is deductible for the company and taxed in the founder's hands at slab rates, while dividends are paid from post-tax profit and then taxed again in the shareholder's hands. A chartered accountant can model both, and the right answer depends on your slab.

Indian founders also carry obligations a US template ignores. A founder-director drawing a salary may need to contribute to PF and other statutory dues, and the salary must be reasonable for the work, or the tax officer can question it at assessment.

Many Indian startups begin with the founder taking almost no salary and living on savings. That is fine for a while, but a business that cannot pay its founder a fair wage is fragile, and investors on the show treat a sane salary as a sign of discipline rather than weakness.

Common Mistakes

  • Paying yourself nothing for years and calling it discipline. A business that cannot pay its founder is fragile.
  • Taking a salary that eats most of the profit, which leaves nothing to reinvest or to survive a slow quarter.
  • Ignoring the tax split between salary and dividends. The cheaper route depends on your slab, so model both.
