Advance Tax: The Four Dates Founders Miss

Four dates, four cumulative payments, one cash flow to protect.

Tax RulesNovember 23, 2026

Income tax on a small business is filed once a year. The tax office does not wait a year to collect it. It asks for the money in four instalments while the year is still running, based on the profit you expect to make. That is advance tax, and it is the one tax deadline that hits a proprietor's cash flow four times instead of once.

This guide covers who has to pay it, what each of the four dates asks for, what a missed instalment costs in rupees, and how to keep the money ready without raiding the account that pays your suppliers. One boutique is worked through a full year, so the numbers reconcile with each other.

Advance tax is pay-as-you-earn

The idea is simple. Tax is due as the profit is earned, not as the return is filed. So the year is cut into four instalments, and by each date you must have paid a share of the tax you expect to owe for the whole year.

The shares are cumulative, not four equal quarters. By June you must have paid 15% of the year's estimate, by September 45%, by December 75%, and by March the whole thing. Each date is measured against the total, so the payment on the day is only the gap between that total and what you have already paid.

Who has to pay it

Anyone whose total tax for the year is likely to be above ten thousand rupees: a shop, a freelancer, a consultant, a landlord, or a salaried person with rent or interest on the side. Below that line, no instalments are due and the tax is paid with the return.

  • Business and professional income: instalments are worked out from the profit you expect for the year.
  • Salary: the employer deducts TDS every month, so the instalment usually comes to nil unless there is other income.
  • Presumptive taxpayers: one payment, by 15 March. There is a section on this below.
  • TDS already deducted counts. You pay only the balance of the estimate.

The four instalments

Meera runs a boutique. In April she expects her profit for the year to leave a tax bill of about 36,000. All figures are in rupees. Her four payments are:

  • 15 June: 15% of 36,000 = ₹5,400
  • 15 September: 45% of 36,000 = 16,200, so a further 10,800
  • 15 December: 75% of 36,000 = 27,000, so a further 10,800
  • 15 March: 100% of 36,000 = 36,000, so a final 9,000

Paid: ₹5,400 + 10,800 + 10,800 + 9,000 = 36,000. The last instalment is smaller than the two middle ones because the first one was small. That is what cumulative means.

The common mistake is to divide the estimate by four and pay 9,000 every quarter. That leaves the June and September dates short, because 9,000 is below 15% and far below 45% of the year's tax. The payments are made with challan 280, the same one used for self-assessment tax.

What a missed instalment costs

The charge is interest, not a penalty, and it runs at 1% a month on the shortfall, for the months the money stays unpaid. If the June ₹5,400 is paid with the September instalment instead, it is three months late, and the interest is 3% of ₹5,400 = ₹162.

There is a second charge at the year end. If less than 90% of the final tax is paid by 31 March, interest runs at 1% a month from 1 April until the balance is paid. A shortfall of ₹9,000 held for four months costs 4% of ₹9,000 = ₹360.

Neither number is large. What makes them add up is that the money was sitting in the current account all year. The interest is the price of having spent it on stock instead.

The presumptive route: one date, not four

A small business can skip the four dates altogether. Under section 44AD, a business with turnover up to 2 crore can declare profit at 8% of turnover, or 6% on receipts that came digitally, and pay the whole advance tax in one instalment by 15 March.

Professionals have a similar option under section 44ADA, with receipts up to 50 lakh and half of them declared as profit. The single date is the same.

The trade-off is real. You give up the right to claim expenses against that income, so the presumptive route suits a business whose actual margin is above the declared percentage, not one that is running at a loss.

Set the money aside monthly

Meera's estimate is 36,000, so 36,000 ÷ 12 = ₹3,000 a month. She moves it out on the day the month's sales are tallied, into an account she does not use for supplier payments.

Four dates then become one habit, and no instalment is ever a surprise. Recheck the estimate in September and December, because if profit has moved, both the next instalment and the monthly amount move with it.

The short version

  • Tax above ten thousand rupees for the year means instalments, not one payment at the end.
  • 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March: 15%, 45%, 75% and 100%, each cumulative.
  • A shortfall costs 1% a month, and under 90% paid by 31 March costs 1% a month from 1 April.
  • Presumptive taxpayers pay the whole amount by 15 March.
  • Divide the estimate by twelve and move that amount every month.

Saal chalte-chalte hi, jitna munafa hone ki ummeed hai usake hisaab se chaar kishton mein paisa maangta hai. Ise edavaans tax kahte hain, aur yahi woh taareekh hai jo dukaanadaar ki kaish phlo par saal mein chaar baar asar daalate hai.

Yeh guide bataate hai ki kise dena hota hai, chaaron taareekhon par kitna dena hota hai, kisht chhootane par rupayon mein kitna lagta hai, aur saplaayar ke paise wale khaate ko chhue bina yeh rakam kaise taiyar rakhein. Ek buteek ka pura saal hisaab mein liya gaya hai taaki saare aankde aapas mein mil jayein.

Edavaans tax pehle se bhara jaane wala tax hai

Baat seedhe hai. Tax tab dena hota hai jab munafa kamaaya jaata hai, ritarn phaail karne ke din nahi. Isliye saal chaar kishton mein baanta jaata hai, aur har taareekh tak aapko poore saal ke anumaanit tax ka ek hissa bhar dena hota hai.

Ye hisse judate chalte hain, chaar baraabar timaahiyaan nahi hoteen. Joon tak saal ke anumaan ka 15% bhara hona chahiye, sitambar tak 45%, disambar tak 75%, aur maarch tak pura. Har taareekh ka hisaab kul rakam se hota hai, isliye us din sirf utna dena hota hai jitna abhe tak bhara nahi hai.

Kise dena hota hai

Jisaka saal ka kul tax das hazaar rupaye se zyada hone ki ummeed hai: dukaan, phreelaansar, kansaltent, makaan maalik, ya naukare ke saath kiraya aur byaj wali aamdani rakhane wala. Us rekha se neeche kishten nahi dene hoteen, tax ritarn ke saath bhar diya jaata hai.

  • Karobar aur peshe ki aamdani: kishten saal ke anumaanit munafe se nikalti hain.
  • Naukare: company har mahine teedees kaatate hai, isliye kisht aam taur par shoony rahate hai, jab tak koi doosare aamdani na ho.
  • Prezamptiv taiksapeyar: ek hi bhugataan, 15 maarch tak. Neeche isaka sekshan hai.
  • Kata hua teedees jud jaata hai. Aap sirf anumaan ka baaki hissa bharate hain.

Chaar kishten

Meera ki ek buteek hai. Aprail mein use lagta hai ki saal ke munafe par lagabhag 36,000 rupaye tax banega. Saare aankde rupayon mein hain. Usake chaar bhugataan ye hain:

  • 15 joon: 36,000 ka 15% = ₹5,400
  • 15 sitambar: 36,000 ka 45% = 16,200, yani 10,800 aur
  • 15 disambar: 36,000 ka 75% = 27,000, yani 10,800 aur
  • 15 maarch: 36,000 ka 100% = 36,000, yani aakhire 9,000

Kul: ₹5,400 + 10,800 + 10,800 + 9,000 = 36,000. Aakhire kisht beech ki do kishton se chhoti hai kyunki pehli kisht chhoti the. Judate hisse ka matlab yahi hai.

Aam ghalate yeh hai ki anumaan ko chaar se baantakar har timaahe 9,000 bhar diye jaate hain. Isase joon aur sitambar ki taareekhen kam pad jaati hain, kyunki 9,000 saal ke tax ka 15% se bhi kam hai aur 45% se to bahut kam. Bhugataan chaalaan 280 se hota hai, wahi jo selph-asesament tax ke liye lagta hai.

Kisht chhootane par kya lagta hai

Yeh jurmaana nahi, byaj hai, aur kam padi rakam par 1% mahine ke hisaab se lagta hai, jitne mahine paisa nahi bhara. Agar joon ki ₹5,400 ki kisht sitambar mein bhare jaye, to woh teen mahine der se hai, aur byaj ₹5,400 ka 3% = ₹162 banata hai.

Saal ke ant par doosara shulk hai. Agar 31 maarch tak aakhire tax ka 90% se kam bhara gaya, to 1 aprail se baaki rakam bharane tak 1% mahine ke hisaab se byaj chalta hai. ₹9,000 ki kame chaar mahine rahane par ₹9,000 ka 4% = ₹360 lagta hai.

Dono rakamen bade nahi lagateen. Unhein bada banati hai yeh baat ki paisa saal bhar karant akaaunt mein pada tha. Yeh byaj usi paise ko stok mein laga dene ki keemat hai.

Prezamptiv raasta: chaar nahi, ek taareekh

Chhota karobar chaaron taareekhen chhod sakta hai. Sekshan 44AD mein 2 karod tak tarnaovar wala karobar tarnaovar ka 8% munafa dikha sakta hai, ya dijital aamdani par 6%, aur pura edavaans tax ek hi kisht mein 15 maarch tak bhar sakta hai.

Propheshanals ke liye sekshan 44ADA mein aisa hi vikalp hai, 50 laakh tak ki aamdani aur uska aadha munafa ghoshit. Taareekh wahi ek hai.

Isamen sauda dono taraf ka hai. Us aamdani par kharch nahi dikhaya ja sakta, isliye yeh raasta us karobar ke liye theek hai jisaka asli margin ghoshit pratishat se upar hai, usake liye nahi jo ghaate mein chal raha hai.

Paisa har mahine alag rakhein

Meera ka anumaan 36,000 hai, to 36,000 ÷ 12 = ₹3,000 mahine. Woh mahine ki bikri milne ke din yeh rakam alag khaate mein daal dete hai, us khaate mein nahi jisase saplaayar ko paisa jaata hai.

Phir chaar taareekhen ek aadat ban jaati hain, aur koi kisht achaanak nahi aate. Sitambar aur disambar mein anumaan dobaara dekhein, kyunki munafa badala to agli kisht aur mahine ki rakam dono badal jaate hain.

Chhoti baat

  • Saal ka tax das hazaar rupaye se upar hai to kishten dene honge, saal ke ant mein ek bhugataan nahi.
  • 15 joon, 15 sitambar, 15 disambar aur 15 maarch: 15%, 45%, 75% aur 100%, har baar judate hisaab se.
  • Kam padi rakam par 1% mahine, aur 31 maarch tak 90% se kam bhara to 1 aprail se 1% mahine.
  • Prezamptiv taiksapeyar poore rakam 15 maarch tak bharate hain.
  • Anumaan ko baarah se baanten aur har mahine utna alag rakhein.
