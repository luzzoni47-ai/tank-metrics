EBITDA vs Gross Margin: The Real Difference

Two profit numbers, two different questions.

Profit & MarginsNovember 11, 2026

A shop owner and an investor can look at the same business and quote two different profit figures, and both can be right. The shop owner says "my margin is 50%". The investor says "EBITDA is 25% of revenue". Neither is wrong. They are answering different questions.

This guide explains the difference between gross margin and EBITDA, works one small business through both, and tells you which number to reach for when.

The one-line difference

Gross margin is what is left of a sale after the direct cost of the goods. EBITDA is what is left after the goods and the running costs of the business, before interest, tax and depreciation.

Gross margin stops at the cost of the product. EBITDA keeps going and pays the rent, the salaries and the electricity first.

Gross margin: the cost of the sale

Gross margin compares two numbers: what you sold something for, and what that thing cost you to buy or make.

  • Gross profit = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold
  • Gross margin = Gross profit ÷ Revenue

Cost of goods sold is only the direct cost: the material, the purchase price, the packaging. It does not include the rent on the shop or the salary of the person who sold the item. That is why a gross margin can look healthy while the business still loses money.

EBITDA: the cost of running the business

EBITDA takes the same revenue and subtracts more. It removes the direct cost of the goods, then the operating expenses — rent, staff, power, marketing — and stops there.

  • EBITDA = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold − Operating Expenses
  • EBITDA margin = EBITDA ÷ Revenue

It deliberately leaves out interest, tax, depreciation and amortization, which is what the letters stand for. The idea is to show the profit the business makes from its operations alone, before the way it was financed or the way its accountant spreads the cost of machines gets in the way.

A worked example

Take a small business with ₹8,000 of revenue in a month. The goods it sold cost ₹4,000. The running costs were ₹2,000.

  • Revenue: ₹8,000
  • Cost of goods sold: ₹4,000
  • Gross profit: ₹8,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹4,000
  • Gross margin: ₹4,000 ÷ ₹8,000 = 50%
  • Operating expenses: ₹2,000
  • EBITDA: ₹4,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹2,000
  • EBITDA margin: ₹2,000 ÷ ₹8,000 = 25%

Now finish the profit and loss account to see where the rest goes:

  • Interest on a loan: ₹200
  • Depreciation on equipment: ₹300
  • Tax: ₹300
  • Net profit: ₹2,000 − ₹200 − ₹300 − ₹300 = ₹1,200

Read the numbers back the other way and they agree: ₹1,200 net profit + ₹200 interest + ₹300 depreciation + ₹300 tax = ₹2,000 EBITDA. Same figure, two routes.

Why the gap between them matters

The gap between a 50% gross margin and a 25% EBITDA margin is the whole cost of running the business. It is the rent, the staff, the power and the marketing. A business with a fat gross margin and a thin EBITDA margin is spending almost everything it earns on the machinery of staying open.

That gap is where most small businesses fail quietly. The product sells well, the gross margin is fine, and the running costs eat the rest before anything reaches the owner.

Which number should you use?

  • To price a single product: gross margin. It tells you whether one sale carries its own weight.
  • To judge the whole business: EBITDA. It tells you whether the business earns anything from what it does, before finance and tax.
  • To compare with another company: EBITDA. Two businesses with the same gross margin can carry very different running costs.
  • To talk to an investor: EBITDA. It is the first number most investors ask for.

Common mistakes

  1. Using them as synonyms. They are different numbers with different jobs. Quoting gross margin when an investor asked for EBITDA is a quick way to lose their confidence.
  2. Forgetting that neither is cash. EBITDA is not the money in your bank. A business can show a healthy EBITDA and still run short, because working capital is not in either figure.
  3. Chasing gross margin alone. A high gross margin on low sales still has to pay the same rent. Cover the fixed costs before you celebrate the percentage.

Bottom line

Gross margin shows whether a single sale is worth making. EBITDA shows whether the business as a whole earns anything from its operations. Use the first to price, the second to judge. When you know both, you can see exactly where the money goes between the product and the owner.

Ek dukaanadaar aur ek niveshak, dono ek hi karobar ko dekhakar do alag munafe ke aankde bata sakte hain, aur dono sahe ho sakte hain. Dukaanadaar kahta hai "mera margin 50% hai". Niveshak kahta hai "EBITDA revenue ka 25% hai". Koi bhi galat nahi hai. Dono alag savaalon ka jawab de rahe hain.

Yeh guide gross margin aur EBITDA ka farq samjhati hai, ek chhote karobar ko dono se nikaalakar dikhati hai, aur bataate hai ki kaun saa aankda kab istemal karein.

Ek laain ka farq

Gross margin woh hai jo ek bikri se saamaan ki seedhe lagat hataane ke baad bachta hai. EBITDA woh hai jo saamaan aur karobar chalaane ke kharch hataane ke baad bachta hai — byaj, tax aur depreciation se pehle.

Gross margin saamaan ki lagat par ruk jaata hai. EBITDA aage badhakar kiraya, salary aur bijli bhi chukaata hai.

Gross margin: bikri ki lagat

Gross margin do aankdon ki tulna karta hai: aapne cheez kitne mein beche, aur woh cheez aapko banane ya khareedane mein kitne ki padi.

  • Gross profit = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold
  • Gross margin = Gross profit ÷ Revenue

Cost of goods sold mein sirf seedhe lagat aate hai: material, khareed ka daam, packaging. Isamen dukaan ka kiraya ya bechane wale ki salary nahi aate. Iseelie gross margin theek dikh sakta hai jabaki karobar paisa kho raha ho.

EBITDA: karobar chalaane ki lagat

EBITDA wahi revenue leta hai aur usamen se zyada ghataata hai. Pehle saamaan ki seedhe lagat, phir operating expenses — kiraya, staff, bijli, marketing — aur wahin ruk jaata hai.

  • EBITDA = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold − Operating Expenses
  • EBITDA margin = EBITDA ÷ Revenue

Yeh jaan-boojhakar byaj, tax, depreciation aur amortization chhod deta hai — yahi in chaaron aksharon ka matlab hai. Makasad hai woh munafa dikhana jo karobar apne asli kaam se kamata hai — isase pehle ki paisa kahan se aaya ya accountant machine ki lagat kaise phailaata hai, beech mein aaye.

Ek udaharan

Maan lijiye ek chhote karobar ki mahine ki revenue ₹8,000 hai. Beche gaye saamaan ki lagat ₹4,000 rahi. Chalaane ke kharch ₹2,000 rahe.

  • Revenue: ₹8,000
  • Cost of goods sold: ₹4,000
  • Gross profit: ₹8,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹4,000
  • Gross margin: ₹4,000 ÷ ₹8,000 = 50%
  • Operating expenses: ₹2,000
  • EBITDA: ₹4,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹2,000
  • EBITDA margin: ₹2,000 ÷ ₹8,000 = 25%

Ab profit and loss account pura karein, baaki paisa kahan jaata hai dekhein:

  • Karz par byaj: ₹200
  • Machinery par depreciation: ₹300
  • Tax: ₹300
  • Net profit: ₹2,000 − ₹200 − ₹300 − ₹300 = ₹1,200

Aankdon ko ulta padhein to bhi mel khaate hain: ₹1,200 net profit + ₹200 byaj + ₹300 depreciation + ₹300 tax = ₹2,000 EBITDA. Wahi aankda, do raaste.

In dono ke beech ka faasala kyon maayne rakhta hai

50% gross margin aur 25% EBITDA margin ke beech ka faasala pura karobar chalaane ki lagat hai. Yahi kiraya, staff, bijli aur marketing hai. Jis karobar ka gross margin mota aur EBITDA margin patla ho, woh apni kamai ka lagabhag sab kuch khula rahane mein laga raha hai.

Yahi faasala hai jahaan zyadatar chhote karobar chupchaap phansate hain. Saamaan achha bikta hai, gross margin theek hai, aur chalaane ke kharch baaki sab kha jaate hain — maalik tak kuch nahi pahunchata.

Kaun saa aankda kab istemal karein?

  • Ek cheez ka daam tay karne ke liye: gross margin. Yeh batata hai ki ek bikri apna bojh khud utha rahi hai ya nahi.
  • Poore karobar ko parakhane ke liye: EBITDA. Yeh batata hai ki karobar apne kaam se kuch kamata hai ya nahi — finance aur tax se pehle.
  • Kisi aur company se tulna ke liye: EBITDA. Ek jaise gross margin wale do karobaron ke chalaane ke kharch bahut alag ho sakte hain.
  • Niveshak se baat karne ke liye: EBITDA. Zyadatar niveshak sabse pehle yahi poochhte hain.

Sirf EBITDA par poore jaanakaare ke liye dekhein woh guide jo ise bahi-khaate se banati hai.

Aam galatiyaan

  1. Inhein ek maan lena. Ye alag aankde hain, alag kaam ke. Niveshak ne EBITDA poochha aur aap gross margin bataayein, to bharosa jaldi tootta hai.
  2. Bhool jaana ki dono mein se koi bhi nakad nahi hai. EBITDA aapke bank mein pada paisa nahi hai. Achha EBITDA dikhane wale karobar ke paas bhi paisa kam pad sakta hai, kyunki working capital dono aankdon mein dikhta hi nahi.
  3. Sirf gross margin ke peechhe bhaagana. Kam bikri par ooncha gross margin bhi wahi kiraya chukaana padta hai. Pehle fixed costs cover karein, phir pratishat ka jashn manaaen.

Chhoti baat

Gross margin batata hai ki ek bikri karne layak hai ya nahi. EBITDA batata hai ki pura karobar apne kaam se kuch kamata hai ya nahi. Daam tay karne ke liye pehla, parakhane ke liye doosara. Dono pata hon to aap theek-theek dekh sakte hain ki saamaan aur maalik ke beech paisa kahan jaata hai.
