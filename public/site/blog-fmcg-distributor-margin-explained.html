FMCG Distributor Margin: Who Earns What

One ₹100 pack pays the company, you and the shop — here is how the three margins split.

Pricing & CostsNovember 10, 2026

If you are an FMCG distributor, the number that decides your income is not the MRP printed on the pack. It is the margin the company allows you to keep, and that margin is almost always quoted as a percentage of MRP rather than of what you paid for the goods.

This guide shows how a ₹100 pack splits between the company, the distributor and the retailer, works through a small example, and explains why the same ₹20 can be called two different margins.

The FMCG chain, in one line

Goods move company → distributor (stockist) → retailer (kirana or supermarket) → customer. Each step adds its own margin to the price it passes on, and the last price is the MRP. Because the MRP is fixed and printed on the pack, the three margins have to fit inside it.

A worked example: where ₹100 of MRP goes

Take a pack with an MRP of ₹100. In the usual Indian FMCG trade terms, the retailer keeps 20% of MRP and the distributor keeps 10% of MRP. Whatever is left is the company's price.

  1. Retailer margin: 20% of ₹100 = ₹20. The shop buys the pack at ₹80.
  2. Distributor margin: 10% of ₹100 = ₹10. The distributor buys the pack at ₹70 and sells it at ₹80.
  3. Company price: ₹100 − ₹20 − ₹10 = ₹70. That is what the company charges the distributor.

The ₹10 on the pack is the distributor's whole income from that sale. Every cost you carry — godown rent, delivery van, salesman salary, breakage — comes out of that ₹10, not out of the ₹80 you billed.

Company price, distributor margin and retailer margin

The three numbers are linked, so fixing any two decides the third. If the company raises its price, either your margin or the retailer's has to give, and in practice it is usually yours.

  • Retailer margin: what the shop keeps, quoted as a percentage of MRP.
  • Distributor margin: what you keep, also quoted as a percentage of MRP.
  • Company price: MRP minus both margins — the invoice value you actually pay.

Why the same ₹20 is a 20% margin or a 25% margin

A ₹20 margin on a ₹100 MRP is 20% of MRP. But the money you put in was ₹80, so that same ₹20 is 25% of what you paid. Two people looking at the same sale can quote 20% and 25% and both be right, which is why every margin figure has to name the base it is taken on.

Turn that into a month. If you buy ₹5,600 of stock, that stock sells at an MRP of ₹8,000, so your margin is ₹800. Your ₹800 is 10% of the MRP and about 14% of the ₹5,600 you put in — the same money, two different percentages.

What quietly eats an FMCG distributor's margin

  1. Scheme stock bought ahead. A 5% scheme sounds free, but the extra cartons sit in the godown and block cash that the next scheme needs.
  2. Expiry and near-expiry returns. A carton that comes back is a margin you already booked being reversed, plus the freight both ways.
  3. Credit to retailers. When a shop pays in 45 days and you pay the company in 15, the gap is funded out of your own pocket.

How to check your own numbers

Work out your margin the way the trade quotes it, then check it against the money you actually put in. On that ₹800 of monthly margin, running costs of ₹600 leave just ₹200 — and one bad expiry month can turn that into a loss.

FMCG ki chain, ek laain mein

Maal chalta hai company → distributor (stockist) → retailer (kirana ya supermarket) → graahak. Har kadam apna margin jodkar aage badhaata hai, aur aakhire keemat MRP hai. Kyunki MRP tay hai aur pack par chhapa hai, teenon margin ko usi ke andar phit hona hai.

Ek udaharan: ₹100 ke MRP ka hisaab

₹100 ke MRP wala pack lijiye. Aam bhaarateey FMCG trade mein retailer MRP ka 20% rakhta hai aur distributor MRP ka 10%. Jo bachta hai woh company ki keemat hai.

  1. Retailer margin: ₹100 ka 20% = ₹20. Dukaan pack ₹80 mein khareedate hai.
  2. Distributor margin: ₹100 ka 10% = ₹10. Distributor pack ₹70 mein khareedata hai aur ₹80 mein bechata hai.
  3. Company ki keemat: ₹100 − ₹20 − ₹10 = ₹70. Yahi company distributor se vasoolate hai.

Us pack par woh ₹10 hi distributor ki poore aamdani hai. Har kharch — godown ka kiraya, delivery van, salesman ki salary, toot-phoot — usi ₹10 mein se jaata hai, us ₹80 se nahi jo aapne bill kiya.

Company ki keemat, distributor margin aur retailer margin

Teenon number jude hue hain, yani koi do tay kar dein to teesara apne aap tay ho jaata hai. Agar company apni keemat badhati hai, to aapka margin ya retailer ka margin kam hona padega, aur asal mein kam aapka hota hai.

  • Retailer margin: dukaan jo rakhate hai, MRP ke pratishat mein.
  • Distributor margin: aap jo rakhte hain, woh bhi MRP ke pratishat mein.
  • Company ki keemat: MRP mein se dono margin hata deejie — yahi invoice value hai jo aap sach mein dete hain.

Wahi ₹20, 20% margin ya 25% margin kaise

₹100 ke MRP par ₹20 ka margin MRP ka 20% hai. Par jo paisa aapne lagaaya woh ₹80 tha, to wahi ₹20 aapke diye paise ka 25% hai. Ek hi bikri par do log 20% aur 25% keh sakte hain aur dono sahe hote hain — isliye har margin ke saath batana padta hai ki woh kis aadhaar par hai.

Ise mahine mein le jaaie. Agar aap ₹5,600 ka maal khareedate hain aur woh maal ₹8,000 ke MRP par bikta hai, to aapka margin ₹800 hai. Woh ₹800 MRP ka 10% hai aur aapke lagaae ₹5,600 ka kareeb 14% — wahi paisa, do alag pratishat.

FMCG distributor ka margin asal mein kya kha jaata hai

  1. Aage ka scheme stock. 5% ka scheme muft lagta hai, par ve extra cartons godown mein pade rehte hain aur woh nakad rok lete hain jo agle scheme ko chahiye.
  2. Expiry aur near-expiry ki vaapasi. Lauta hua carton woh margin ulat deta hai jo aapne pehle hi gin liya tha, saath mein dono taraph ka bhaada.
  3. Retailer ko udhaar. Jab dukaan 45 din mein paisa dete hai aur aap company ko 15 din mein, to woh antar aapki jeb se bharata hai.

Apne aankde kaise jaanchein

Apna margin usi tarah nikaalie jaise trade mein bola jaata hai, aur phir use us paise se milaaie jo aapne sach mein lagaaya. Us ₹800 mahine ke margin par, ₹600 ka kharch chalaane ke baad sirph ₹200 bachate hain — aur expiry ka ek bura mahina use ghaate mein badal sakta hai.
