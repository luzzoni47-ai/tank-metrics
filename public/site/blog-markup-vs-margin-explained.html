Markup vs Margin: Why a ₹8,000 Sale Earns You 50%, Not 100%

The same sale gives two very different percentages. Here is which one your business actually lives on, with the arithmetic in rupees.

Pricing & CostsOctober 21, 2026

Ask two sellers what they made on the same sale and you can get two different answers. One says 100%, the other says 50%. Both are right — one is quoting markup, the other margin.

Mixing the two up is one of the most expensive small mistakes in pricing. It makes a thin deal look generous, and it makes a fair price look like a rip-off.

What markup and margin actually measure

Both describe the same thing — the profit on a sale — but each divides that profit by a different number.

  • Markup is profit as a percentage of what the item cost you.
  • Margin is profit as a percentage of what the customer paid.

That one difference in the denominator is why the two numbers never match, and why margin is always the smaller of the two.

A worked example: a ₹8,000 sale

Say you buy an item for ₹4,000 and sell it for ₹8,000.

  1. Profit: ₹8,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹4,000.
  2. Markup: ₹4,000 ÷ ₹4,000 = 100%.
  3. Margin: ₹4,000 ÷ ₹8,000 = 50%.

One sale, one profit of ₹4,000, and two honest answers: a 100% markup and a 50% margin. The markup is measured against the ₹4,000 you spent. The margin is measured against the ₹8,000 the customer paid.

Why the same profit gives two different numbers

Markup always looks bigger because it is a percentage of the smaller base — your cost. Margin is a percentage of the larger base — the selling price.

That gap widens as the margin rises. At a 20% margin the markup is 25%, close enough to be confused. At a 50% margin the markup is 100%, and the two numbers stop looking related at all.

Working backwards from a target margin

Margin is the number your business actually lives on, so set it first and let it decide the price.

Say you want a 60% margin on an item that costs you ₹2,000. Divide the cost by one minus the margin.

  1. ₹2,000 ÷ (1 − 0.60) = ₹2,000 ÷ 0.40 = ₹5,000.
  2. Check the profit: ₹5,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹3,000.
  3. Margin: ₹3,000 ÷ ₹5,000 = 60%. Markup: ₹3,000 ÷ ₹2,000 = 150%.

The same ₹5,000 price is a 60% margin and a 150% markup. Add the markup to the cost instead and you would land short of the margin you wanted, every single time.

Three mistakes that cost money

  1. Adding a markup and calling it margin. The two numbers are quoted as percentages, so a 60% markup reads like a 60% margin. It is not — it leaves you with less than you planned.
  2. Comparing your margin with someone else's markup. A supplier advertising a 40% margin and a seller bragging about a 40% markup are describing very different deals.
  3. Pricing from cost alone. Cost tells you the floor, not the price. Start from the margin you need, then add the cost back in.

The short version

Markup divides profit by cost. Margin divides profit by the selling price. On an item that costs ₹4,000 and sells for ₹8,000 the profit is ₹4,000 — a 100% markup but a 50% margin. To hold a 60% margin on a ₹2,000 cost, price at ₹5,000.

Markup aur margin asal mein naapate kya hain

Dono ek hi cheez batate hain — bikri par hua munafa — par har ek use alag sankhya se baantata hai.

  • Markup munafe ko us rakam ka pratishat batata hai jo cheez mein aapko lage.
  • Margin munafe ko us rakam ka pratishat batata hai jo graahak ne dee.

Har mein yahi ek farq hai jisake vajah se dono sankhyaaen kabhi mel nahi khaateen, aur margin hamesha chhota nikalta hai.

Ek udaharan: ₹8,000 ki bikri

Maan lijiye aap ek cheez ₹4,000 mein khareedate hain aur ₹8,000 mein bechte hain.

  1. Munafa: ₹8,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹4,000.
  2. Markup: ₹4,000 ÷ ₹4,000 = 100%.
  3. Margin: ₹4,000 ÷ ₹8,000 = 50%.

Ek bikri, ek munafa ₹4,000, aur do sachche jawab: 100% markup aur 50% margin. Markup un ₹4,000 par naapa jaata hai jo aapne lagaae. Margin un ₹8,000 par naapa jaata hai jo graahak ne diye.

Wahi munafa do alag sankhyaaen kyon deta hai

Markup hamesha bada dikhta hai kyunki woh chhote aadhaar ka pratishat hai — aapki lagat. Margin bade aadhaar ka pratishat hai — bikri keemat.

Yeh farq margin badhne ke saath badhta jaata hai. 20% margin par markup 25% hota hai, itana paas ki gadabad ho jaye. 50% margin par markup 100% ho jaata hai, aur dono sankhyaaen ek jaise lagane hi band ho jaati hain.

Tay margin se ulta hisaab

Margin hi woh aankda hai jis par aapka kaam chalta hai, to use pehle tay karein aur keemat usse nikalane dein.

Maan lijiye aap ₹2,000 lagat wali cheez par 60% margin chahte hain. Lagat ko ek mein se margin ghataakar baanten.

  1. ₹2,000 ÷ (1 − 0.60) = ₹2,000 ÷ 0.40 = ₹5,000.
  2. Munafa jaanchein: ₹5,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹3,000.
  3. Margin: ₹3,000 ÷ ₹5,000 = 60%. Markup: ₹3,000 ÷ ₹2,000 = 150%.

Wahi ₹5,000 ki keemat 60% margin hai aur 150% markup. Isake bajaay markup ko lagat mein jod dein, to har baar aap manachaahe margin se peechhe rah jayenge.

Teen galatiyaan jo paisa khaate hain

  1. Markup jodkar use margin keh dena. Dono pratishat mein bole jaate hain, to 60% markup 60% margin jaisa lagta hai. Hai nahi — isase aapke haath mein tay se kam bachta hai.
  2. Apna margin kisi aur ke markup se milana. 40% margin batata supplier aur 40% markup par itaraata seller do bahut alag saude bata rahe hote hain.
  3. Sirf lagat se keemat tay karna. Lagat sirf nichale had bataate hai, keemat nahi. Pehle zaroore margin tay karein, phir lagat jodein.

Chhoti baat

Markup munafe ko lagat se baantata hai. Margin munafe ko bikri keemat se baantata hai. ₹4,000 lagat aur ₹8,000 keemat wali cheez par munafa ₹4,000 hai — 100% markup, par 50% margin. ₹2,000 lagat par 60% margin rakhana hai to keemat ₹5,000 rakhein.

Zaroore margin tay karein aur usi se keemat nikalein — tab ye dono sankhyaaen aapko ulajhaaenge nahi.
