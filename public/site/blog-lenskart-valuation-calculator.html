Frequently asked questions

How do you value a company on a revenue multiple?

Multiply yearly revenue by the multiple. At ₹100 crore of revenue and a 4x multiple, the valuation is ₹400 crore.

What revenue multiple do Indian D2C brands get?

It varies with growth and margin, and early brands usually sit in a lower range than fast-growing ones. Test a few multiples in the calculator instead of trusting one figure.

Does this show Lenskart's exact valuation?

No. It shows how a valuation is built from revenue and a multiple, the same method investors use, without claiming a precise current number for any company.

What is peyush bansal lenskart valuation calculator?

A Lenskart style valuation calculator works on the revenue multiple, which is the number of times a company's yearly revenue an investor is willing to pay. If a brand earns ₹100 crore in a year and an investor applies a 4x multiple, the valuation is ₹400 crore. Lenskart itself grew from a small online eyewear store into a business whose reported annual revenue from operations crossed ₹5,000 crore, and it is that scale, not a single flattering deal, that makes investors take it seriously.

The multiple is not fixed, and it moves with the quality of the business. Fast-growing brands with strong repeat purchases and healthy margins command a higher multiple than a slow, thin-margin retailer. This is why two companies with the same revenue can be valued very differently. The tool lets you change the multiple and watch the valuation move, so you can see how sensitive your own number really is.

For an Indian founder, the useful part is the honesty. A revenue multiple does not pretend to know the future, it simply says what the market pays today for every rupee of revenue. Build the revenue and the multiple takes care of itself, and the tool keeps every figure in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores.

Formula

The formulaValuation = Yearly Revenue × Revenue Multiple

Revenue in ₹ crore and the multiple as a plain number, for example 4x. The result is the valuation in ₹ crore.

Worked Example (in ₹ Crores)

Take an eyewear or lifestyle brand and apply different revenue multiples to the same revenue. Every row is one multiplication.

Yearly RevenueRevenue MultipleValuation
₹50 crore3x₹150 crore
₹100 crore4x₹400 crore
₹250 crore5x₹1,250 crore
₹500 crore6x₹3,000 crore

The maths is deliberately simple: revenue times the multiple. Move the multiple from 4x to 6x on ₹100 crore of revenue and the valuation jumps from ₹400 crore to ₹600 crore, which is why the multiple is the whole argument in a funding conversation.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Indian valuation advice is often copied from a US template that talks in millions and in EBITDA multiples that suit mature firms. Early Indian brands are usually valued on revenue, because profits arrive late, so a revenue multiple calculator is closer to how deals are actually struck here.

Indian eyewear, apparel and D2C brands also carry a heavier inventory and store cost than a pure software business, which is why their multiples sit lower than a SaaS company's. A founder who quotes a software multiple for a retail brand will be corrected in the first investor meeting.

On the compliance side, a valuation used in a share issue may need a chartered accountant or a merchant banker to certify it, and the price cannot be arbitrary. A clean revenue multiple is the easiest number to justify on that paperwork.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying a software style multiple to a retail or inventory heavy brand, which investors will reject in the first meeting.
  • Using an old revenue figure. The multiple is applied to the last twelve months, so a stale number understates or overstates the valuation.
  • Treating the multiple as fixed. It changes with growth, margin and how badly the investor wants the deal, so always test a range.
