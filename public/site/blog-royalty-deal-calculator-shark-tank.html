Frequently asked questions

What is a Shark Tank royalty deal?

In a royalty deal the investor takes less equity but also a percentage of sales (a royalty) until the invested amount is recouped. For example, ₹1.5 crore for 2.25% equity plus 1% royalty until ₹1 crore is recouped.

Is a royalty deal good or bad for a founder?

It depends. It can save equity if sales grow fast and the royalty ends soon. If sales are slow, the royalty runs long and the total cost can exceed a pure equity deal, so calculate the break-even first.

What happens after the royalty is recouped?

Once the recoup amount is fully paid, the royalty stops and the investor keeps only the equity. That is why the recoup time matters so much.

In Shark Tank India Season 3, Decode Age took ₹1.5 crore for 2.25% equity plus a 1% royalty until ₹1 crore was recouped. A royalty deal gives the shark downside protection and lets the founder keep more equity, but it also takes a slice of every month's sales. What is shark tank royalty deal calculator?

A royalty deal means the investor takes a smaller equity stake plus a percentage of sales (a royalty) until a fixed amount is recouped. After that, the royalty stops and only the equity remains. It became common on Shark Tank India from Season 3 as a way to close a gap when founders and sharks disagree on valuation.

A pure equity deal takes equity only. A royalty deal saves equity up front but charges your cash flow every month. Tank Metrics puts the two side by side so you can see how many months the royalty runs and what it costs in total, all in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores.

The trade is simple to state. A royalty deal buys you a smaller equity cut today and charges you a monthly slice of sales until the recoup is complete. Whether that is cheap or expensive depends entirely on how fast your sales grow from here.

One rule of thumb helps here. If the royalty would take more than about three years to recoup at your current sales, the deal is leaning toward the investor. If it recoups in under a year, the founder is giving away cash that pure equity might have cost less to part with. The right answer sits between those two ends.

Formula

The formulaMonths to Recoup = Recoup Amount ÷ (Monthly Sales × Royalty %)

Effective cost = equity given + total royalty paid until the recoup is complete.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Royalty deals took off in India from Season 3, when sharks felt founders were asking for too-high valuations. On the US Shark Tank, Kevin O'Leary is famous for royalty offers, but in India the recoup amount is set in rupees and the royalty runs against rupee sales.

In India a royalty payment also attracts TDS, and GST sits on the revenue itself, so the net cash flow differs from a US deal. US royalty calculators ignore these Indian layers. Tank Metrics keeps the whole comparison in ₹ Crores and in Indian tax terms.

India also treats the royalty differently for each side. It is an operating cost for the investor and revenue for the company, which is exactly why a royalty deal is usually structured with a recoup cap. That cap is the number to negotiate hardest.

Common Mistakes

  • Agreeing to a royalty with no recoup cap, which means the payment never ends.
  • Comparing a royalty percentage with an equity percentage as if they were the same kind of number.
  • Forgetting TDS on the royalty, which quietly reduces the founder's net cash each month.
