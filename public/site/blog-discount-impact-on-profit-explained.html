Why a 10% Discount Cuts Your Profit by 25%

A discount comes out of profit, not revenue. One ₹1,000 kurta shows exactly how much each percentage point of discount really costs you.

Pricing & CostsOctober 23, 2026

Every discount feels like a small gift to the customer. On your books it is not small at all, because a discount comes out of profit, not out of revenue.

This guide puts rupees on that. One kurta, one price, one 10% discount — and the arithmetic that shows what every extra point of discount really costs.

Why a discount costs more than it looks

A rupee off the price is a rupee off your profit. Your costs do not move when you discount: the fabric, the stitching and the packaging cost the same whether you sell at full price or at 20% off.

So the discount is not shared between you and the customer. You pay all of it, and it comes straight out of the one number you are trying to grow.

A ₹1,000 kurta, discounted

Say you sell a kurta for ₹1,000. It costs you ₹600 to make and pack, so each sale earns you ₹400 — a margin of 40%.

Now run a 10% discount. The price falls to ₹900. The cost stays at ₹600, so the profit falls to ₹300.

  1. Profit before the discount: ₹1,000 − ₹600 = ₹400.
  2. Profit after the discount: ₹900 − ₹600 = ₹300.
  3. Profit lost: ₹400 − ₹300 = ₹100, which is a quarter of ₹400.

A 10% discount, a 25% cut in profit. That gap is the whole story of this guide.

The discount-to-profit table

Keep the same ₹600 cost and the same ₹1,000 price, and walk the discount up one step at a time.

  1. No discount: price ₹1,000, profit ₹400.
  2. 10% off: price ₹900, profit ₹300 — a quarter of the profit gone.
  3. 20% off: price ₹800, profit ₹200 — half the profit gone.
  4. 30% off: price ₹700, profit ₹100 — three-quarters gone.
  5. 40% off: price ₹600, profit ₹0 — the discount has eaten the entire profit.

Read the last row again. A 40% discount does not halve your profit on this kurta, it removes it. At ₹600 you have sold the kurta for exactly what it cost you to make.

Thin margins break first

How much a discount hurts depends on the margin you started with, not on the size of the discount alone.

Take a second item that costs ₹800 and sells for ₹1,000 — a margin of 20%. The same 10% discount drops the price to ₹900 and the profit from ₹200 to ₹100. Half of it, gone, on the same 10% off.

The comfortable 40% margin lost a quarter of its profit. The thin 20% margin lost half. The smaller your cushion, the harder the same discount lands.

How much extra volume a discount demands

Sellers often accept a discount because they expect more sales to cover it. Run the arithmetic before you believe that.

Say you sell 75 kurtas a month at ₹400 profit each. Give the 10% discount and each kurta earns ₹300 instead, so earning what those 75 kurtas used to bring now takes 100 kurtas.

  1. At full price: 75 kurtas at ₹400 profit each.
  2. At 10% off: 100 kurtas at ₹300 profit each.
  3. Extra kurtas needed: 100 − 75 = 25, a third more than before.

A third more stock, a third more packing and 25 extra buyers — for exactly the same profit you already had.

Three ways to discount without wrecking profit

  1. Discount the slow stock, not everything. A discount is cheapest where the stock is not moving anyway. Take 20% off one dead colour, not off the whole shop.
  2. Swap the discount for a bundle. Instead of ₹200 off one kurta, add a second item to the sale at a small markdown. The customer gets more, and the profit per order holds.
  3. Check the numbers before you announce it. Decide the profit you are willing to give up, then work back to the discount that fits. A discount you regret cannot be undone once the customer has seen the price.

The short version

A discount comes out of profit, not revenue. On a ₹1,000 kurta that costs ₹600, a 10% discount takes the profit from ₹400 to ₹300 — a 25% cut. A 40% discount takes it to zero. On a thinner 20% margin, the same 10% off halves the profit.

Choose the discount from the profit you can spare, never from the price tag.

Discount dikhane se zyada mahanga kyon padta hai

Keemat se gaya ek rupaya aapke profit se gaya ek rupaya hai. Discount dene par aapki lagat hilate nahi: kapada, silaaee aur packaging ka kharch wahi rehta hai, chaahe aap poore keemat par bechen ya 20% kam par.

To discount aapke aur graahak ke beech bantata nahi. Pura aap chukaate hain, aur woh seedhe usi number se jaata hai jise aap badhaana chahte hain.

₹1,000 ka kurta, discount ke saath

Maan lijiye aap ek kurta ₹1,000 mein bechate hain. Use banane aur pack karne mein ₹600 lagte hain, yani har bikri par aapko ₹400 milate hain — 40% ka margin.

Ab 10% ka discount chalaaie. Keemat ₹900 par aa jaati hai. Lagat ₹600 par tike rahate hai, to profit ₹300 rah jaata hai.

  1. Discount se pehle profit: ₹1,000 − ₹600 = ₹400.
  2. Discount ke baad profit: ₹900 − ₹600 = ₹300.
  3. Gaya profit: ₹400 − ₹300 = ₹100, jo ₹400 ka ek chauthai hai.

10% ka discount, profit mein 25% ki kataute. Yahi antar is poore guide ki kahaane hai.

Discount se profit ki taalika

Wahi ₹600 lagat aur wahi ₹1,000 keemat rakhie, aur discount ko ek-ek kadam upar chadhaaie.

  1. Discount nahi: keemat ₹1,000, profit ₹400.
  2. 10% kam: keemat ₹900, profit ₹300 — profit ka ek chauthai gaya.
  3. 20% kam: keemat ₹800, profit ₹200 — aadha profit gaya.
  4. 30% kam: keemat ₹700, profit ₹100 — teen chauthai gaya.
  5. 40% kam: keemat ₹600, profit ₹0 — discount ne pura profit kha liya.

Aakhire pankti phir padhie. Is kurta par 40% ka discount aapke profit ko aadha nahi karta, pura hata deta hai. ₹600 par aapne kurta theek utane mein becha jitna woh banane mein laga.

Patla margin pehle tootta hai

Discount kitna chot pahunchaata hai, yeh us margin par nirbhar hai jisase aapne shuru kiya, sirf discount ke aakaar par nahi.

Ek doosare cheez lijiye jisake lagat ₹800 hai aur keemat ₹1,000 — 20% ka margin. Wahi 10% ka discount keemat ₹900 par aur profit ₹200 se ₹100 par le aata hai. Aadha, gaayab, usi 10% par.

Aaraamadeh 40% margin ne apne profit ka ek chauthai khoya. Patle 20% margin ne aadha khoya. Aapka cushion jitna chhota, wahi discount utni zor se padta hai.

Discount kitni extra bikri maangta hai

Dukaanadaar aksar discount maan lete hain kyunki ummeed rahate hai ki zyada bikri use dhak lege. Yeh maanane se pehle hisaab lagaaie.

Maan lijiye aap mahine ke 75 kurta bechate hain, har ek par ₹400 profit. 10% ka discount deejie to har kurta ₹300 kamata hai, yani un 75 kurta ka profit ab 100 kurta se aaega.

  1. Poore keemat par: 75 kurta, har ek par ₹400 profit.
  2. 10% kam par: 100 kurta, har ek par ₹300 profit.
  3. Zyada chahiye: 100 − 75 = 25, yani pehle se ek tihaaee zyada.

Ek tihaaee zyada stock, ek tihaaee zyada packing aur 25 extra graahak — aur profit bilkul wahi jo pehle tha.

Profit bachaate hue discount dene ke teen tarike

  1. Dheeme stock par discount deejie, sab par nahi. Discount wahaan sabse sasta hai jahaan stock vaise bhi nahi bik raha. Ek mare huee rang par 20% ghataaie, poore dukaan par nahi.
  2. Discount ki jagah bundle deejie. Ek kurta par ₹200 kam dene ke bajaay bikri mein doosare cheez thode kam keemat par jodie. Graahak ko zyada milta hai, aur har order ka profit tika rehta hai.
  3. Ailaan se pehle number dekhie. Tay keejie ki kitna profit chhodne ko taiyar hain, phir utane hi discount tak peechhe aaie. Jis discount par pachhataava ho, woh graahak ke keemat dekhane ke baad vaapas nahi aata.

Chhoti baat

Discount revenue se nahi, profit se jaata hai. ₹1,000 ka kurta jisake lagat ₹600 hai, us par 10% ka discount profit ko ₹400 se ₹300 par le aata hai — 25% ki kataute. 40% ka discount use shoony par le jaata hai. Patle 20% margin par wahi 10% aadha profit le jaata hai.

Discount us profit se chunie jo aap chhod sakte hain, keemat ke tag se kabhi nahi.
