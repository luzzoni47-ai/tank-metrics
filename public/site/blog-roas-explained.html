Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) Explained

Ad spend, revenue, and the break-even ROAS in between.

Customers & Unit EconomicsNovember 3, 2026

ROAS — return on ad spend — is the one number that tells you whether your advertising is paying for itself. It answers a simple question: for every rupee you put into ads, how many rupees of revenue came back?

This guide gives you the ROAS formula, walks through a small worked example, explains how ROAS differs from ROI, and shows the break-even ROAS your ads must clear before they make you money.

What ROAS means

ROAS compares the revenue an ad campaign brings in with the amount you spent on it. A ROAS of 4x, or 400%, means every ₹1 of ad spend returned ₹4 of revenue. It is a revenue measure, not a profit measure — a high ROAS can still lose money if your margins are thin.

  • Revenue — the sales value the campaign generated, before any costs.
  • Ad spend — what you paid the platform, plus any agency or creative cost you choose to include.

The ROAS formula

ROAS = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend

You can write the same result as a multiple (2x) or a percentage (200%). Both mean the same thing: the revenue is twice the spend. To get the percentage, multiply the multiple by 100.

  • ROAS 1x (100%) — you got back exactly what you spent, before any product cost.
  • ROAS 2x (200%) — ₹1 in ads returned ₹2 in revenue.
  • ROAS below 1x — the campaign lost money on revenue alone.

A worked example

Say a small online store spends ₹4,000 on ads in a month and the campaign brings in ₹8,000 of revenue. ROAS = 8,000 ÷ 4,000 = 2x, or 200%.

That looks healthy, but revenue is not profit. If the store's gross margin is 60%, the gross profit on that revenue is ₹4,800. Take away the ₹4,000 of ad spend and the campaign added ₹800 of profit.

Now change the spend. The same store spends ₹6,000 and the campaign returns ₹9,000. ROAS = 9,000 ÷ 6,000 = 1.5x, or 150%. The gross profit at 60% is ₹5,400, and after the ₹6,000 of ad spend the campaign is down ₹600. The ROAS fell from 2x to 1.5x, and the campaign went from profit to loss.

ROAS vs ROI: not the same number

ROAS uses revenue. ROI (return on investment) uses profit. Because ROAS ignores your cost of goods, packaging, delivery and overheads, a 3x ROAS can still be a losing campaign. Use ROAS to compare campaigns and creatives; use ROI to judge whether the whole ad programme made money.

  • ROAS = revenue ÷ ad spend. Fast, platform-friendly, ignores costs.
  • ROI = (profit ÷ spend) × 100. Slower, but tells you the truth.

Break-even ROAS: the number that decides if ads pay

Break-even ROAS is the ROAS at which the campaign neither makes nor loses money. It depends only on your gross margin: Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross margin.

At a 60% gross margin, break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.60 = 1.67x. Any campaign below 1.67x loses money on that sale; anything above it adds profit. At a 25% margin the break-even jumps to 4x, which is why low-margin sellers need a far higher ROAS to stay in profit.

  • 60% margin → break-even ROAS 1.67x.
  • 50% margin → break-even ROAS 2x.
  • 25% margin → break-even ROAS 4x.

Common mistakes with ROAS

  1. Reading ROAS as profit. It is revenue, and your product cost comes out of it.
  2. Ignoring the margin. The same 3x ROAS is a win at 60% margin and a loss at 20%.
  3. Comparing platforms on ROAS alone, when each one counts revenue differently.
  4. Chasing ROAS so hard that spend falls, revenue falls faster, and growth stops.

How to use ROAS in a small business

Set a target ROAS above your break-even number, leave a margin for the costs the platform does not count, and review it by campaign rather than across the whole account. When a campaign clears break-even, scale the spend slowly and watch the ROAS hold; when it does not, fix the creative or the audience before you add budget.

ROAS — return on ad spend — woh ek number hai jo batata hai ki aapka advertising apna kharch nikaal raha hai ya nahi. Yeh seedha sawal hai: ads mein lagaae har ek rupaye ke badle kitne rupaye ki revenue vaapas aaee?

Yeh guide aapko ROAS ka formula dete hai, ek chhota udaharan dikhati hai, bataate hai ki ROAS aur ROI alag kyon hain, aur woh break-even ROAS bataate hai jise paar kiye bina ads munafa nahi dete.

ROAS ka matlab

ROAS ek ad campaign ki revenue ki tulna us par kiye gaye kharch se karta hai. 4x ya 400% ROAS ka matlab hai ki ads ke har ₹1 par ₹4 ki revenue vaapas aaee. Yeh revenue ka maap hai, munafe ka nahi — patle margin par ooncha ROAS bhi nuksan de sakta hai.

  • Revenue — campaign se aaee bikri ki keemat, kisi kharch se pehle.
  • Ad spend — platform ko diya paisa, aur agency ya creative ka kharch jo aap jodna chaahein.

ROAS ka formula

ROAS = Ads se revenue ÷ Ad spend

Isi nateeje ko multiple (2x) ya percentage (200%) mein likh sakte hain. Dono ka matlab ek hi hai: revenue kharch se dugane hai. Percentage paane ke liye multiple ko 100 se guna karein.

  • ROAS 1x (100%) — jo kharch kiya theek utna hi vaapas aaya, maal ki lagat se pehle.
  • ROAS 2x (200%) — ads mein ₹1 ne ₹2 ki revenue lautaaee.
  • ROAS 1x se kam — campaign revenue par hi ghaate mein raha.

Ek udaharan

Maan lijiye ek chhota online store mahine mein ₹4,000 ads par kharch karta hai aur campaign ₹8,000 ki revenue laata hai. ROAS = 8,000 ÷ 4,000 = 2x, ya 200%.

Yeh theek lagta hai, par revenue munafa nahi hai. Agar store ka gross margin 60% hai, to us revenue par gross profit ₹4,800 banata hai. Usamen se ₹4,000 ka ad spend ghataaie, to campaign ne ₹800 ka munafa joda.

Ab kharch badalie. Wahi store ₹6,000 kharch karta hai aur campaign ₹9,000 lautata hai. ROAS = 9,000 ÷ 6,000 = 1.5x, ya 150%. 60% par gross profit ₹5,400 hai, aur ₹6,000 ke ad spend ke baad campaign ₹600 ghaate mein hai. ROAS 2x se 1.5x gira, aur campaign munafe se nuksan mein chala gaya.

ROAS aur ROI ek nahi hain

ROAS revenue leta hai. ROI (return on investment) munafa leta hai. Kyunki ROAS aapke maal ki lagat, packaging, delivery aur overheads chhod deta hai, 3x ROAS bhi ghaate ka campaign ho sakta hai. Campaign aur creative ki tulna ROAS se karein; poore ad programme ka faisala ROI se karein.

  • ROAS = revenue ÷ ad spend. Tez, platform ko pasand, kharch chhodta hai.
  • ROI = (profit ÷ spend) × 100. Dheema, par sach batata hai.

Break-even ROAS: woh number jo tay karta hai ki ads faayade ke hain

Break-even ROAS woh ROAS hai jahaan campaign na munafa deta hai na nuksan. Yeh sirf aapke gross margin par tika hai: Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross margin.

60% gross margin par break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.60 = 1.67x. 1.67x se neeche har campaign us bikri par paisa khota hai; usse upar munafa jodata hai. 25% margin par yeh 4x par pahunch jaata hai, isliye kam margin wale seller ko munafe mein rahane ke liye kaheen ooncha ROAS chahiye.

  • 60% margin → break-even ROAS 1.67x.
  • 50% margin → break-even ROAS 2x.
  • 25% margin → break-even ROAS 4x.

ROAS ki aam galatiyaan

  1. ROAS ko munafa samajhana. Yeh revenue hai, aur maal ki lagat isamen se jaati hai.
  2. Margin bhool jaana. Wahi 3x ROAS 60% par jeet hai aur 20% par haar.
  3. Sirf ROAS par platform ki tulna karna, jabaki har platform revenue alag ginata hai.
  4. ROAS ke peechhe itana bhaagana ki kharch gire, revenue usse tez gire, aur growth ruk jaye.

Chhote karobar mein ROAS kaise istemal karein

Apne break-even se upar target ROAS tay karein, un kharchon ke liye thode jagah chhoden jo platform nahi ginata, aur poore account ke bajaay har campaign par nazar rakhein. Jab koi campaign break-even paar kare, to kharch dheere-dheere badhayein aur ROAS tika rahe yeh dekhein; jab na kare, to budget badhane se pehle creative ya audience theek karein.
