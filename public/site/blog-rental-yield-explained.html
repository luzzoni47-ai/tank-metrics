Rental Yield Explained: What Your Flat Earns

Yearly rent divided by the price you paid — the one number that says whether a flat is a good buy.

Real EstateNovember 15, 2026

A flat that rents for ₹9,000 a month sounds like a good buy. Whether it is depends on what you paid for it — and rental yield is the one number that compares the two. It turns a monthly rent into a yearly percentage of the property price, so a ₹15 lakh flat and a ₹27 lakh flat can be judged side by side.

This guide shows the formula, works one flat through from rent to yield, and explains why the number a broker quotes is usually the gross one, while the money you actually keep is lower.

What rental yield actually tells you

Rental yield is the yearly rent a property earns, divided by what it cost, shown as a percentage. It answers a simple question: if you bought this flat for cash and rented it out, what return would the rent give you each year?

It is useful because rent and price move separately. A flat's rent might rise 5% while its price rises 15%, and the yield quietly falls. Watching the yield tells you which side of the deal has become expensive.

The formula, in one line

The whole calculation is one division:

  • Gross rental yield = (monthly rent × 12) ÷ property price × 100
  • A ₹9,000 rent on a ₹27 lakh flat is ₹1.08 lakh a year, so ₹1.08 lakh ÷ ₹27 lakh = 4%.

Two rules keep it honest. Use the rent you can actually collect, not the rent the broker hopes for. And use the full price you pay, including stamp duty and registration, not just the quoted price.

A worked example: ₹9,000 a month on a ₹27 lakh flat

Say a one-bedroom flat costs ₹27 lakh all in, and it rents for ₹9,000 a month.

  • Monthly rent: ₹9,000
  • Yearly rent: ₹9,000 × 12 = ₹1.08 lakh
  • Property price: ₹27 lakh
  • Gross yield: ₹1.08 lakh ÷ ₹27 lakh = 4%

That 4% is before any cost. It is the number most listings quote, and it is the starting point, not the finish line.

Gross yield and net yield are not the same

Between the rent you collect and the money you keep sit three quiet costs: the months the flat sits empty, the repairs and repainting, and the property tax and society charges you pay as owner.

Put a rough quarter of the rent aside for those three, and the picture changes:

  • Gross yield: 4%
  • Costs set aside: about a quarter of the rent
  • Net yield: about 3%

The 1% gap is the difference between what a brochure shows and what reaches your bank. Always ask which one you are being told.

Same rent, two prices

Yield is most useful when you compare two properties that earn the same rent. Take the same ₹9,000 a month:

  • A flat at ₹18 lakh earns ₹1.08 lakh a year, so a 6% gross yield.
  • A flat at ₹27 lakh earns the same ₹1.08 lakh, so a 4% gross yield.

Identical rent, two very different returns. The cheaper flat is working harder for your money — the only question is whether the price difference is real, or hiding a worse location, an older building or a bigger repair bill.

Where rental yield misleads you

The number is a guide, not a verdict. Three things it leaves out:

  • Price growth. A 3% yield in a fast-growing area can beat a 6% yield in a flat market, once you sell.
  • Loan interest. If you borrowed to buy, the interest comes out of the rent before anything reaches you.
  • Effort. A tenant who pays late, a broken geyser and a vacant month are all costs a bank fixed deposit never charges.

Compare the yield with a fixed deposit rate, not with zero. If a flat yields less than a bank deposit and prices are flat, you are being paid nothing for the work.

The short version

Rental yield is yearly rent divided by price. A ₹9,000 rent on a ₹27 lakh flat is 4% gross, and about 3% after vacancy and repairs. Use it to compare two properties on the same rent, and check it against what a fixed deposit pays before you decide the flat is a bargain.

Yeh guide formula dikhati hai, ek phlait ko kiraae se yeeld tak pura hisaab karti hai, aur yeh bhi samjhati hai ki brokar jo aankda batata hai woh aam taur par gros hota hai, jabaki aapke haath mein aane wala paisa usse kam hota hai.

Rental yeeld asal mein kya bataate hai

Rental yeeld saalana kiraae ko proparte ki keemat par bhaag dekar pratishat mein nikaale jaati hai. Yeh seedha sawal poochhate hai: agar yeh phlait nakad khareeda jaye aur kiraae par diya jaye, to kiraya har saal kitna ritarn dega?

Yeh kaam ki baat hai kyunki kiraya aur keemat alag-alag chalte hain. Kiraya 5% badh sakta hai jabaki keemat 15% badh jaye, aur yeeld chupchaap gir jaati hai. Yeeld dekhane se pata chalta hai ki saude ka kaun saa hissa mahanga ho gaya hai.

Formula, ek laain mein

Pura hisaab ek bhaag ka hai:

  • Gros rental yeeld = (mahine ka kiraya × 12) ÷ proparte ki keemat × 100
  • ₹27 laakh ke phlait par ₹9,000 ka kiraya saal mein ₹1.08 laakh banata hai, yani ₹1.08 laakh ÷ ₹27 laakh = 4%.

Do niyam ise imandar rakhte hain. Jo kiraya vaakaee mil sakta hai wahi len, brokar ki ummeed nahi. Aur woh poore keemat len jo aap chukaate hain, staamp dyoote aur rajistreshan samet, sirf bataaee gayi keemat nahi.

Ek udaharan: ₹27 laakh ke phlait par ₹9,000 mahina

Maan lijiye ek bedaroom phlait sab milaakar ₹27 laakh ka pada, aur woh ₹9,000 mahine mein kiraae par chala.

  • Mahine ka kiraya: ₹9,000
  • Saalana kiraya: ₹9,000 × 12 = ₹1.08 laakh
  • Proparte ki keemat: ₹27 laakh
  • Gros yeeld: ₹1.08 laakh ÷ ₹27 laakh = 4%

Yeh 4% kisi lagat se pehle ka hai. Zyadatar listing yahi aankda bataate hai, aur yeh shuruaat hai, manzil nahi.

Gros yeeld aur net yeeld ek nahi hain

Jo kiraya aap vasoolate hain aur jo paisa aap rakhte hain, unake beech teen chupchaap baithe laagaten hain: ve mahine jab phlait khaale rehta hai, marammat aur rangaaee, aur proparte tax tatha sosaaite chaarj jo maalik bharata hai.

In teenon ke liye kiraae ka kareeb ek chauthai alag rakhein, to tasveer badal jaati hai:

  • Gros yeeld: 4%
  • Laagaton ke liye alag: kiraae ka kareeb ek chauthai
  • Net yeeld: kareeb 3%

Yeh 1% ka farq wahi hai jo broshar dikhata hai aur jo aapke bank tak pahunchata hai. Hamesha poochein ki aapko kaun saa aankda bataya ja raha hai.

Ek hi kiraya, do keematen

Yeeld tab sabse kaam ki hai jab do proparte ek hi kiraya kamaate hon. Wahi ₹9,000 mahina len:

  • ₹18 laakh ka phlait saal mein ₹1.08 laakh kamata hai, yani 6% gros yeeld.
  • ₹27 laakh ka phlait wahi ₹1.08 laakh kamata hai, yani 4% gros yeeld.

Kiraya ek, ritarn do. Sasta phlait aapke paise par zyada mehnat kar raha hai — sawal sirf yeh hai ki keemat ka farq asli hai, ya usamen kharaab lokeshan, puraane imaarat ya bada marammat kharch chhipa hai.

Rental yeeld kahan bhatakaate hai

Yeh aankda raasta dikhata hai, faisala nahi karta. Teen cheezein yeh chhod dete hai:

  • Keemat ki badhotri. Tezi se badhte ilaake mein 3% yeeld, sthir bazaar ki 6% yeeld ko bechane ke baad hara sakate hai.
  • Lon ka byaj. Agar khareedane ke liye udhaar liya, to byaj kiraae mein se pehle nikalta hai.
  • Mehnat. Der se bharane wala kiraayedaar, toota geezar aur ek khaale mahina — ye sab laagaten hain jo bank ki FD kabhi nahi lete.

Yeeld ki tulna shoony se nahi, bank ki FD dar se karein. Agar phlait FD se kam de raha hai aur keematen sthir hain, to aapko is mehnat ka kuch nahi mil raha.

Chhoti baat

Rental yeeld saalana kiraya bhaag keemat hai. ₹27 laakh ke phlait par ₹9,000 ka kiraya 4% gros hai, aur khaale mahine tatha marammat ke baad kareeb 3%. Isase ek hi kiraae wali do proparte ki tulna karein, aur yeh dekhein ki FD kya dete hai — tab tay karein ki phlait sauda hai ya nahi.
