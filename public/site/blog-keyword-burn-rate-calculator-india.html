Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate burn rate?

Net Burn = Monthly Expenses − Monthly Income. If expenses are ₹5 lakh and income is ₹2 lakh, the net burn is ₹3 lakh a month.

How much runway should a startup have?

Indian startups should keep at least twelve months of runway, because a new round takes three to six months to close. Under six months is the danger zone.

What is the difference between gross burn and net burn?

Gross burn is the total spend; net burn is spend minus income. Net burn is the real number because it shows how fast your cash is falling.

On Shark Tank India, the question founders get asked most often is not about valuation — it is "monthly burn kitna hai?" Sharks ask it because a great story with a broken burn is still a dead business. Skippi Ice Pops halved its burn after the show and then grew 40x. Your burn rate and your runway are the two numbers that decide how much time you really have.

What is burn rate calculator for startups india?

Burn rate is how much cash your business spends each month beyond what it earns. There are two versions: gross burn, the total spend, and net burn, the spend minus income. Net burn is the honest number, because it is what actually drains your bank account every month.

If you spend ₹5 lakh a month and earn ₹2 lakh, your net burn is ₹3 lakh. If you have ₹30 lakh in the bank, you have ten months of runway. Tank Metrics shows both the net burn and the runway in ₹ Lakhs, the way an Indian founder checks their own account, not in dollars.

Runway is the second half of the same question. Burn tells you how fast the money is leaving; runway tells you how many months that leaves you. A founder who knows both can raise the next round from a position of strength instead of from panic.

A useful habit is to write the burn and the runway down on the first of every month, before the invoices arrive. It takes two minutes and it turns a vague worry into a number you can act on. Founders who do this catch a rising burn two or three months early, which is exactly the window where a small fix is still cheap.

Formula

The formulaNet Burn = Monthly Expenses − Monthly Income

Runway in months = Cash in Bank ÷ Net Burn.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

An Indian startup carries monthly liabilities that US burn tools ignore: GST, TDS and PF payments. Left out of the expense figure, they make the burn look smaller than it is. Adding them back gives the true picture of an Indian founder's month.

In India, a runway of more than twelve months is considered safe, because closing a funding round here takes three to six months. A US benchmark of six months is too tight for India. Tank Metrics keeps the runway and the burn in rupees, with the Indian timeline in mind.

India also has strong seasonal cash swings. The festive months bring revenue and the months after bring returns and slower sales, so a single month's burn can flatter the picture. An average of three months gives a truer runway than any one month does.

Common Mistakes

  • Counting a one-off grant or a tax refund as monthly income, which hides the real burn.
  • Leaving founder salaries out of the expense figure, so the burn looks smaller than it is.
  • Measuring the burn once and never again. It moves every month and the runway moves with it.
