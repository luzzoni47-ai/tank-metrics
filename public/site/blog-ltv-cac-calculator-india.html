Frequently asked questions

What is a good LTV to CAC ratio in India?

3:1 or better is the usual rule. Below 1:1 you lose money on every customer; between 1 and 3 you are spending too much to grow. Low-margin businesses need a higher ratio than sticky ones.

How do I calculate LTV for an Indian D2C brand?

Average order value × purchases per year × customer lifespan in years × gross margin %. Use profit, not revenue, or the ratio overstates what a customer is worth.

What should CAC include?

Every cost of winning a customer: ad spend, the sales team's salaries, agency fees and introductory discounts - divided by the new customers won in the same period.

What is ltv cac calculator india?

The LTV:CAC ratio puts two numbers side by side: what a customer is worth to you over their whole life (LTV), and what it cost to win them (CAC). Divide one by the other and you get the single figure investors in Indian D2C and SaaS ask for first.

A ratio of 3:1 or better is the common rule of thumb. Below 1:1 you lose money on every customer you win; between 1 and 3 you are spending too much to grow. A D2C brand with an LTV of ₹4,500 and a CAC of ₹1,500 is at 3:1 - healthy. A quick-commerce brand with ₹800 of LTV and ₹1,200 of CAC is at 0.67:1 - it loses money on every order.

The ratio only means something when both sides are measured the same way. LTV should be built on gross-margin profit, not revenue, and CAC should include ads, salaries and agency fees - not just the ad bill. Measure one side generously and the other tightly and the ratio lies.

Formula

The formulaLTV : CAC = Lifetime Value ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost

Use profit-based LTV and fully-loaded CAC. An LTV of ₹4,500 over a ₹1,500 CAC gives 3 : 1. Below 1:1 the business loses money on every customer; 3:1 and above is the range investors look for.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Indian D2C founders usually track revenue and order counts, not unit economics, because that is what the marketplace dashboard shows. The LTV:CAC ratio forces the two numbers that decide survival onto one screen.

Indian SaaS companies often bill in dollars but pay for talent and ads in rupees, so the ratio gets distorted if the two sides are converted carelessly. Keep both sides in ₹ over the same period and the number is honest.

Indian investors have become far stricter about this ratio since the funding winter - a 1.2:1 pitch that raised money in 2021 does not raise it now. Having the number ready, and honest, is the difference between a second meeting and a polite no.

Common Mistakes

  • Using revenue-based LTV against a fully-loaded CAC, which makes a loss-making business look healthy.
  • Leaving sales salaries and agency fees out of CAC, so the cost of winning a customer looks far lower than it is.
  • Using a blended CAC that hides a channel where you lose money on every single customer.
