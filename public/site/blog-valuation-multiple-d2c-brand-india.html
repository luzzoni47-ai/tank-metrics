Frequently asked questions

What is a good valuation multiple for a D2C brand in India?

It depends on growth, repeat purchases and margin, and early brands usually get a lower multiple than established ones. Test a few values in the calculator rather than trusting a single figure.

How do you calculate a D2C brand valuation?

Apply the multiple to your yearly revenue. At ₹10 crore of revenue and a 3x multiple, the valuation works out to ₹30 crore.

Why is D2C valued lower than software?

D2C carries inventory, shipping, returns and marketplace fees, so less of each rupee of revenue becomes profit, which pulls the multiple down.

What is valuation multiple for d2c brand india?

A valuation multiple for a D2C brand is the number of times the brand's yearly revenue that an investor is willing to pay for the whole company. If your brand sells ₹10 crore a year and an investor applies a 3x multiple, the offer values you at ₹30 crore. The multiple is the single most argued number in a D2C funding round, and it is set by growth, repeat purchase rate, gross margin and how crowded your category is.

D2C is a harder business to value than software because it carries real inventory, shipping, returns and marketplace fees. A brand that sells ₹10 crore but spends heavily on ads to get there will be offered a lower multiple than one that grows on repeat customers. That is why two Indian D2C brands with identical revenue can be valued several crores apart, and why founders who understand the multiple negotiate better.

Formula

The formulaValuation = Yearly Revenue × Valuation Multiple

Revenue in ₹ crore, the multiple as a plain number such as 3x. The product is the valuation in ₹ crore.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

India's D2C playbook is different from the US one because a large share of sales still flows through marketplaces and quick commerce, each taking a commission. A US template that values on gross sales ignores those fees and quietly overstates the valuation, so Indian founders need to apply the multiple to the revenue they actually keep.

Indian investors also weight repeat purchase and contribution margin far more heavily than they used to, after a wave of D2C brands grew fast on discounts and then stalled. A brand with steady repeat buyers earns a better multiple than a brand that depends on fresh ad spend every single month.

GST registration and timely filing matter here too, because investors verify revenue against filed returns. A multiple on revenue that cannot be matched to your GST filings will not survive due diligence, however good the pitch sounded.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying the multiple to projected revenue instead of actual sales, which inflates the number and collapses in due diligence.
  • Forgetting marketplace commissions and returns, so the revenue you multiply is bigger than the revenue you keep.
  • Assuming one multiple is the truth. The multiple is negotiated, so always arrive with a range in mind.
