Frequently asked questions

What is a cap table for an Indian startup?

It is the sheet listing every shareholder and their percentage, including founders, investors and the ESOP pool. It shows who owns what before and after each round.

Who does the ESOP pool come out of?

In India the ESOP pool is usually carved out of the founder's stake, so your own percentage falls by the pool size as well as by the investor's equity.

How do you calculate founder dilution?

Founder Stake After Round = Founder Stake × (100 - Investor % - ESOP %). With 15% to an investor and a 5% pool, founders holding 100% drop to 80%.

What is cap table calculator for indian startups?

A cap table is the sheet that lists every person and fund that owns a piece of your company, and the exact percentage each one holds. Before a round it usually holds only the founders. After a round it holds the founders, the new investor and the ESOP pool, all diluted to make room. A cap table calculator for Indian startups does that dilution in one step, so you can see the picture before you sign anything.

The number founders underestimate most is the ESOP pool. Employee stock options are almost always carved out of the founder's stake, not the investor's, so a 10% pool can quietly take your own holding down by far more than the investor's cheque does. Seeing founder, investor and ESOP percentages on one sheet makes that cost visible instead of surprising.

A clean cap table is also what due diligence checks first. Investors, and later a chartered accountant or a merchant banker, will ask who owns what and whether the percentages add up. Keeping the table current, in whole numbers and in ₹ terms, saves weeks of confusion during a raise, because every figure the investor sees is one you can stand behind.

Formula

The formulaFounder Stake After Round = Founder Stake × (100 - Investor % - ESOP %)

All three figures are percentages of the post-money company, and the founder stake is the pre-round holding you are diluting.

Worked Example (in ₹ Crores)

Take a company where the founders hold 100% before the round. A new investor takes 15% and an ESOP pool of 5% is created, and the table shows who owns what afterwards.

The founders started with the whole company and finished with 80%, because 15% went to the investor and 5% went to the ESOP pool. That 20% fall is the real cost of the round, and it is easier to accept when you see it before the paperwork.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Indian cap tables are built on CCPS, or compulsorily convertible preference shares, rather than plain equity, because that is the instrument investors prefer and the one that converts to equity later. A US template that models only common shares will not match the instrument your Indian investor actually receives.

Indian startups also usually create their ESOP pool before the priced round, and it is standard for that pool to come out of the founder's holding. Founders who do not model this early are often shocked when their own percentage falls by more than the investor's cheque alone would suggest.

There is a compliance layer too. Share allotments must be reported, valuations may need certification, and the register of members has to stay accurate. A cap table that matches your filings keeps a chartered accountant happy and stops small errors becoming expensive ones.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting the ESOP pool when modelling dilution, so the founder stake you expect is higher than the stake you actually keep.
  • Tracking only the investor's percentage and not the whole table, which hides how much the founders have really given up.
  • Letting the cap table drift out of line with the company's filings, which slows due diligence and can delay a round.
