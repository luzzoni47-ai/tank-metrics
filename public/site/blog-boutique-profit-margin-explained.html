Boutique Profit Margin: What You Really Keep

What a boutique keeps on every piece, what the rent quietly takes, and the sales volume that covers it all.

Profit & MarginsOctober 28, 2026

A boutique is not a shop that sells expensive clothes. It is a shop that sells a small number of pieces, each one made or finished by hand, at a price the customer cannot compare with a mall. That is a good business — but the margin on it is almost never what the owner thinks it is.

This guide works one boutique through the whole calculation: what a single piece really earns, what the rent and the electricity take every month, how many pieces cover those costs, and the one thing that quietly erases the margin — stock that does not sell.

A boutique margin has two layers

When people say "margin" they usually mean the gap between the price and the cost of the piece. That is the gross margin, and it is only the first layer. The second layer is what is left after the costs that run whether you sell anything or not: the rent, the electricity, the helper's salary. What survives both layers is your net margin, and that is the only one you actually spend.

A boutique can look very healthy on gross margin and still make nothing, because the fixed costs are heavy and the number of pieces sold is small. Both numbers have to be on the same page before the margin means anything.

A ₹2,000 kurta, worked through

Say a boutique sells a hand-finished kurta for ₹2,000. The fabric, the tailoring and the finishing come to ₹1,200. So the piece earns ₹800 before any of the shop's own costs — a gross margin of 40%.

  • Selling price: ₹2,000
  • Fabric, tailoring and finishing: ₹1,200
  • Gross profit per piece: ₹2,000 − ₹1,200 = ₹800
  • Gross margin: ₹800 ÷ ₹2,000 = 40%

That ₹800 is real, but it is not the owner's money yet. It still has to carry the shop.

Where the fixed costs sit

Fixed costs are the ones that arrive every month whether the till rings or not. For a small boutique they usually look like this:

  • Shop rent: ₹5,000
  • Electricity and water: ₹1,000
  • Helper's salary: ₹2,000
  • Total fixed cost per month: ₹5,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹8,000

₹8,000 a month is the hurdle. Until the pieces cover it, every sale is only paying back the shop itself, not the owner.

The break-even volume

Break-even is the number of pieces whose gross profit exactly equals the fixed costs. Divide one by the other:

  1. Fixed cost per month: ₹8,000
  2. Gross profit per piece: ₹800
  3. Break-even: ₹8,000 ÷ ₹800 = 10 pieces

Sell 10 pieces and the boutique has covered the month and made nothing. Sell 12 and the extra 2 pieces bring ₹1,600 more, so the month leaves ₹1,600: 12 × ₹800 = ₹9,600, and ₹9,600 − ₹8,000 = ₹1,600. The first 10 pieces were never profit; they were the rent.

Dead stock is what actually kills the margin

Now the part most boutique owners do not price in. Say the shop stitches 12 pieces for the month but sells only 10. The 10 sold bring in ₹8,000 of gross profit, which is exactly the ₹8,000 of fixed costs — so the month ends at ₹0, not ₹1,600.

The 2 unsold pieces also tie up ₹2,400 of fabric and tailoring that is sitting on a shelf: 2 × ₹1,200 = ₹2,400. That money is not lost, but it is not working either, and next month it competes with fresh stock for the same small rack.

Why the margin looks better than it is

  1. The owner's own time is free. If the owner does the stitching and does not count it, the ₹800 per piece is flattered. Value that time at a real wage and the gross margin drops.
  2. Only the pieces that sell get counted. Dead stock means the true cost per sold piece is higher than the ₹1,200 on the bill. Ten pieces sold from twelve stitched carry part of the two that did not move.
  3. A full-price margin is assumed. Boutiques discount to clear slow stock, and as every discount does, it comes straight out of the ₹800, never out of the customer.

The short version

A boutique's margin is two layers: the ₹800 gross profit on a ₹2,000 piece, and what is left of it after ₹8,000 of fixed costs. Ten pieces a month is the break-even. Above that the shop earns; below it, the rent eats the stock. And two pieces of dead stock are enough to turn a ₹1,600 month into a ₹0 one.

Count the pieces that sell, not the pieces on the rack — and check the break-even before you decide the month went well.

Boutique koi aise dukaan nahi hai jo mahange kapade bechti hai. Yeh woh dukaan hai jo thode se pieces bechti hai, har ek haath se bana ya pura kiya hua, aise keemat par jise graahak mol se tulna nahi kar sakta. Yeh achha karobar hai — par isaka margin lagabhag kabhi woh nahi hota jo maalik sochta hai.

Yeh guide ek boutique ka pura hisaab dikhati hai: ek piece asal mein kitna kamata hai, kiraya aur bijli har mahine kitna le jaate hain, kitne pieces un kharchon ko dhakte hain, aur woh ek cheez jo margin ko chupchaap mita dete hai — woh stock jo bikta nahi.

Boutique ke margin ki do paraten

Jab log "margin" kahte hain to aam taur par keemat aur piece ki lagat ke beech ka antar matlab hota hai. Woh gross margin hai, aur woh sirf pehli parat hai. Doosare parat woh hai jo un kharchon ke baad bachate hai jo chalte rehte hain, chaahe aap kuch bechen ya nahi: kiraya, bijli, helper ki salary. Dono paraton ke baad jo bachta hai wahi aapka net margin hai, aur kharch sirf wahi hota hai.

Ek boutique gross margin par bahut swasth dikh sakate hai aur phir bhi kuch na kamaye, kyunki fixed kharch bhaari hote hain aur bikne wale pieces kam. Margin ka matlab tab tak nahi banata jab tak dono aankde ek saath na dekhe jayein.

₹2,000 ka kurta, pura hisaab

Maan lijiye ek boutique haath se pura kiya kurta ₹2,000 mein bechti hai. Kapada, silai aur finishing milkar ₹1,200 padate hain. Yani dukaan ke apne kharchon se pehle piece ₹800 kamata hai — 40% ka gross margin.

  • Bikri keemat: ₹2,000
  • Kapada, silai aur finishing: ₹1,200
  • Har piece par gross profit: ₹2,000 − ₹1,200 = ₹800
  • Gross margin: ₹800 ÷ ₹2,000 = 40%

Woh ₹800 asli hai, par abhe maalik ka paisa nahi hai. Use pehle dukaan ko chalaana hai.

Fixed kharch kahan baithate hain

Fixed kharch ve hain jo har mahine aate hain, chaahe galla baje ya nahi. Ek chhoti boutique mein ve aam taur par aise hote hain:

  • Dukaan ka kiraya: ₹5,000
  • Bijli aur paani: ₹1,000
  • Helper ki salary: ₹2,000
  • Har mahine kul fixed kharch: ₹5,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹8,000

₹8,000 mahine ki rukaavat hai. Jab tak pieces ise dhak na dein, har bikri sirf dukaan ko vaapas chuka rahi hai, maalik ko nahi.

Break-even ka volume

Break-even woh sankhya hai jis par pieces ka gross profit theek fixed kharchon ke baraabar ho jaata hai. Ek ko doosare se bhaag deejie:

  1. Har mahine fixed kharch: ₹8,000
  2. Har piece par gross profit: ₹800
  3. Break-even: ₹8,000 ÷ ₹800 = 10 pieces

10 pieces bechie to mahine ka kharch dhak gaya aur kuch nahi bacha. 12 bechie to extra 2 pieces ₹1,600 aur laate hain, yani mahina ₹1,600 deta hai: 12 × ₹800 = ₹9,600, aur ₹9,600 − ₹8,000 = ₹1,600. Pehle 10 pieces kabhi profit nahi the; ve kiraya the.

Margin ko asal mein woh stock maarata hai jo bikta nahi

Ab woh hissa jise zyadatar boutique maalik hisaab mein nahi lete. Maan lijiye dukaan mahine ke 12 pieces silate hai par bikate sirf 10. Bike 10 pieces ₹8,000 ka gross profit laate hain, jo theek ₹8,000 ke fixed kharch ke baraabar hai — yani mahina ₹0 par band hota hai, ₹1,600 par nahi.

Ve 2 anabike pieces ₹2,400 ka kapada aur silai bhi baandh lete hain jo shelf par pada hai: 2 × ₹1,200 = ₹2,400. Woh paisa khoya nahi hai, par kaam bhi nahi kar raha, aur agle mahine woh usi chhote rack par nae stock se takrata hai.

Margin asal se behtar kyon dikhta hai

  1. Maalik ka apna samay muft maana jaata hai. Agar maalik khud silai karta hai aur use hisaab mein nahi leta, to har piece ka ₹800 badha-chadha dikhta hai. Us samay ko asli wage deejie to gross margin gir jaata hai.
  2. Sirf bikne wale pieces gine jaate hain. Anabike stock ka matlab hai ki bike piece ki asli lagat bil par likhe ₹1,200 se zyada hai. Baarah mein se bike das pieces un do ka bhi hissa dhote hain jo chale nahi.
  3. Poore keemat ka margin maan liya jaata hai. Boutique dheeme stock ko nikalne ke liye discount dete hai, aur har discount ki tarah woh seedhe ₹800 se jaata hai, graahak se kabhi nahi.

Chhoti baat

Boutique ka margin do paraton ka hai: ₹2,000 ke piece par ₹800 ka gross profit, aur ₹8,000 ke fixed kharch ke baad usamen se jo bacha. Mahine ke 10 pieces break-even hain. Usse upar dukaan kamaate hai; neeche kiraya stock kha jaata hai. Aur 2 anabike pieces ₹1,600 ke mahine ko ₹0 ka bana dete hain.

Rack par pade pieces nahi, bikne wale pieces ginie — aur yeh tay karne se pehle ki mahina achha gaya, break-even dekhie.
