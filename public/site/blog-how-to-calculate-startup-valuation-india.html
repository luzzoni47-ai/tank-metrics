Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my startup's valuation in India?

Work out annual revenue and annual profit, apply a sector multiple to each, then compare the answers with a recent deal in your sector. The band between the lowest and highest result is your valuation range.

What if my startup has no revenue yet?

Use an early-stage method such as the scorecard or the Berkus approach, which scores the team, the product and the market, and expect a range of roughly ₹2 crore to ₹5 crore for a strong early team.

Is a valuation from this calculator final?

No. It is a negotiation range for your own planning. A real round in India may also need a chartered accountant or a merchant banker to certify the figure.

On Shark Tank India a founder's valuation claim is tested with one blunt question: what does the business actually earn? A pitch that asks for ₹1 crore in exchange for 4% is claiming a ₹25 crore valuation, and the sharks immediately ask for revenue, margin and repeat orders to see whether that claim can hold. This page explains how to calculate the valuation of a startup in India, from revenue and profit multiples to the early-stage methods Indian angels use, and it does every step in ₹ Crores.

What is how to calculate valuation of startup in india?

To calculate the valuation of a startup in India, you begin with what the business earns today and then apply a multiple that reflects its sector, growth and risk. Revenue and profit are the two starting points. A services firm with steady contracts is valued differently from a consumer brand spending heavily on advertising, even when both report exactly the same revenue.

The order of the calculation matters. First work out the annual revenue and the annual profit, then pick a multiple, then multiply, then compare the answer with a real recent deal in the same sector. Only after those steps should you argue about a number. Founders who start from the number they want and work backwards usually end up defending a figure they cannot explain.

Formula

The formulaValuation = Revenue × Multiple, or Profit × Multiple

Revenue and profit in ₹ crore, the multiple a plain number. The lowest and highest answers become your valuation band.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

A US guide to startup valuation assumes a Delaware company, a 409A valuation and dollars. An Indian founder works with a private limited company, shares issued as CCPS, and figures written in lakh and crore, so the same method produces a number that has to be read completely differently.

In India the first outside money often comes from family and friends, then an angel network. Angel networks commonly ask for 10% to 20% equity and sometimes a board seat, and they usually want the ESOP pool created in the same round, before the new investor's stake is fixed.

If the company later raises from a registered venture fund, the round may need a valuation report from a merchant banker for regulatory filings. That report is a formal check on the range the founder negotiated, not a replacement for it.

Common Mistakes

  • Starting from the valuation you want and working backwards, instead of starting from revenue and profit.
  • Using a single multiple from a listed company without adjusting for the startup's size and risk.
  • Ignoring the ESOP pool, so the founder's stake after the round is smaller than the model suggests.
