GST From Nov 1: Easier Discounts, Faster Refunds

From November 1, a post-sale discount needs only a credit note, inverted-duty refunds release 90% early, and the ₹1,000 export refund floor is scrapped.

Tax RulesOctober 28, 2026

Three changes to India's GST law take effect on November 1, 2026. They come from Notification No. 03/2026–Central Tax dated October 26, 2026, which brought sections 153, 154 and 155 of the Finance Act, 2026 into force and amended sections 15, 34 and 54 of the CGST Act, 2017. The measures were first announced in the 2026-27 budget.

For a shop, a distributor or a small exporter, they matter for one reason: they move the work from the front of a sale to the back. Paperwork after the invoice now decides how much tax you actually pay.

What changes on November 1

  • Post-sale discounts: the old conditions — a discount agreed in advance and tied to specific invoices — are dropped. A credit note from the supplier and a reversal of input tax credit by the buyer are now the test.
  • Credit notes: section 34 now expressly covers a discount given under the new valuation rule.
  • Refunds: a 90% provisional refund is extended to businesses whose input tax is higher than their output tax.
  • Export refunds: the ₹1,000 minimum that blocked tiny refunds no longer applies to tax paid on goods exported out of India.

The credit note is now the whole game

Under the old section 15(3)(b), a discount given after the supply could reduce the taxable value only if it was agreed before the sale, linked to particular invoices, and the buyer reversed the credit. Those first two conditions are gone. What remains is that the supplier must issue a credit note and the buyer must reverse the input tax credit attributable to the discount.

That is a real relief for year-end and volume schemes. A manufacturer who promises dealers an incentive in April, but only knows the payout in March, no longer has to defend the arrangement as if the exact figure were fixed up front.

The arithmetic on a ₹10 lakh discount

Say a dealer earns a ₹10 lakh discount for the year, expressed before tax. At 18% GST, the tax on that discount is ₹1,80,000, so the supplier issues a credit note for ₹11,80,000.

The supplier then reduces taxable value and output tax by ₹1,80,000, provided the credit note is issued in time. The dealer reverses ₹1,80,000 of input tax credit. If the dealer had already claimed that credit, the reversal keeps the government's books whole; if the dealer does not reverse it, the supplier loses the benefit.

Before November 1 the same discount could be questioned because it was not fixed before the sale. Now the test is simpler: was a credit note issued, and did the buyer reverse the credit?

Faster refunds when your input tax is higher

When the tax on your inputs is higher than the tax on what you sell, credit piles up month after month. Section 54(6) already allowed a 90% provisional refund for zero-rated supplies. The amendment extends that facility to inverted duty structure refunds, so the law itself now backs a faster release.

Take a business with ₹1 crore of credit stuck every year. Under the new rule, ₹90 lakh of it can come back early once conditions are met and the claim is verified. The remaining ₹10 lakh follows after verification. For a firm that borrows to fund working capital, getting ₹90 lakh early can cut the interest it pays.

Early is not final. A refund later found to be too high can be recovered, so reconcile your returns before you file, not after a notice arrives.

Small exporters: the ₹1,000 floor goes

Section 54(14) said no refund under ₹1,000 would be paid. From November 1 that limit does not apply to the refund of tax on goods exported out of India with payment of tax. A small exporter shipping low-value consignments will no longer see a refund held back just because it fell under the threshold.

The exception is narrow. It covers goods exported with payment of tax, not refunds on the export of services, and not other refund categories. Do not stretch it.

What it means for your shop

Write your discount schemes down, even though advance agreement is no longer a legal condition, and set a calendar for credit notes. Check that any buyer who takes a discount reverses the matching input tax credit, or the tax saving disappears. If you carry a large pile of input credit because of the inverted duty structure, plan the refund claim and reconcile first. If you export goods, list the small refunds that were blocked earlier and claim them from November 1.

None of this removes paperwork. It moves the burden to the back of the sale: credit notes, reversals and reconciliations now carry the weight.

1 November 2026 se GST kanoon mein teen badlav lagu honge. Ye Notification No. 03/2026–Central Tax, 26 October 2026 se aaye hain, jisne Finance Act 2026 ki sections 153, 154 aur 155 ko lagu kiya aur CGST Act 2017 ki sections 15, 34 aur 54 ko badla. Ye kadam 2026-27 budget mein announce hue the.

Dukaan, distributor ya chhote exporter ke liye baat ek hai: kaam sale ke aage se hat kar peeche aa gaya hai. Ab invoice ke baad ka paperwork tay karta hai ki aap kitna tax bharenge.

1 November se kya badlega

  • Post-sale discount: purani shartein — discount pehle se tay ho aur khaas invoices se juda ho — hata di gayi hain. Ab supplier ka credit note aur buyer dwara input tax credit ka reversal hi kasauti hai.
  • Credit note: section 34 ab naye valuation rule ke discount ko saaf-saaf cover karta hai.
  • Refund: 90% provisional refund un businesses ko bhi milega jinka input tax output tax se zyada hai.
  • Export refund: ₹1,000 ki minimum hadd, jo chhote refund rok deti thi, ab India se bahar bheje gaye goods par bhare tax par lagu nahi hogi.

Ab credit note hi asli khel hai

Purane section 15(3)(b) mein supply ke baad ka discount taxable value kam kar sakta tha sirf tab, jab wo sale se pehle tay ho, khaas invoices se juda ho, aur buyer credit reverse kare. Pehli do shartein khatam. Ab sirf itna chahiye: supplier credit note de aur buyer discount ka input tax credit reverse kare.

Ye saal ke ant aur volume schemes ke liye asli rahat hai. Manufacturer April mein dealers ko incentive ka wada karta hai, par pura hisaab March mein banta hai — ab use ye sabit karne ki zarurat nahi ki figure pehle se fix tha.

₹10 lakh discount ka hisaab

Maan lijiye ek dealer ko saal ka ₹10 lakh discount milta hai, tax se pehle. 18% GST par us discount ka tax ₹1,80,000 banta hai, to supplier ₹11,80,000 ka credit note deta hai.

Supplier taxable value aur output tax ₹1,80,000 se kam kar deta hai, bas credit note time par milna chahiye. Dealer ₹1,80,000 ka input tax credit reverse karta hai. Agar dealer ne wo credit pehle le liya tha, to reversal sarkar ka hisaab barabar rakhta hai; agar dealer reverse nahi karta, to supplier ko fayda nahi milega.

1 November se pehle yahi discount sawal mein aa sakta tha kyunki wo sale se pehle tay nahi tha. Ab test aasan hai: credit note aaya, aur buyer ne credit reverse kiya ya nahi?

Jab input tax output se zyada ho to refund jaldi

Jab input par tax output se zyada ho, to credit har mahine jama hota rehta hai. Section 54(6) pehle se zero-rated supply ke liye 90% provisional refund deta tha. Amendment ab inverted duty structure refund par bhi ye suvidha deta hai, to kanoon khud tez release ko support karta hai.

Maan lijiye ek business ka ₹1 crore credit har saal atka rehta hai. Naye rule se ₹90 lakh jaldi wapas aa sakta hai, jab shartein poori ho aur claim verify ho jaye. Baaki ₹10 lakh verification ke baad milta hai. Jo firm working capital ke liye udhaar leti hai, uske liye ₹90 lakh jaldi milna byaaj bacha sakta hai.

Jaldi ka matlab final nahi. Baad mein zyada nikla refund recover ho sakta hai, isliye return notice se pehle, filing se pehle mila lijiye.

Chhote exporters: ₹1,000 ki hadd khatam

Section 54(14) kehta tha ki ₹1,000 se kam refund nahi milega. 1 November se ye hadd India se bahar bheje gaye goods par bhare tax ke refund par lagu nahi hogi. Kam value ke consignment bhejne wale chhote exporter ka refund ab sirf is wajah se nahi rukega ki wo threshold se neeche tha.

Chhoot sankri hai. Ye sirf un goods par hai jo tax bharkar export hue, services ke export par nahi, aur baaki refund categories par nahi. Isko badha-chadha kar mat samjhiye.

Aapki dukaan ke liye iska matlab

Apni discount schemes likh kar rakhiye, chahe pehle se tay karna ab kanooni shart na ho, aur credit note ke liye calendar banaiye. Dekhiye ki discount lene wala buyer matching input tax credit reverse kare, warna tax ki bachat gayab ho jayegi. Agar inverted duty structure ki wajah se aapka input credit bahut jama hai, to refund claim plan kijiye aur pehle mila lijiye. Agar aap goods export karte hain, to pehle ruke chhote refunds ki list bana kar 1 November se claim kijiye.

Isse paperwork khatam nahi hota. Bojh sale ke peeche chala jata hai: credit notes, reversals aur reconciliations hi ab asli kaam hain.
