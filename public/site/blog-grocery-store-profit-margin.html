Grocery Store Profit Margin: What You Keep

A kirana runs on a few rupees per sale. Here is the margin on one sale, the fixed costs, and the break-even a small grocery store must hit.

A grocery store sells almost everything a household needs, which is why it is one of the first businesses a family starts in India. It is also one of the thinnest businesses there is. A shop can turn over a good month and still have nothing left, because a kirana runs on a few rupees of profit per sale and the rent arrives whether or not a single customer walks in.

This guide works one small grocery store through both numbers: the margin on a single sale, and what is left of the month after the rent, the electricity and the helper are paid. It then shows the number of sales that covers the shop, and the one that leaves the owner working for nothing.

A grocery shop earns two margins, not one

The gap between what a customer pays and what the goods cost is the gross margin, and it is the number owners quote. What survives the rent and the bills is the net margin, and it is the only one you can spend. A shop can look healthy on gross margin and still make nothing, because the fixed costs are paid in full whether the counter is busy or empty.

A ₹100 sale, worked through

Say one typical basket sells for ₹100. The goods inside it were bought for ₹80. So the sale earns ₹20 before the shop's own costs.

  • Selling price: ₹100
  • Cost of the goods: ₹80
  • Gross profit: ₹100 − ₹80 = ₹20
  • Gross margin: ₹20 ÷ ₹100 = 20%

Twenty percent is normal for grocery, and it is also not the owner's money yet.

The fixed costs that arrive every month

Fixed costs are the ones that come whether you sell one basket or a hundred:

  • Shop rent: ₹6,000
  • Electricity and water: ₹1,000
  • Helper's salary: ₹2,000
  • Total fixed cost per month: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 a month is the hurdle. Until the sales cover it, every rupee of gross profit is only paying the shop back, not the owner.

The break-even: how many sales a day

Break-even is the number of sales whose gross profit exactly equals the fixed costs. Divide one by the other:

  1. Fixed cost per month: ₹9,000
  2. Gross profit per sale: ₹20
  3. Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹20 = 450 sales a month

450 sales a month is about 15 a day. Sell fewer than that and the shop is still short; the first 450 sales of the month were never profit, they were the rent.

A slow month, and what it costs

Sell 480 baskets and the month works: 480 × ₹20 = ₹9,600 of gross profit, and ₹9,600 − ₹9,000 leaves ₹600. Sell 400 instead — a quiet month, a shut market week — and the same shop brings ₹8,000 of gross profit against ₹9,000 of fixed costs. That is a loss of ₹1,000, and the rent was still paid in full.

Three things that quietly cut the margin

  1. The owner's own hours are not counted. If the owner stands behind the counter all day, that time is a cost even though no salary leaves the till.
  2. Goods that spoil or go unsold. A pack that is thrown away was bought at full price and earned nothing. In grocery this is the quietest way to lose money.
  3. Credit given to regulars. Money owed for weeks is money not buying the next stock, and some of it is never collected.

Know the gross profit on each line, not just the total. Push the lines with the better margin, order less of the ones that spoil, and count the fixed costs every month so the break-even figure stays in your head. A grocery store is a volume business, but the owner who knows the margin per sale is the one who keeps something at the end.

Kiraana dukaan ghar ki lagabhag har zaroorat bechti hai, isliye bhaarat mein parivaar ka pehla karobar aksar yahi hota hai. Yeh sabse patla karobar bhi hai. Achha mahina bhi nikal jaye to haath mein kuch nahi bachta, kyunki har bikri par kuch hi rupaye ka munafa hota hai aur kiraya graahak aaye ya na aaye, har haal mein dena padta hai.

Yeh guide ek chhoti kiraana dukaan ke dono aankade nikaalate hai: ek bikri par hua munafa, aur mahine ke aakhir mein kiraya, bijli aur sahaayak ko dene ke baad kya bacha. Phir woh sankhya dikhati hai jo dukaan ka kharch nikaal dete hai, aur woh jis par maalik muft mein kaam karta rah jaata hai.

Kiraana dukaan ka munafa do hote hain, ek nahi

Graahak jo deta hai aur maal jis daam par aaya uska farq gross margin hai, aur yahi sankhya maalik batata hai. Kiraya aur bil kaatane ke baad jo bachta hai woh net margin hai, aur sirf wahi kharch kiya ja sakta hai. Dukaan gross margin par swasth dikh sakate hai aur phir bhi kuch na kamaye, kyunki tay kharch pura dena padta hai, chaahe kaauntar bhara ho ya khaale.

₹100 ki ek bikri, pura hisaab

Maan lijiye ek aam bikri ₹100 ki hoti hai. Usamen rakha maal ₹80 mein aaya tha. Yani dukaan ke kharch se pehle us bikri par ₹20 banate hain.

  • Bikri keemat: ₹100
  • Maal ki lagat: ₹80
  • Gross profit: ₹100 − ₹80 = ₹20
  • Gross margin: ₹20 ÷ ₹100 = 20%

Kiraana mein bees pratishat samanya hai, aur yeh bhi abhe maalik ka paisa nahi hai.

Har mahine aane wale tay kharch

Tay kharch ve hain jo tab bhi aate hain jab aap ek bikri karein ya sau:

  • Dukaan ka kiraya: ₹6,000
  • Bijli aur paani: ₹1,000
  • Sahaayak ka vetan: ₹2,000
  • Mahine ka kul tay kharch: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 mahine ki rukaavat hai. Jab tak bikri ise nahi nikaalate, har rupaya dukaan ko hi vaapas kar raha hai, maalik ko nahi.

Break-even: din mein kitni bikri

Break-even woh sankhya hai jisake gross profit theek tay kharch ke baraabar ho jaate hain. Ek ko doosare se baantie:

  1. Mahine ka tay kharch: ₹9,000
  2. Ek bikri par gross profit: ₹20
  3. Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹20 = mahine ki 450 bikri

Mahine ki 450 bikri yani din ki lagabhag 15. Isase kam bechen to dukaan ab bhi kam hai; mahine ki pehli 450 bikri kabhi munafa nahi theen, ve kiraya theen.

Dheema mahina, aur uski keemat

480 bikri karein to mahina chal jaata hai: 480 × ₹20 = ₹9,600 gross profit, aur ₹9,600 − ₹9,000 se ₹600 bachate hain. Isake bajaay 400 bechen — shaant mahina, band bazaar ka hafta — to wahi dukaan ₹8,000 gross profit par ₹9,000 tay kharch laate hai. Yeh ₹1,000 ka nuqasaan hai, aur kiraya pura dena pada.

Teen cheezein jo chupchaap margin kaatate hain

  1. Maalik ke apne ghante gine nahi jaate. Maalik din bhar kaauntar par khada rahe to woh samay bhi kharch hai, bhale galle se koi vetan na jaye.
  2. Kharaab ya bin bika maal. Jo paiket phenka gaya woh poore daam par aaya aur kuch na kamaaya. Kiraana mein paisa khone ka yeh sabse chup tareeqa hai.
  3. Niyamit graahakon ko udhaar. Hafton tak bakaaya paisa agla maal khareed nahi raha, aur usamen se kuch kabhi vasool nahi hota.

Har laain par gross profit jaanie, sirf kul nahi. Jinamen margin behtar hai unhein badhaaie, jo kharaab hote hain unaka order ghataaie, aur tay kharch har mahine ginie taaki break-even ki sankhya dimaagh mein rahe. Kiraana maatra ka karobar hai, par jo maalik prati bikri margin jaanta hai, ant mein usi ke haath kuch bachta hai.
