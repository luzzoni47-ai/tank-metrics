What Import Duty Really Adds to Your Order

Duty and IGST, in the order they are charged — with a worked example that shows why the second one is bigger than you expect.

Pricing & CostsOctober 26, 2026

If you buy goods from abroad to resell in India, the amount on the supplier's invoice is not what the order costs you. Two charges land on top before the goods reach your warehouse: customs duty, and then IGST. Both are calculated in a way that catches first-time importers out, because the second one is charged on the first one.

This guide walks through the order the charges arrive in, shows a small worked example, and explains why there is no single "import duty" number you can look up.

The two charges, and the order they arrive in

Customs duty is a percentage of the product value. IGST is then charged on the product value plus the duty — not on the product value alone. That one detail is why an order that looks like a modest cost increase often turns out to be closer to forty percent.

A worked example

Say you import goods worth ₹5,000, with a 20% customs duty rate and 18% IGST.

  1. Customs duty: 20% of ₹5,000 = ₹1,000.
  2. Value plus duty: ₹5,000 + ₹1,000 = ₹6,000.
  3. IGST: 18% of ₹6,000 = ₹1,080.
  4. Total landed cost: ₹6,000 + ₹1,080 = ₹7,080.

A ₹5,000 order therefore costs ₹7,080 — ₹2,080 more, which is about 41.6% of the product value. Look closely at step three: the IGST is not 18% of ₹5,000, which would have been ₹900. It is ₹1,080, because the duty was added to the base first.

Run the same order at a smaller value and the pattern holds. On ₹1,000 of goods, duty is ₹200, and IGST is 18% of ₹1,200 — ₹216. The total is ₹1,416, not the ₹1,180 a simple "add 18%" would give you.

Why the duty rate is never one number

The rate depends on the HSN code of your product. Two items that look identical on a shelf can sit in different categories with very different rates. There is no single "import duty percentage" in India, which is exactly why you should confirm the rate for your own HSN code with a customs broker before you price anything.

What the exchange rate does to this

If you pay in dollars, the rupee rate is a third variable, and it does not sit still. A weaker rupee raises the rupee value of the same dollar invoice, and both the duty and the IGST are then charged on that larger figure. The two charges compound the currency move rather than absorbing it.

How to price against it

Price against the landed cost, not the invoice value. If you set your selling price from the ₹5,000 you paid, you have already given away the ₹2,080 of duty and tax before shipping, storage or your own time are counted. Put the duty and the IGST into the cost line first, then decide what the product can sell for.

Keep the two charges separate in your own records as well. The IGST you pay on an import is generally available as input credit if you are registered, so it is a cash-flow cost rather than a final one, while the customs duty is not. Treating them as a single lump hides that difference.

Agar aap baahar se maal mangaakar bhaarat mein bechate hain, to saplaayar ke bil par likhe rakam aapke order ki asli lagat nahi hai. Maal aapke godaam tak pahunchane se pehle do cheezein upar judate hain: kastams dyoote, aur usake baad IGST. Dono ka hisaab ek aise tarike se lagta hai jo pehli baar aayaat karne wale ko chakama de deta hai, kyunki doosara pehle wale par lagta hai.

Yeh guide bataate hai ki ye chaarj kis kram mein aate hain, ek chhota udaharan dikhati hai, aur samjhati hai ki aap koi ek "aayaat shulk" ka aankda kyon nahi khoj sakte.

Do chaarj, aur unaka kram

Kastams dyoote maal ki keemat ka ek pratishat hoti hai. Usake baad IGST maal ki keemat aur dyoote ke jod par lagta hai — sirf maal ki keemat par nahi. Yahi ek baat hai jisake vajah se jo order sirf thoda mehanga lagta hai woh asal mein lagabhag chaalees pratishat tak pahunch jaata hai.

Ek udaharan

Maan lijiye aap ₹5,000 ka maal aayaat karte hain, jis par 20% kastams dyoote aur 18% IGST hai.

  1. Kastams dyoote: ₹5,000 ka 20% = ₹1,000.
  2. Keemat aur dyoote: ₹5,000 + ₹1,000 = ₹6,000.
  3. IGST: ₹6,000 ka 18% = ₹1,080.
  4. Kul lagat: ₹6,000 + ₹1,080 = ₹7,080.

Yani ₹5,000 ka order asal mein ₹7,080 padta hai — ₹2,080 zyada, jo maal ki keemat ka lagabhag 41.6% hai. Teesare charan ko dhyaan se dekhie: IGST, ₹5,000 ka 18% nahi hai, varana woh ₹900 hota. Yeh ₹1,080 hai, kyunki pehle dyoote ko joda gaya.

Chhoti rakam par bhi yahi dharra chalta hai. ₹1,000 ke maal par dyoote ₹200 hai, aur IGST, ₹1,200 ka 18% yani ₹216 hai. Kul ₹1,416 banata hai, woh ₹1,180 nahi jo sirf 18% jodne par aata.

Dar kabhi ek aankda kyon nahi hoti

Dar aapke maal ke HSN kod par nirbhar karti hai. Dukaan mein ek jaise dikhane wale do saamaan alag-alag shreniyon mein ho sakte hain, aur unake daren bahut alag hoti hain. Bhaarat mein koi ek "aayaat shulk pratishat" nahi hai, iseelie keemat tay karne se pehle apne HSN kod ki dar kisi kastams brokar se zaroor pakke kar len.

Isamen eksachenj ret ka asar

Agar aap dolar mein bhugataan karte hain, to rupaye ki dar teesara badalta hua pahaloo ban jaata hai. Kamzor rupaya usi dolar bil ki rupaye wali keemat badha deta hai, aur phir dyoote aur IGST dono us bade aankde par lagte hain. Dono chaarj karense ke is badlav ko sokhate nahi, use aur badha dete hain.

Isake hisaab se keemat kaise tay karein

Keemat bil ki rakam par nahi, asli lagat par tay karein. Agar aap apni bikri keemat us ₹5,000 se tay karte hain jo aapne diya, to shiping, bhandaaran ya apne samay se pehle hi aap ₹2,080 dyoote aur tax ke roop mein ganva chuke hain. Pehle dyoote aur IGST ko lagat mein jodie, phir tay keejie ki maal kitne mein bik sakta hai.

Apne rikord mein bhi dono ko alag rakhie. Agar aap rajistard hain to aayaat par diya gaya IGST aam taur par inaput kredit ke roop mein mil jaata hai, isliye woh kaish-flo ki lagat hai, aakhire nahi, jabaki kastams dyoote aakhire hai. Dono ko ek dher maanakar chalane par yeh farq chhip jaata hai.
