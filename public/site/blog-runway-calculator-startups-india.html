Frequently asked questions

What is a good runway for a startup in India?

Most Indian investors want at least 12 months of runway, and 18 months is comfortable. Under six months, you are usually raising from a position of weakness.

How do I calculate runway in months?

Divide cash in the bank by your monthly net burn. With ₹60 lakh of cash and ₹5 lakh of monthly net burn, the runway is 12 months.

Should I use gross burn or net burn for runway?

Use net burn for the realistic picture, because revenue reduces how fast cash actually leaves. Gross burn tells you the size of the outflow before any money comes in.

What is runway calculator startups india?

A runway calculator for startups in India answers one blunt question: at your current spending, how many months can your business survive on the cash it already holds? The calculation is cash in the bank divided by monthly burn. If you have ₹60 lakh and you spend ₹5 lakh a month, your runway is 12 months. Nothing about the maths is complicated, but the number it produces decides whether you hire, whether you cut, or whether you must raise money in the next quarter.

The subtlety is gross burn versus net burn. Gross burn is every rupee that leaves the company each month. Net burn is what leaves after you subtract the money coming in from sales. A company with ₹8 lakh of monthly expenses and ₹3 lakh of monthly revenue has a gross burn of ₹8 lakh but a net burn of only ₹5 lakh, so its real runway is longer than a casual look at its bank statement suggests. This calculator works on the burn you give it, so enter net burn if you want the honest survival number.

Indian founders usually discover runway the hard way, at the end of a month when salaries, rent, cloud bills and GST payments all land together. Seeing the number in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores, before the money is gone, is the difference between raising on your own terms and raising in a hurry. Investors ask for this number in every meeting, so it pays to know it cold before you are asked.

Formula

The formulaRunway (Months) = Cash in Bank ÷ Monthly Net Burn

Cash and burn both in ₹. If net burn is zero the business is cash-flow positive and runway is effectively unlimited.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

A US runway template assumes salaries, rent and software in dollars and a monthly payroll that looks nothing like an Indian one. In India a startup's biggest line items are usually team salaries, office rent in a metro, and cloud and marketing spends, and the mix shifts sharply once you leave a tier-1 city. A runway number built on dollar assumptions is not your runway at all.

Indian investors also read runway differently. A seed-stage angel is comfortable if you have 12 to 18 months and a clear plan to reach the next milestone, while a bridge round is usually raised when you are down to about six months. Founder salaries in India are deliberately kept modest early on, which quietly stretches runway in a way a US template will not show you.

There is a tax angle too. GST on services and TDS on many payments leave the bank account on a schedule, so the true monthly outflow is often higher than the invoice total. Add those to your burn before you trust the runway figure, because a runway that ignores tax is a runway that lies.

Common Mistakes

  • Counting booked revenue as cash. Sales that have not been collected are not money in the bank, and a long payment cycle can leave you short even when the order book looks healthy.
  • Forgetting one-off costs such as annual insurance, software renewals and quarterly GST payments, which make the real burn spike in some months and drag the average down in others.
  • Assuming the next round will close exactly on schedule. Add two to three months of buffer to your runway, because funding always takes longer than the plan says.
