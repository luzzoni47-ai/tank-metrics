A coffee shop looks like a high-margin business. A cup sells for ₹100 and the coffee, milk and cup inside it cost about ₹40, so every cup appears to earn ₹60. The mistake is stopping there. That ₹60 has to cover the rent, the electricity and the staff before any of it belongs to the owner — and on a quiet day there are not enough cups to do it.

This guide works one small coffee shop through both numbers: the margin on a single cup, and the margin on the month after the fixed costs are paid. It then shows the number of cups a day that covers the shop, and the one that leaves the owner working for nothing.

A coffee shop earns two margins, not one

The gap between the cup's price and what went into it is the gross margin, and it is the number owners quote. What survives the rent and the bills is the net margin, and it is the only one you can spend. A shop can look healthy on gross margin and still make nothing, because the fixed costs are paid in full whether the machine runs or not.

A ₹100 cup, worked through

Say a cup sells for ₹100. The beans, milk, sugar and the disposable cup come to ₹40. So the cup earns ₹60 before the shop's own costs.

  • Selling price: ₹100
  • Beans, milk and cup: ₹40
  • Gross profit per cup: ₹100 − ₹40 = ₹60
  • Gross margin: ₹60 ÷ ₹100 = 60%

Sixty percent is a good-looking number. It is also not the owner's money yet.

The fixed costs that arrive every month

Fixed costs are the ones that come whether you sell one cup or a hundred:

  • Shop rent: ₹6,000
  • Electricity and water: ₹1,000
  • Helper's salary: ₹2,000
  • Total fixed cost per month: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 a month is the hurdle. Until the cups cover it, every sale is only paying the shop back, not the owner.

The break-even: how many cups a day

Break-even is the number of cups whose gross profit exactly equals the fixed costs. Divide one by the other:

  1. Fixed cost per month: ₹9,000
  2. Gross profit per cup: ₹60
  3. Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹60 = 150 cups a month

150 cups a month is about 5 cups a day. Sell fewer than that and the shop is still short; the first 150 cups of the month were never profit, they were the rent.

A slow month, and what it costs

Sell 160 cups and the month works: 160 × ₹60 = ₹9,600 of gross profit, and ₹9,600 − ₹9,000 leaves ₹600. Sell 120 cups instead — a rainy month, a shutdown week — and the same shop brings ₹7,200 of gross profit against ₹9,000 of fixed costs. That is a loss of ₹1,800, and the rent was still paid in full.

Three things that quietly cut the margin

  1. The owner's own hours are not counted. If the owner stands behind the machine for free, the ₹600 looks better than it is. Pay that time a real wage and the month may be flat.
  2. Waste is invisible. Spilled milk, expired beans and a cup given away do not appear on the ₹40. Every wasted cup is ₹60 of gross profit that never arrived, and a handful a day makes the break-even target quietly wrong.
  3. A discount comes out of the ₹60, not the customer. A ₹20 discount takes the gross profit on the cup from ₹60 to ₹40, so it takes 15 discounted cups to match the ₹600 that 10 full-price cups bring.

The short version

A ₹100 cup earns ₹60 before costs, a 60% gross margin. The shop carries ₹9,000 of fixed costs a month, so 150 cups a month — about 5 a day — is break-even. At 160 cups the month leaves ₹600; at 120 cups it loses ₹1,800. Watch the waste and the discounts, because both come straight out of the ₹60, and neither one is paid by the customer.

Count the cups, know your break-even, and check the net figure before you decide the shop had a good month.

Ek kofe shop dekhane mein high margin business lagti hai. Ek kap ₹100 mein bikta hai aur usake andar ki kofe, doodh aur kap milaakar lagabhag ₹40 ka hota hai, to har kap ₹60 kamata dikhta hai. Galat wahin hoti hai jab hisaab yahin rok diya jaye. Woh ₹60 pehle kiraya, bijli aur staff ko kavar karna hai, tab jaakar kuch maalik ke paas bachta hai — aur dheeme din mein utane kap hote hi nahi.

Yeh guide ek chhoti kofe shop ko dono nambaron par le jaati hai: ek kap ka margin, aur fiksd kharche nikalne ke baad mahine ka margin. Phir woh kap par de dikhati hai jo shop ko kavar karta hai, aur woh number jo maalik se muft kaam karavaata hai.

Kofe shop do margin kamaate hai, ek nahi

Kap ki keemat aur usamen lage saamaan ke beech ka farq gross margin hai, aur yahi number maalik batate hain. Kiraya aur bil nikalne ke baad jo bachta hai woh net margin hai, aur sirf wahi kharch ho sakta hai. Shop gross margin par swasth dikh sakate hai aur phir bhi kuch na kamaye, kyunki fiksd kharche poore lagte hain chaahe machine chale ya na chale.

₹100 ka kap, pura hisaab

Maano ek kap ₹100 mein bikta hai. Beens, doodh, cheene aur dispozebal kap milaakar ₹40 ke hain. To kap shop ke kharche se pehle ₹60 kamata hai.

  • Bikri keemat: ₹100
  • Beens, doodh aur kap: ₹40
  • Prati kap gross profit: ₹100 − ₹40 = ₹60
  • Gross margin: ₹60 ÷ ₹100 = 60%

Saath parasent achha number lagta hai. Yeh abhe maalik ka paisa nahi hai.

Fiksd kharche jo har mahine aate hain

Fiksd kharche ve hain jo aate hain chaahe ek kap bike ya sau:

  • Shop ka kiraya: ₹6,000
  • Bijli aur paani: ₹1,000
  • Helpar ki salary: ₹2,000
  • Kul fiksd kharch prati mahina: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 mahine ka hardal hai. Jab tak kap ise kavar nahi karte, har bikri sirf shop ko vaapas kar rahi hai, maalik ko nahi.

Brek-eevan: ek din mein kitne kap

Brek-eevan ve kap hain jinaka gross profit fiksd kharche ke baraabar ho. Ek ko doosare se divaaid karein:

  1. Fiksd kharch prati mahina: ₹9,000
  2. Gross profit prati kap: ₹60
  3. Brek-eevan: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹60 = 150 kap mahine mein

150 kap mahine mein matlab lagabhag 5 kap roz. Isase kam bechen to shop abhe bhi kam hai; mahine ke pehle 150 kap kabhi profit nahi the, ve kiraya the.

Dheema mahina, aur uski keemat

160 kap bechen to mahina chal jaata hai: 160 × ₹60 = ₹9,600 gross profit, aur ₹9,600 − ₹9,000 ke baad ₹600 bachta hai. Iski jagah 120 kap bechen — baarish ka mahina, band ka hafta — to wahi shop ₹9,000 fiksd kharche ke saamane ₹7,200 gross profit laate hai. Yeh ₹1,800 ka nuqasaan hai, aur kiraya pura lag chuka hai.

Teen cheezein jo chupake se margin kaat lete hain

  1. Maalik ke apne ghante nahi gine jaate. Agar maalik muft mein machine ke peechhe khada hai to ₹600 asli se behtar lagta hai. Us samay ki asli mazadoore lagaao to mahina flait ho sakta hai.
  2. Vest dikhta nahi. Gira hua doodh, kharaab beens aur muft diya gaya kap ₹40 mein nahi dikhte. Har vest kap ₹60 ka gross profit hai jo kabhi aaya hi nahi, aur roz ke kuch kap brek-eevan ka taaraget chupake se ghalat kar dete hain.
  3. Discount ₹60 se nikalta hai, kastamar se nahi. ₹100 ke kap par ₹20 ka discount gross profit ko ₹60 se ₹40 kar deta hai, to 10 ful-praais kap ke ₹600 ko maich karne ke liye 15 diskaaunted kap chahiye.

Chhota saa nichod

₹100 ka kap kharche se pehle ₹60 kamata hai, 60% gross margin. Shop par mahine ke ₹9,000 fiksd kharche hain, to 150 kap mahine mein — lagabhag 5 roz — brek-eevan hai. 160 kap par mahina ₹600 chhodta hai; 120 kap par ₹1,800 ka nuqasaan hota hai. Vest aur discount dono par nazar rakhein, kyunki dono seedhe ₹60 se nikalte hain, aur dono ka bhugataan kastamar nahi karta.

Kap ginen, apna brek-eevan jaanein, aur yeh tay karne se pehle ki mahina achha tha, net number dekhein.
