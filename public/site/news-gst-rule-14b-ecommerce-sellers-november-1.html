GST Registration for Small E-Commerce Sellers Opens Nov 1

Small sellers can register in a new state from November 1 using the platform's warehouse as their place of business, if monthly credit is under ₹2.5 lakh.

Tax RulesOctober 23, 2026

From November 1, 2026, a small seller who sells only through Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho or another e-commerce platform can register for GST in a new state without opening an office or a shop there. A senior finance ministry official said on October 22 that the system changes needed for the new route are ready and the facility will go live on the GST portal from the beginning of next month.

The route was proposed by the 57th GST Council earlier in October, under a new Rule 14B of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017. It is separate from the enforcement and refund changes the same Council approved. This one is about the paperwork that stops a seller from shipping to customers outside their home state.

What actually changes on November 1

Under the rules as they stand, a seller who wants to hold stock in another state and sell from there needs a GST registration in that state. That usually means a rented premises, an address proof and a separate compliance routine. Many small sellers stay in one state for exactly this reason.

Rule 14B offers an optional alternative. A small seller of goods who supplies exclusively through electronic commerce operators can declare the warehouse of the e-commerce platform as their principal place of business in that state. They do not need to set up their own office or shop there. The consent of the e-commerce operator is obtained through the system, so the platform has to agree before the registration goes through.

Prashanth Agarwal, a partner at PwC India, called it a radical reform that can have a positive impact beyond GST on the economy at large. He said e-commerce sellers are generally reluctant to expand beyond their home state because of the compliance requirement, and that letting platform players support these sellers through the GSTN platform should go a long way in solving that.

Who qualifies, and the ₹2.5 lakh rule

Three conditions decide whether this is for you. First, you must already have a physical presence and a GST registration in at least one state, and that state will remain your home state. Second, the registration obtained this way can be used only for supplies made through e-commerce platforms, not for your own counter sales in that state. Third, you must pass on input tax credit of not more than ₹2.5 lakh a month.

The third condition is the one to check with your accountant, because it is a ceiling on credit, not a ceiling on turnover.

The arithmetic of the ₹2.5 lakh ceiling

Input tax credit is the GST you have already paid on your purchases, which you set off against the GST you collect on your sales. At 18%, every ₹10,00,000 of purchases carries ₹1,80,000 of credit, because 10,00,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = 1,80,000. So a monthly credit ceiling of ₹2.5 lakh works out to roughly ₹13,00,000 to ₹14,00,000 of purchases a month at 18%.

Across a year, ₹2.5 lakh a month is ₹30,00,000 of credit. If your purchases run above that, this simplified route is not for you and you will keep registering the ordinary way. If you sell goods at 5% or 12%, the same ₹2.5 lakh of credit covers more purchases, because each rupee of purchase carries less tax.

What it costs you in extra filings

Every additional state registration means another GSTIN, and another monthly return. That is real work, and it is why a seller should add a state only when the sales there justify it. Say you sell ₹5,00,000 of goods in a month in one state and charge 18% GST. The tax inside that month's bills is ₹90,000, because 5,00,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = 90,000, and the customer pays ₹5,90,000 in all.

Now set that against the credit you would claim on the stock you moved into the state. If that stock cost ₹4,00,000, the credit on it is ₹72,000. The extra return you file each month is the price of holding stock locally and shipping in a day instead of a week.

What it means for your shop or startup

If you sell only through marketplaces and your monthly input tax credit stays under ₹2.5 lakh, this is the cheapest way to start selling in a second state. The first thing to check is whether your platform has opted into the consent process, because the registration cannot go through without it. Detailed circulars explaining the process and the related notifications are expected in the next few days, so read them before you apply rather than after.

Separately, notifications and circulars covering the refund changes approved by the Council are also expected soon, which matters if you sell zero-rated or exported goods. And the long-running dispute over Section 16(2)(c), which lets a genuine buyer lose credit when a supplier does not deposit tax, has gone to a committee of officers. Its report is expected within three months, it will then go to the Council, and any decision is targeted for implementation from April 1, 2027. That is the bigger number for most sellers, and it is still open.

1 November 2026 se, jo chhota seller sirf Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho ya kisi aur e-commerce platform par bechta hai, wo naye state mein GST registration karwa sakta hai bina wahan office ya dukaan khole. Ek senior finance ministry official ne 22 October ko kaha ki naye raaste ke liye zaroori system changes taiyar hain aur ye facility agle mahine ki shuruaat se GST portal par live ho jayegi.

Ye raasta 57th GST Council ne October ke shuru mein propose kiya tha, Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017 ke naye Rule 14B ke tehat. Ye usi Council ke enforcement aur refund wale changes se alag hai. Ye us paperwork ke baare mein hai jo seller ko apne home state ke bahar customers ko bhejne se rokta hai.

1 November se asli mein kya badalta hai

Abhi ke rules ke hisaab se, jo seller doosre state mein stock rakh kar wahan se bechna chahta hai, usse us state mein GST registration chahiye. Iska matlab aam taur par kiraye ka premises, address proof aur alag compliance routine. Bahut se chhote sellers sirf isi wajah se ek hi state mein rehte hain.

Rule 14B ek optional raasta deta hai. Jo chhota seller sirf electronic commerce operators ke through goods bechta hai, wo us state mein e-commerce platform ke warehouse ko apna principal place of business declare kar sakta hai. Unhe wahan apna office ya dukaan set up karne ki zaroorat nahi hai. E-commerce operator ki consent system ke through li jaati hai, isliye platform ko pehle haan kehna padta hai.

PwC India ke partner Prashanth Agarwal ne ise radical reform kaha jo GST se aage ja kar poori economy par positive asar daal sakta hai. Unhone kaha ki e-commerce sellers aam taur par compliance requirement ki wajah se apne home state se bahar jaane mein hichakte hain, aur platform players ko GSTN ke through in sellers ki madad karne dene se ye problem kaafi had tak hal ho sakti hai.

Kaun qualify karta hai, aur ₹2.5 lakh ka rule

Teen shartein tay karti hain ki ye aapke liye hai ya nahi. Pehli, aapke paas kam se kam ek state mein physical presence aur GST registration pehle se hona chahiye, aur wahi state aapka home state rahega. Doosri, is tarah liya gaya registration sirf e-commerce platforms ke through ki gayi supplies ke liye use ho sakta hai, us state mein aapki apni counter sales ke liye nahi. Teesri, aapko mahine mein ₹2.5 lakh se zyada input tax credit pass on nahi karna chahiye.

Teesri shart apne accountant se check karein, kyunki ye credit par limit hai, turnover par nahi.

₹2.5 lakh ceiling ka hisaab

Input tax credit wo GST hai jo aap apni kharidari par pehle hi de chuke ho, aur jise aap apni sales par collect kiye gaye GST se set off karte ho. 18% par, har ₹10,00,000 ki kharidari par ₹1,80,000 ka credit banta hai, kyunki 10,00,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = 1,80,000. Isliye ₹2.5 lakh ki monthly credit limit 18% par lagbhag ₹13,00,000 se ₹14,00,000 ki mahine ki kharidari ke barabar hai.

Saal bhar mein ₹2.5 lakh mahina matlab ₹30,00,000 ka credit. Agar aapki kharidari isse zyada hai, to ye simplified raasta aapke liye nahi hai aur aap purane tareeke se hi registration karte rahenge. Agar aap 5% ya 12% ke goods bechte ho, to wahi ₹2.5 lakh ka credit zyada kharidari cover karta hai, kyunki har rupaye ki kharidari par kam tax hota hai.

Extra filings ka kitna kharcha

Har extra state registration ka matlab ek aur GSTIN, aur ek aur monthly return. Ye asli kaam hai, isliye seller ko state tabhi add karna chahiye jab wahan ki sales ise justify karein. Maano aap ek state mein ek mahine mein ₹5,00,000 ke goods bechte ho aur 18% GST lagate ho. Us mahine ke bills mein tax ₹90,000 hai, kyunki 5,00,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = 90,000, aur customer kul ₹5,90,000 deta hai.

Ab ise us credit se compare karo jo us state mein bheje gaye stock par milega. Agar wo stock ₹4,00,000 ka tha, to uspar credit ₹72,000 hai. Har mahine ki ye extra return us price ka hissa hai jo aap local stock rakhne aur hafte ki jagah ek din mein ship karne ke liye dete ho.

Aapki dukaan ya startup ke liye iska matlab

Agar aap sirf marketplaces par bechte ho aur aapka monthly input tax credit ₹2.5 lakh se neeche rehta hai, to doosre state mein bechna shuru karne ka ye sabse sasta raasta hai. Sabse pehle ye dekho ki aapka platform consent process mein shamil hua hai ya nahi, kyunki iske bina registration nahi ho sakta. Process samjhane wale detailed circulars aur related notifications agle kuch dinon mein aane ki umeed hai, isliye apply karne se pehle padho, baad mein nahi.

Alag se, Council ne jo refund changes approve kiye hain, unke notifications aur circulars bhi jaldi aane ki umeed hai, jo aapke liye matter karta hai agar aap zero-rated ya export goods bechte ho. Aur Section 16(2)(c) ka purana vivaad, jisme supplier tax jama na kare to ek genuine buyer ka credit chala jaata hai, officers ki committee ko bhej diya gaya hai. Uski report teen mahine mein aane ki umeed hai, phir ye Council ke saamne jaayegi, aur koi bhi faisla 1 April 2027 se lagu karne ka target hai. Zyadatar sellers ke liye yahi bada number hai, aur ye abhi khula hai.
