Frequently asked questions

How much equity should I give for ₹1 crore?

It depends on your valuation: Equity % = (1 crore ÷ valuation) × 100. At a ₹10 crore valuation it is 10%, at ₹20 crore it is 5%, and at ₹50 crore it is only 2%.

Is giving more equity ever fine?

Early on, 15-20% is normal because the investor takes real risk. But giving more than 30% for ₹1 crore is expensive for your long-term ownership.

Does giving equity take away my control?

Giving 10% does not by itself hand over control, but a board seat and veto rights can come with the deal. This calculator shows your remaining stake so you can plan.

On Shark Tank India, Revamp Moto gave just 1.5% for ₹1 crore, because its valuation was about ₹66.7 crore. The Sass Bar gave 35% for ₹50 lakh. Same show, and the equity for a rupee is not fixed at all — it depends entirely on what your company is worth. This calculator tells you the honest number for your business.

What is how much equity to give for 1 crore?

How much equity you give for ₹1 crore is decided by your valuation, not by the cheque size. If your company is worth ₹10 crore and you raise ₹1 crore, you should be giving about 10%. If it is worth ₹20 crore, the same ₹1 crore costs you only 5%. Raise the valuation and the equity for the same money falls.

There is also a practical floor to keep in mind. Investors rarely accept much below 1% for a meaningful cheque, because a tiny stake is hard to track and harder to sell later. So even a very high valuation has a minimum equity below which the deal stops making sense for the investor.

The cleanest way to hold your equity is to arrive at the negotiation with your own valuation already worked out from revenue and profit. A founder who can show the arithmetic keeps far more of the company than one who accepts the first percentage on the table.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

In India an equity round is not just a percentage. Along with the equity come a board seat, veto rights and a liquidation preference, all of which sit on top of the headline number. A plain US calculator that returns only a percentage misses every one of those Indian deal layers.

For a small Indian business, angel investors are comfortable taking 10% to 20% for a ₹1 crore seed round. Once revenue and profit grow, the same cheque becomes 5% or even 2%. That shift is normal in India, and it is the reason valuation work pays for itself.

India also taxes a transfer differently from a fresh issue. A new issue of shares is not a taxable event for the founder, but a secondary sale of existing shares is, so the structure of the round quietly changes the real cost of the equity you give away.

Common Mistakes

  • Deciding the equity before the valuation. Do it in that order and you will almost always overpay.
  • Forgetting that the ESOP pool is carved out of the founder's stake, not the investor's, so your own percentage falls further than the headline suggests.
  • Comparing your equity to a US deal that used a completely different valuation method.
