Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

In the US, founder wealth is usually described through a valuation set by a 409A exercise and reported in dollars. In India the same discussion happens in ₹ Crores and in stakes that are often held through a holding company or a family trust, so a direct percentage comparison with a US founder is misleading.

Indian founders usually hold a large part of their wealth in unlisted shares, which cannot be sold at will. A stake value is a paper number until a secondary sale, an IPO or a buyback turns it into cash, and even then the price is negotiated rather than quoted on a screen.

When unlisted shares are actually sold, the gain is taxed as a capital gain, and the buyer may deduct tax at source. So the rupee value of a stake and the cash a founder finally receives are never exactly the same figure.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating a paper stake value as cash in the bank, when unlisted shares cannot be sold on demand.
  • Using a listed company's revenue multiple for an unlisted startup without any size discount.
  • Ignoring the ESOP pool and preference shares, which sit ahead of the founder's own shares in a sale.
