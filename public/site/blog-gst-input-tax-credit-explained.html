Input Tax Credit: Why You Get GST Back

What ARPU means, how to work it out from your own numbers, and the levers that quietly move it.

Tax RulesOctober 29, 2026

A GST bill has two numbers on it, and most first-time business owners read only the bigger one. The tax you pay on what you buy is not always a cost — if you are registered, you usually get it back as input tax credit.

Get that wrong and you overpay on every purchase. Get it right and the tax you collect from customers largely cancels out the tax you already paid.

What input tax credit actually is

When you buy something for your business, the seller charges you GST. That tax is called input tax. When you sell, you charge your customer GST. That is output tax.

Input tax credit, or ITC, lets you subtract the input tax from the output tax and pay only the difference to the government. You are not refunded the input tax in cash; you simply do not pay that tax twice.

  • Registered business: the input tax is a credit you set against what you owe.
  • Unregistered business: the input tax is a cost you carry, and it eats your margin.

A worked example: the ₹5,900 bill

Say a shop buys goods for ₹5,000 before tax. At 18% GST the tax is ₹900, so the bill comes to ₹5,900.

The shop sells those goods for ₹8,000 before tax. At 18% GST the output tax is ₹1,440, so the customer pays ₹9,440.

  1. Output tax: ₹1,440.
  2. Input tax: ₹900.
  3. GST payable to the government: ₹1,440 − ₹900 = ₹540.

The shop does not send ₹1,440. It sends ₹540, because the ₹900 it already paid is set off. That ₹900 is the input tax credit, and it stayed with the shop instead of turning into a cost.

The same sale, with and without registration

Take that same ₹8,000 sale and compare two shops.

Unregistered shop: it pays the full ₹5,900 for the goods, because it cannot claim the ₹900. It sells for ₹8,000. Profit: ₹8,000 − ₹5,900 = ₹2,100.

Registered shop: it pays ₹5,900, claims ₹900, so its effective cost is ₹5,000. It sells for ₹8,000 plus ₹1,440 GST, sends ₹540 to the government, and keeps ₹8,000. Profit: ₹8,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹3,000.

Same goods, same selling price, and the registered shop is ₹900 ahead. That ₹900 is exactly the input tax credit.

Why the 180-day rule can take the credit back

ITC is not free money you can claim on any invoice you receive. You have to actually pay your supplier.

If you do not pay the supplier within 180 days of the invoice date, you must add the credit back and pay it to the government. On the ₹900 credit above, that is ₹900 you suddenly owe.

This is how a business that delays payments to hold on to cash gets tripped up by its own GST return. The credit and the payment are linked.

Credit you cannot claim

Some purchases are blocked, which means the input tax on them is a permanent cost. Blocked credit includes:

  • Food and beverages, outdoor catering, and club memberships.
  • Beauty treatment, health services and cosmetic surgery, unless that is the business itself.
  • Cars and other vehicles for passenger transport, unless the business is in that trade.

Say you take a client to lunch. The bill is ₹2,000 plus 5% GST of ₹100, so you pay ₹2,100. Because restaurant credit is blocked, that ₹100 never comes back. Your real cost is ₹2,100, not ₹2,000.

What ITC means for your cash

ITC changes the price you are willing to pay. A registered buyer who can claim the tax sees a ₹5,900 bill as ₹5,000 of goods and ₹900 of tax that will come back. An unregistered buyer sees ₹5,900 of cost.

That is why two shops can quote the same selling price and still earn very different profits — the registered one keeps the input tax, and the unregistered one pays it.

The short version

Input tax credit lets a registered business set the GST it paid on purchases against the GST it collected on sales. On a ₹5,000 purchase at 18%, the ₹900 credit turns a ₹1,440 GST bill into ₹540 payable, and it makes a registered shop ₹900 richer on the same ₹8,000 sale than an unregistered one. Claim it only on purchases you have paid for within 180 days, and never on blocked items like a restaurant bill.

GST ke bil par do sankhyaaen hoti hain, aur zyadatar nae karobari sirph bade wali padhate hain. Jo cheez aap khareedate hain us par laga tax hamesha lagat nahi hota — agar aap rajistard hain, to aam taur par woh input tax credit ke roop mein vaapas mil jaata hai.

Ise galat samjhein to har khareed par zyada paisa denge. Sahe samjhein to graahak se vasoola gaya tax aapke pehle diye tax ko lagabhag kaat deta hai.

Input tax credit asal mein kya hai

Jab aap karobar ke liye kuch khareedate hain, to vikreta aapase GST leta hai. Us tax ko input tax kahte hain. Jab aap bechte hain, to aap graahak se GST lete hain. Use output tax kahte hain.

Input tax credit, yani ITC, aapko output tax mein se input tax ghataane deta hai aur sarakaar ko sirph antar dena padta hai. Input tax nakad vaapas nahi hota; aap bas woh tax dobaara nahi dete.

  • Rajistard karobar: input tax ek credit hai jise aap apne dey par kaat lete hain.
  • Arajistard karobar: input tax ek lagat hai jo aap uthaate hain, aur yeh aapka margin kha jaata hai.

Ek udaharan: ₹5,900 ka bil

Maan lijiye ek dukaan ₹5,000 ka maal khareedate hai, tax se pehle. 18% GST par tax ₹900 hota hai, to bil ₹5,900 ka banata hai.

Dukaan wahi maal ₹8,000 mein bechti hai, tax se pehle. 18% GST par output tax ₹1,440 hota hai, to graahak ₹9,440 deta hai.

  1. Output tax: ₹1,440.
  2. Input tax: ₹900.
  3. Sarakaar ko dey GST: ₹1,440 − ₹900 = ₹540.

Dukaan ₹1,440 nahi bhejate. Woh ₹540 bhejate hai, kyunki jo ₹900 usane pehle diya tha woh kat gaya. Woh ₹900 hi input tax credit hai, aur woh lagat banane ke bajaay dukaan ke paas hi raha.

Wahi bikri, rajistreshan ke saath aur bina

Usi ₹8,000 ki bikri par do dukaanon ki tulna karein.

Arajistard dukaan: woh maal ke liye poore ₹5,900 dete hai, kyunki woh ₹900 ka daava nahi kar sakate. Woh ₹8,000 mein bechti hai. Munafa: ₹8,000 − ₹5,900 = ₹2,100.

Rajistard dukaan: woh ₹5,900 dete hai, ₹900 ka daava karti hai, to uski asli lagat ₹5,000 rah jaati hai. Woh ₹8,000 mein bechti hai, aur ₹1,440 GST vasoolate hai, ₹540 sarakaar ko bhejate hai, aur ₹8,000 apne paas rakhate hai. Munafa: ₹8,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹3,000.

Wahi maal, wahi bikri keemat, aur rajistard dukaan ₹900 aage hai. Woh ₹900 theek input tax credit hai.

180 din ka niyam credit vaapas kyon le sakta hai

ITC koi muft paisa nahi hai jise aap kisi bhi bil par maang len. Aapko vikreta ko asal mein bhugataan karna padta hai.

Agar aap bil ki taareekh se 180 din ke bheetar vikreta ko bhugataan nahi karte, to aapko credit vaapas jodkar sarakaar ko dena padta hai. Upar ke ₹900 credit par yeh ₹900 achaanak bakaaya ban jaata hai.

Isi tarah jo karobar nakade rokane ke liye bhugataan taalata hai, woh apni hi GST ritarn mein ulajh jaata hai. Credit aur bhugataan ek-doosare se bandhe hain.

Jo credit nahi maanga ja sakta

Kuch khareed par credit blok hota hai, yani un par laga input tax hamesha ke liye lagat ban jaata hai. Blocked credit mein ye shaamil hain:

  • Khaana-peena, baahar ka kaitaring, aur klab ki sadasyata.
  • Byoote treetament, svaasthy sevaaen aur kosmetik sarjare, agar yahi karobar na ho.
  • Kaar aur savaare dhone wale vaahan, agar karobar usi vyaapaar mein na ho.

Maan lijiye aap graahak ko lanch par le jaate hain. Bil ₹2,000 ka hai aur 5% GST ke ₹100, to aap ₹2,100 dete hain. Restorent ka credit blok hai, to woh ₹100 kabhi vaapas nahi aata. Aapki asli lagat ₹2,100 hai, ₹2,000 nahi.

ITC aapki nakade ke liye kya matlab rakhta hai

ITC tay karta hai ki aap kaun see keemat dena chaahenge. Rajistard khareedaar jo tax ka daava kar sakta hai, ₹5,900 ke bil ko ₹5,000 ke maal aur ₹900 ke tax ke roop mein dekhta hai jo vaapas aaega. Arajistard khareedaar use ₹5,900 ki lagat dekhta hai.

Iseelie do dukaanen ek hi bikri keemat rakhakar bhi bahut alag munafa kamaate hain — rajistard wali input tax rakh lete hai, aur arajistard wali woh de dete hai.

Chhoti baat

Input tax credit ek rajistard karobar ko khareed par diya GST bikri par vasoole GST se kaatane deta hai. ₹5,000 ki 18% khareed par ₹900 ka credit ₹1,440 ke GST bil ko ₹540 dey bana deta hai, aur ₹8,000 ki usi bikri par rajistard dukaan ko arajistard se ₹900 aage kar deta hai. Isaka daava sirph unheen khareed par karein jinaka bhugataan 180 din mein kar diya ho, aur restorent ke bil jaise blok cheezon par kabhi nahi.
