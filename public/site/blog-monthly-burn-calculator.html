Frequently asked questions

What is monthly burn in a startup?

It is the cash a company consumes each month. Gross burn is total expenses and net burn is expenses minus revenue collected, which is the amount that actually leaves your bank account.

How do I calculate net burn?

Subtract monthly revenue from monthly expenses. With ₹12 lakh of expenses and ₹7 lakh of revenue, the net burn is ₹5 lakh a month.

What is a healthy burn rate in India?

It depends on your cash. A useful rule is to keep at least 12 months of runway, so with ₹50 lakh of cash a net burn above ₹4 lakh a month is a warning sign.

In Shark Tank India, when a founder asks for a large cheque, sharks such as Aman Gupta and Anupam Mittal often ask how much the company spends every month and how long the money will last. A brand that spends ₹12 lakh a month but earns ₹7 lakh has a net burn of ₹5 lakh, and with ₹50 lakh in the bank that is 10 months of life. Skippi Ice Pops came to the tank with zero external capital, funded entirely by its own sales, which is the opposite end of the same equation. What is monthly burn calculator?

A monthly burn calculator tells a founder how much cash the business consumes every month and how many months remain before the money runs out. There are two ways to read burn. Gross burn is the total of everything you spend, such as salaries, rent, marketing and software. Net burn is that total minus the revenue you collected in the same month. Net burn is the number that actually empties your bank account.

The difference between the two is not academic. A company with ₹15 lakh of monthly expenses and ₹12 lakh of revenue has a gross burn of ₹15 lakh but a net burn of only ₹3 lakh, so it lasts five times longer than a founder reading only the expense line would fear. Conversely, a company with high gross burn and weak collections can be close to zero cash even while its profit and loss statement looks respectable.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Indian founders often measure burn from the bank statement alone, which hides the money already committed to salaries, GST and vendor payments that will leave next month. A US template also assumes a single clean payroll, whereas Indian teams frequently mix full-time staff, interns and contractors, each with a different payment cycle.

Angel and seed investors in India watch net burn closely because it sets the pace of the next raise. A company burning ₹5 lakh a month with ₹50 lakh in the bank has 10 months and a comfortable window to raise, while one burning ₹14 lakh has under four months and will be negotiating from desperation.

Seasonality and working capital complicate the Indian picture. A festive quarter can lift revenue and cut net burn, while a slow quarter with delayed receivables can widen it. Tracking burn month by month, rather than averaging the year, is how an Indian founder keeps the runway number honest.

Common Mistakes

  • Reading only the expense line and calling it burn. If revenue covers part of the cost, your real net burn is smaller and your runway is longer.
  • Ignoring committed costs such as annual subscriptions, insurance and quarterly GST, which land in one month and distort a single month's burn.
  • Averaging burn across the year. A festive spike can hide a slow quarter, so track net burn month by month to see the true trend.
