Frequently asked questions

What is a good revenue multiple in India?

It depends on the sector. SaaS and high-growth software often get 6x to 12x revenue, branded consumer goods 2x to 4x, and low-margin services 1x or less.

How do you calculate valuation from revenue?

Multiply annual revenue by the revenue multiple. ₹4 crore of revenue at 5x is a ₹20 crore valuation, and the same revenue at 10x is ₹40 crore.

Is revenue multiple better than profit multiple?

For a fast-growing company with little profit, revenue multiple is the only workable measure. Once EBITDA is stable, investors switch to an EBITDA multiple.

On Shark Tank India, when a founder asks ₹2 crore for 10% equity, the deal quietly claims a ₹20 crore valuation. If that company earns ₹4 crore of yearly revenue, the cheque is pricing it at a 5x revenue multiple. The sharks rarely say the multiple out loud, but every deal on the show carries one, and it is the fastest way to compare a software brand against a snack brand. This calculator turns your annual revenue and a multiple into a valuation in ₹ Crores, so you can check any deal in seconds.

What is revenue multiple calculator india?

A revenue multiple calculator India founders actually use takes two things, your yearly revenue and a multiple, and returns what the business is worth. The multiple is simply how many times one year of sales an investor is willing to pay. A 3x multiple on ₹5 crore of revenue gives a ₹15 crore valuation, while a 10x multiple on the same revenue gives ₹50 crore. The revenue figure comes from your profit and loss statement, not from a projection, because buyers pay for what the company already earns.

The multiple itself is not random. It is set by the market, and in India it swings widely by sector. A high-growth software or SaaS business can command 6x to 12x revenue, a branded consumer goods company often sits near 2x to 4x, and a services firm with thin margins may get 1x or less. Growth rate, gross margin and how repeatable the revenue is all push the multiple up or down, so two companies with identical sales can be valued very differently.

This tool also shows the valuation at fixed 5x and 10x, so you can see the band your business might fall into. That band is useful in a negotiation, because it tells you whether the number on the table is at the bottom, the middle or the top of what your sector can support. Everything is shown in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores, the units Indian founders, angels and chartered accountants use every day, with no dollar conversion anywhere on the page.

Choosing the multiple is the real judgement call. Start with your sector's range, then adjust for growth, margins and how predictable the revenue is. A company growing 80% a year with high repeat sales deserves the top of the range, while a one-time project business belongs near the bottom. Write down the multiple you used and why, because an investor will ask.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

A US template prices a company in millions of dollars and calls a 10x multiple normal for anything with a subscription. India is more grounded. A profitable Indian business with real margins is still valued conservatively, and founders who copy a Silicon Valley multiple into an Indian pitch usually get asked one blunt question, show me the profit.

Indian angel and venture deals are mostly issued as CCPS, and a valuation often has to be certified by a chartered accountant or a SEBI-registered merchant banker. That means the multiple you quote has to survive a paper trail, not just a slide. Indian angels are also comfortable taking 10% to 20% for a seed cheque, which is a different starting point from a US round.

Valuation in India has to be defensible on paper. A chartered accountant, or for larger rounds a SEBI-registered merchant banker, may certify the price, so the multiple you quote should follow from your own numbers rather than a wish. That discipline is exactly why a revenue multiple calculator, and not a feeling, should set your ask.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up revenue and profit. A revenue multiple is applied to sales, an EBITDA multiple to profit, and the two numbers are not interchangeable.
  • Using a projected revenue number instead of last year's actual sales. Buyers pay for earned revenue, not for a forecast.
  • Copying a US SaaS multiple onto an Indian services business. The sector sets the multiple, not the ambition.
