Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate a Shark Tank India valuation?

Use Valuation = (Investment ÷ Equity) × 100. If someone gives ₹1 crore for 10% equity, the valuation is ₹10 crore. Skippi Ice Pops gave 15% for ₹1 crore, so its valuation was ₹6.67 crore.

What is the difference between pre-money and post-money valuation?

Post-money is the value after the investment. Pre-money is post-money minus the investment. For ₹1 crore at 10%, post-money is ₹10 crore and pre-money is ₹9 crore.

What is Shark Tank India valuation calculator in crores?

A Shark Tank India valuation calculator turns two numbers from a pitch — the amount invested and the equity given for it — into the valuation of the whole company. Investors call the answer the post-money valuation: the value of the business after the new money is in. The maths is a single division, but the number it produces is the one founders and sharks argue about for the whole episode.

The tool also shows the pre-money valuation (the value before the investment) and the stake the founder keeps. On Tank Metrics everything is shown in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores, because that is how Indian founders, investors and chartered accountants actually talk. There is no dollar conversion and no US template in the way.

One more thing this calculator settles is the gap between what a founder asks for and what the sharks finally agree to. Most pitches open with a valuation claim and then settle lower once the equity is negotiated. Seeing both numbers side by side is how you learn to price a deal before you are in the room.

The formulaValuation = (Investment ÷ Equity) × 100

Investment in ₹ crore, equity as a percentage. The result is the post-money valuation.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

American valuation tools count in millions and billions; India counts in lakh and crore, and one crore is ten million. The same value reads as a very different number depending on the unit, which is exactly where founders get confused when they copy a US calculator.

Indian equity deals are usually issued as CCPS (Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares), and a valuation often has to be certified by a chartered accountant or a SEBI-registered merchant banker. That is a different system from the US 409A valuation, so an Indian founder needs a calculator that thinks in ₹ and in Indian deal terms.

Indian founders also raise from family and friends before any angel, and those early rounds are rarely written down with a formal valuation. When a later angel asks for equity, this calculator is how you turn an informal round into a real number you can defend in front of a chartered accountant.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the pre-money valuation as the post-money valuation. The investment is added on top, so a ₹9 crore pre-money with ₹1 crore invested becomes ₹10 crore post-money.
  • Quoting a valuation without also quoting the equity percentage. The two only make sense together.
  • Assuming the sharks' first offer is final. Most deals close lower on equity than the opening ask.
