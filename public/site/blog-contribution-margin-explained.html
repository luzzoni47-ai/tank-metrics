Contribution Margin: What Each Sale Adds

What every sale leaves after its variable cost.

Profit & MarginsNovember 18, 2026

Every sale you make has to do two jobs: cover the cost of making or buying the thing, and leave something behind to pay the rent, the salaries and the electricity. The money left after the first job is your contribution margin. It is the most honest number in a small business, because it sets aside the fixed costs you cannot change and shows what each sale actually contributes.

This guide shows the formula, runs a full example on a ₹500 product, and explains why the percentage matters more than the rupees.

What contribution margin actually measures

Contribution margin is the selling price of one unit minus its variable cost. A variable cost is one that only exists because you sold that unit: the material, the purchase price, the packing, the platform commission, the delivery. Rent, salaries and the internet bill are not variable — you pay them whether you sell one unit or a hundred.

So contribution margin answers one question: of every rupee a customer pays, how many paise are left to fight the fixed costs and then turn into profit?

The formula, in one line

  • Contribution margin = Selling price − Variable cost per unit
  • CM ratio = Contribution margin ÷ Selling price × 100

Two rules keep it honest. Count every cost that changes with a sale, because a forgotten packing charge quietly inflates the margin. And use the price you really get, after any discount, not the list price.

A worked example: ₹500 a unit

Say you sell a product at ₹500. The variable cost — purchase, packing and the courier — is ₹300 a unit.

  • Selling price: ₹500
  • Variable cost per unit: ₹300
  • Contribution margin: ₹500 − ₹300 = ₹200
  • CM ratio: ₹200 ÷ ₹500 = 40%

Every sale leaves ₹200 towards the fixed costs. That is the number the rest of the business is built on.

Why the percentage matters more than the rupees

Two products can both add ₹200 a sale, yet be very different businesses.

  • Product A sells at ₹500 with a ₹200 contribution — a 40% ratio.
  • Product B sells at ₹400 with the same ₹200 contribution — a 50% ratio.

Same rupees, different effort. Product B keeps half of every rupee it takes, so it needs less cash to run and survives a slow month better. When you compare products, or decide what to push, the ratio is the fair comparison; the flat ₹200 hides how much each sale had to bring in to earn it.

Contribution margin is not gross margin

The two are close cousins, and the difference is one line of costs. Gross margin takes only the direct cost of the goods — the purchase price or the material. Contribution margin also subtracts the variable costs that ride along with a sale: the commission, the packing, the delivery, the card fee.

For an online seller those extras are real, so the contribution margin is usually the lower, truer figure. If the two numbers are far apart, your selling costs are eating a slice you have not been counting.

What it tells you about break-even

Once you know the contribution per unit, break-even is one division away. Suppose your fixed costs are ₹6,000 a month and each sale contributes ₹200.

  • Break-even sales = ₹6,000 ÷ ₹200 = 30 units a month
  • At 40 units: contribution is ₹8,000, minus ₹6,000 fixed = ₹2,000 profit

The first 30 units only get you to zero. Every unit after that adds its full ₹200 to profit, because the fixed costs are already paid. That is why the contribution margin is the lever on both break-even and profit.

The short version

Contribution margin is selling price minus variable cost. A ₹500 product with a ₹300 variable cost contributes ₹200, a 40% ratio. Divide your fixed costs by that ₹200 to find the units that cover them, then watch the ratio, not just the rupees, when you decide what to sell more of.

Kantreebyooshan margin asal mein kya naapata hai

Kantreebyooshan margin ek yoonit ki bikri keemat mein se uski veriebal lagat ghataakar nikalta hai. Veriebal lagat woh hai jo sirf isliye paida hoti hai ki aapne woh yoonit beche: kachcha maal, khareed mooly, paiking, pletaform commission, dileevare. Kiraya, salary aur intaranet ka bil veriebal nahi hain — aap inhein ek yoonit bechen ya sau, chukaana padta hai.

To kantreebyooshan margin ek sawal ka jawab deta hai: graahak jo bhi rupaya deta hai, usamen se kitne paise tay laagaton se ladane ke baad munafa banane ke liye bachate hain?

Formula, ek laain mein

  • Kantreebyooshan margin = bikri keemat − prati yoonit veriebal lagat
  • Seem anupaat = kantreebyooshan margin ÷ bikri keemat × 100

Do niyam ise imandar rakhte hain. Har woh lagat ginen jo bikri ke saath badalate hai, kyunki bhoole huee paiking fees chupchaap margin badha dete hai. Aur woh keemat len jo vaakaee milate hai, chhoot ke baad, soocheebaddh keemat nahi.

Ek udaharan: ₹500 prati yoonit

Maan lijiye aap ek product ₹500 mein bechte hain. Veriebal lagat — khareed, paiking aur kooriyar — ₹300 prati yoonit hai.

  • Bikri keemat: ₹500
  • Prati yoonit veriebal lagat: ₹300
  • Kantreebyooshan margin: ₹500 − ₹300 = ₹200
  • Seem anupaat: ₹200 ÷ ₹500 = 40%

Har bikri tay laagaton ki taraf ₹200 chhodti hai. Aage ka pura hisaab isi par banata hai.

Pratishat rupayon se zyada kyon maayne rakhta hai

Do product ek hi ₹200 prati bikri jod sakte hain, phir bhi bahut alag karobar ho sakte hain.

  • Product A ₹500 mein bikta hai aur ₹200 jodata hai — 40% anupaat.
  • Product B ₹400 mein bikta hai aur wahi ₹200 jodata hai — 50% anupaat.

Rupaye ek, mehnat alag. Product B har rupaye ka aadha rakhta hai, isliye use chalaane ke liye kam nakad chahiye aur woh sust mahine ko behtar jhelata hai. Product ki tulna karte samay, ya tay karte samay ki kise aage badhaana hai, anupaat hi sahe paimaana hai; sirf ₹200 yeh chhipa deta hai ki use kamaane ke liye kitna bechna pada.

Kantreebyooshan margin, gros margin nahi hai

Dono kareebe rishtedaar hain, aur farq lagat ki ek laain ka hai. Gros margin sirf maal ki seedhe lagat leta hai — khareed mooly ya kachcha maal. Kantreebyooshan margin un veriebal laagaton ko bhi ghataata hai jo bikri ke saath chalti hain: commission, paiking, dileevare, kaard fees.

Online vikreta ke liye ye atirikt kharch asli hote hain, isliye kantreebyooshan margin aam taur par kam aur sachcha aankda hota hai. Agar dono aankde bahut door hon, to aapki bikri laagaten ek aisa hissa kha rahi hain jise aap gin nahi rahe.

Yeh brek-eevan ke baare mein kya batata hai

Prati yoonit kantreebyooshan pata ho, to brek-eevan ek bhaag door hai. Maan lijiye aapki tay lagat ₹6,000 mahine ki hai aur har bikri ₹200 jodate hai.

  • Brek-eevan bikri = ₹6,000 ÷ ₹200 = 30 yoonit mahine
  • 40 yoonit par: kantreebyooshan ₹8,000, ghatayein ₹6,000 tay = ₹2,000 munafa

Pehli 30 yoonit sirf shoony tak laate hain. Usake baad har yoonit apna pura ₹200 munafe mein jodate hai, kyunki tay laagaten chuk chuke hain. Iseelie kantreebyooshan margin brek-eevan aur munafe dono ka leevar hai.

Chhoti baat

Kantreebyooshan margin bikri keemat ghata veriebal lagat hai. ₹500 ka product, jisake veriebal lagat ₹300 hai, ₹200 jodata hai, yani 40% anupaat. Apni tay laagaton ko us ₹200 se bhaag dein, jitane yoonit chahiye woh mil jaaege; aur tay karte samay ki kya zyada bechna hai, anupaat dekhein, sirf rupaye nahi.
