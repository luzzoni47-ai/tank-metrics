Frequently asked questions

How do I check a Shark Tank India deal?

Enter the amount and the equity from the deal. The calculator returns the post-money valuation, the pre-money valuation and the founder's remaining stake.

What is pre-money vs post-money valuation?

Post-money is the value after the investment is added, and pre-money is post-money minus the investment. For ₹1 crore at 10%, post-money is ₹10 crore and pre-money is ₹9 crore.

Does this calculator cover debt and royalty deals?

It handles the equity part of any deal. For equity plus debt structures, add the debt separately, since the debt is repaid and is not part of the equity valuation.

What is shark tank india deal calculator?

A Shark Tank India deal calculator checks any pitch in one step. You enter two things, the amount invested and the equity handed over, and it returns the post-money valuation, which is the value of the company after the cheque. The maths is one division, but it is the number that decides whether a deal is generous or expensive, and it is almost never stated on screen.

The same calculator also shows the pre-money valuation, which is the post-money value minus the cheque, and the founder's remaining stake. Pre-money is what the company was worth walking in, and post-money is what it is worth walking out. Confusing the two is the single most common mistake in a pitch, and seeing both side by side makes the difference obvious.

Because every figure is in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores, you can line up a snack brand against a software company and compare the real value on offer. The tool works for any deal, past or present, on the show or off it. It is also a quick way to replay an old episode: pause at the offer, type the two numbers, and you know the valuation the founders and sharks were really negotiating over, without waiting for a caption that never comes.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Shark Tank India deals are not simple equity purchases. Many are structured as a mix of equity and debt, and some come with royalty until the investment is repaid, which a plain percentage cannot capture. Bamboo India's ₹50 lakh for 3.5% plus ₹30 lakh of debt is a good example of a deal that is part equity and part loan.

Indian rounds also carry terms beyond the headline stake, such as a board seat, veto rights, a liquidation preference, and an ESOP pool that is usually carved out of the founder's side. So the percentage a founder gives away is only the start of the real dilution.

Valuation also has to be defensible on paper in India. A chartered accountant, or for larger rounds a SEBI-registered merchant banker, may certify the price, so the multiple behind a deal has to follow from the company's own numbers rather than from a slide.

Finally, remember that the show is entertainment as well as business. A dramatic valuation makes good television, so some asks are deliberately high and some offers deliberately low. Checking the real numbers with a calculator keeps you grounded, and it turns every episode into a free lesson in how Indian deals are actually priced.

Common Mistakes

  • Reading the pre-money figure as the post-money valuation. The cheque is added on top, so the two differ by the investment amount.
  • Ignoring the debt or royalty inside a deal. A mixed structure is not the same as a pure equity round.
  • Assuming the founder keeps exactly the remaining percentage. The ESOP pool and any secondary sale reduce it further.
