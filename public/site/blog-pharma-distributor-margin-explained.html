Pharma Distributor Margin: Who Earns What

The MRP on a strip is fixed, so every margin has to fit inside it. Here is how the split works, with numbers you can copy.

Pricing & CostsOctober 27, 2026

If you are a stockist, a sub-stockist or a chemist, the number that decides your income is not the MRP printed on the strip. It is the margin you are allowed to keep, and in pharma that margin is almost always quoted as a percentage of MRP rather than of what you paid for the goods.

This guide shows how a strip's MRP splits between the manufacturer, the distributor and the retailer, works through a small example, and explains why the same ₹25 can be called two different margins.

The pharma distribution chain, in one line

Goods move manufacturer → distributor (stockist) → retailer (chemist) → patient. Each step adds its own margin to the price it passes on, and the last price is the MRP. Because the MRP is fixed and printed on the pack, the three margins have to fit inside it.

A worked example: where ₹100 of MRP goes

Take a strip with an MRP of ₹100. In the usual Indian pharma trade terms, the retailer keeps 20% of MRP and the distributor keeps 10% of MRP. Whatever is left is the manufacturer's price.

  1. Retailer margin: 20% of ₹100 = ₹20. The chemist buys the strip at ₹80.
  2. Distributor margin: 10% of ₹100 = ₹10. The stockist buys the strip at ₹70.
  3. Manufacturer realises ₹70 a strip.
  4. Check: ₹70 + ₹10 + ₹20 = ₹100, the full MRP.

The two margins take ₹30 out of a ₹100 strip, so the manufacturer keeps ₹70. Nothing is missing and nothing is counted twice — the split has to total the MRP exactly.

Now scale it to a month. If your shop moves 100 strips of that product, the distributor margin is ₹10 × 100 = ₹1,000, and the retailer margin is ₹20 × 100 = ₹2,000. Same strip, same percentages, very different earnings — which is why volume matters as much as the rate.

The same ₹25 can be a 25% margin or a 20% margin

This is where most people trip. A distributor who buys a pack at ₹100 and sells it at ₹125 has made ₹25. As a share of what they paid, that is 25%. As a share of what they sold it for, it is 20%. Both answers are "the margin", and they differ only because the base differs.

Pharma trade margins are quoted on MRP, which is a selling price. So when a company offers you "10% margin", check whether it means 10% of MRP or 10% on your cost. On a ₹100 strip, 10% of MRP is ₹10, but 10% on a ₹70 cost is only ₹7 — a real difference on every strip you sell.

What quietly eats the margin

The headline percentage is only the start. Three things move it in practice.

Schemes and free goods. A "10+1" scheme gives you one strip free with every ten, which raises your effective margin — but only if you sell all eleven before they expire. Unsold free stock is not profit.

Expiry and returns. Pharma stock expires. A strip that comes back unsold has earned you nothing and has cost you shelf space, so build a small allowance for expiry into your pricing rather than treating the headline margin as final.

Credit terms. If you pay the manufacturer in 7 days but your chemist pays you in 45, you are funding that gap yourself. The margin can look fine on paper while the cash position is tight, so compare the rate on your own working capital with the margin you are earning before you call the deal good.

How to check your own numbers

Start with the MRP and work backwards: subtract the retailer's margin to get the price to retailer, then the distributor's margin to get the price to stockist. Whatever is left is the manufacturer's price. If your actual buy price is higher than that, your margin is being squeezed, and you want to see it before the month closes rather than after.

Keep the two margin bases separate in your own records as well. Write down whether each figure is a percentage of MRP or a percentage of cost, because mixing them is what turns a healthy-looking deal into a thin one.

Pharma distribution chain, ek pankti mein

Maal Manufacturer se Distributor (Stockist), phir Retailer (Chemist), phir patient tak jaata hai. Har charan apna margin jodkar aage ki keemat banata hai, aur aakhire keemat hi MRP hai. Choonki MRP tay hai aur paik par chhapa hai, teenon margin ko usi ke andar phit hona padta hai.

Ek udaharan: ₹100 ke MRP ka bantavaara

Maan lijiye ek patte ka MRP ₹100 hai. Bhaarateey Pharma vyaapaar ki aam sharton mein Retailer MRP ka 20% rakhta hai aur Distributor MRP ka 10% rakhta hai. Jo bachta hai woh Manufacturer ki keemat hai.

  1. Retailer margin: ₹100 ka 20% = ₹20. Chemist patta ₹80 mein khareedata hai.
  2. Distributor margin: ₹100 ka 10% = ₹10. Stockist patta ₹70 mein khareedata hai.
  3. Manufacturer ko har patte par ₹70 milta hai.
  4. Jaanch: ₹70 + ₹10 + ₹20 = ₹100, pura MRP.

Dono margin ₹100 ke patte mein se ₹30 le lete hain, isliye Manufacturer ke paas ₹70 bachta hai. Kuch bhi gaayab nahi hai aur kuch bhi do baar nahi gina gaya — bantavaara theek MRP tak judana chahiye.

Ab ise ek mahine tak badhaaie. Agar aapki dukaan us product ke 100 patte bechti hai, to Distributor margin ₹10 × 100 = ₹1,000 hai, aur Retailer margin ₹20 × 100 = ₹2,000 hai. Wahi patta, wahi pratishat, par kamai bahut alag — iseelie dar jitna hi volyoom bhi maayne rakhta hai.

Wahi ₹25 kabhi 25% margin, kabhi 20% margin

Yahin par zyadatar log phansate hain. Jo Distributor ek paik ₹100 mein khareedakar ₹125 mein bechata hai, usane ₹25 kamaye. Jo usane diya usake hisaab se yeh 25% hai. Jis par becha usake hisaab se yeh 20% hai. Dono jawab "margin" hain, aur farq sirf aadhaar ka hai.

Pharma vyaapaar margin MRP par bataye jaate hain, jo ek bikri keemat hai. Isliye jab koi company aapko "10% margin" ka offer de, to jaanch len ki isaka matlab MRP ka 10% hai ya aapki lagat par 10%. ₹100 ke patte par MRP ka 10% to ₹10 hai, par ₹70 ki lagat par 10% sirf ₹7 hai — har bikne wale patte par yeh asli farq hai.

Margin ko chupchaap kya khaata hai

Upar wala pratishat to sirf shuruaat hai. Asal mein teen cheezein ise hilaate hain.

Scheme aur free maal. "10+1" scheme har das par ek patta free dete hai, jo aapka asardar margin badhati hai — par sirf tab, jab aap gyaarahon patte expiry se pehle bech dein. Na bika free maal munafa nahi hai.

Expiry aur vaapasi. Pharma stok expire hota hai. Jo patta bina bike laut aaya, usane aapko kuch nahi diya aur shelf ki jagah kharch kar dee, isliye keemat tay karte samay expiry ke liye thode gunjaish rakhein, upar wale margin ko aakhire na maanein.

Credit ki shartein. Agar aap Manufacturer ko 7 din mein bhugataan karte hain par aapka Chemist aapko 45 din mein deta hai, to woh antar aap apni jeb se bhar rahe hain. Kaagaz par margin theek lag sakta hai jabaki nakad ki haalat tang ho, isliye saude ko achha kehne se pehle apni kaaryasheel poonji par lagane wali dar ki tulna apne margin se kar len.

Apne aankde kaise jaanchein

MRP se shuru karein aur ulta chalen: Retailer ka margin ghataakar Retailer ko dee jaane wali keemat nikalein, phir Distributor ka margin ghataakar Stockist ko dee jaane wali keemat. Jo bache woh Manufacturer ki keemat hai. Agar aapki asli khareed keemat usse zyada hai, to aapka margin dab raha hai, aur aap ise mahina band hone ke baad nahi, pehle dekhna chaahenge.

Apne rikord mein bhi dono margin ke aadhaar alag rakhein. Likh len ki har aankda MRP ka pratishat hai ya lagat ka, kyunki inhein milaane se hi ek achha dikhane wala sauda patla ban jaata hai.
