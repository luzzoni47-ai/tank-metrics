Google Buys 1.43% of Rigi at a ₹670 Crore Value

Google put ₹10 crore into Bengaluru's Rigi for a 1.43% stake, pricing the startup near ₹670 crore. The maths behind a valuation, and what it means for owners.

StartupsNovember 28, 2026

Google International has put ₹10 crore into Rigi, a Bengaluru startup that began by helping creators sell paid communities and now runs a micro-drama app called Rigi TV. The cheque is small by Google standards. The number that matters is the one behind it: after the share issue, Google holds 1.43% of the company, which prices Rigi at roughly ₹670 crore.

What Google actually bought

The deal was not a round with a lead investor and a press release full of growth numbers. It was a private allotment: 1,543 preference shares at ₹61,796 each. Multiply those two figures and you get about ₹9.5 crore, which rounds to the ₹10 crore that was reported. Divide ₹10 crore by 1.43% and you land near ₹700 crore. The reported post-money valuation of ₹670 crore sits in the same place, so the three numbers agree with each other.

Rigi was founded in 2021 by Swapnil Saurav and Ananya Singhal. It started as a creator monetisation business: paid communities on WhatsApp and Telegram, online courses, webinars, workshops and one-on-one mentorship, with the company taking platform fees on each paid interaction. It later moved into entertainment with Rigi TV, which shows short-form originals and micro-dramas and earns from advertising and in-app purchases. “With Google’s entry into our shareholder base, we’re excited to see the team continue expanding its presence in short-form entertainment,” Singhal said in a social media post.

A valuation is just a price for 1%

Once you see a valuation that way, the big numbers become easy to handle. At ₹670 crore, one per cent of Rigi costs about ₹6.7 crore. A tenth of a per cent costs about ₹67 lakh. Google paid for a slice just over 1%, and the slice is worth what the price says it is worth, no more.

Run the same arithmetic on a business you know. A shop valued at ₹2 crore selling a 10% stake raises ₹20 lakh, and the owner keeps 90%. A ₹5 crore business selling 20% raises ₹1 crore. The percentage, not the cheque size, decides how much of the company walks out of the door.

The stake also moves everyone else down a little. When a company issues new shares equal to 1.43% of itself, the people already holding shares own 1.43% less of the larger company. An owner who held 40% before the allotment holds about 39.4% after it. Small, but it is real, and it compounds with every future round.

What the ₹670 crore tag would need to be earned

We do not know Rigi’s revenue, so treat the next line as a what-if, not a fact. If a short-video business traded at ten times its yearly sales, a ₹670 crore valuation would imply about ₹67 crore of annual revenue. At twenty times sales it would imply about ₹33 crore. Investors in media businesses pay for attention and for the advertising revenue they believe that attention will produce, and the multiple is the argument about how much of that future they will pay for today.

What this means for a shop or a small brand

Three things are worth taking away. First, a valuation is arithmetic, not applause: money in divided by the percentage given. Anyone who quotes you a valuation without the percentage is telling you half the story. Second, small cheques from large names are usually priced for reasons beyond cash. Google gets a look at how short-form entertainment in India is being built, and Rigi gets a shareholder whose name opens doors. If a big customer or platform wants a small stake in your business, ask what else the deal brings, because the money alone rarely justifies the dilution.

Third, the advertising shift is the part that touches a local business. Micro-dramas hold attention in short bursts, and that attention is sold as ad space. If your customers are already spending evenings on short video, the budget you put into that format is competing with the hoarding outside your shop. Measure it the same way: rupees spent, customers reached, sales made. A ₹10,000 test spend that brings ₹40,000 of sales is a 4x return; one that brings ₹8,000 is a loss, no matter how modern the format looks.

Google International ne Bengaluru ki startup Rigi mein ₹10 crore lagaye hain. Rigi pehle creators ko paid community aur course bechne mein madad karti thi, aur ab Rigi TV naam ka micro-drama app chalati hai. Google ke paas ab company ka 1.43% hissa hai, jisse Rigi ki value takreeban ₹670 crore maani ja rahi hai.

Google ne asal mein kya kharida

Yeh koi bada funding round nahi tha. Yeh ek private allotment tha: 1,543 preference shares, har ek ₹61,796 ka. In dono numbers ko guna karo to takreeban ₹9.5 crore aata hai, jo ₹10 crore ke barabar hai. ₹10 crore ko 1.43% se divide karo to ₹700 crore ke aas paas pahunchte ho. Report ki hui ₹670 crore valuation isi range mein baithti hai, matlab teeno numbers ek doosre se match karte hain.

Rigi 2021 mein Swapnil Saurav aur Ananya Singhal ne shuru ki thi. Shuruaat creator monetisation se hui: WhatsApp aur Telegram par paid community, online course, webinar, workshop aur one-on-one mentorship, jahan company har paid interaction par platform fee leti thi. Baad mein company entertainment mein aayi aur Rigi TV launch kiya, jahan short original shows aur micro-dramas chalti hain aur kamai advertising aur in-app purchase se hoti hai. Singhal ne social media post mein kaha, “With Google’s entry into our shareholder base, we’re excited to see the team continue expanding its presence in short-form entertainment.”

Valuation matlab 1% ka price

Jab aap valuation ko is tarah dekhte ho to bade numbers aasan ho jaate hain. ₹670 crore par Rigi ka ek per cent takreeban ₹6.7 crore ka hai. 0.1 per cent takreeban ₹67 lakh ka. Google ne 1% se thoda zyada ka hissa kharida, aur us hisse ki value utni hi hai jitna price batata hai.

Yahi hisaab apne business par lagao. ₹2 crore value wali dukaan 10% hissa bech kar ₹20 lakh uthati hai, aur owner ke paas 90% rehta hai. ₹5 crore ka business 20% bech kar ₹1 crore uthati hai. Cheque ka size nahi, percentage decide karta hai ki company ka kitna hissa bahar jaata hai.

Naye shares nikalne se purane shareholders ka hissa thoda kam ho jaata hai. Agar company apne 1.43% ke barabar naye shares nikalti hai, to purane log badi company ka 1.43% kam hissa rakhte hain. Pehle 40% wale owner ke paas ab takreeban 39.4% rehta hai. Chhota farak hai, par asli hai, aur har round ke saath badhta jaata hai.

₹670 crore ki value kama kar dikhani padegi

Rigi ki revenue humein nahi pata, isliye agla line sirf ek example hai. Agar short-video business apni saalana sale ke 10 guna par trade kare, to ₹670 crore valuation ka matlab takreeban ₹67 crore saalana revenue hota hai. 20 guna par matlab takreeban ₹33 crore. Media business mein investor attention ke liye paisa deta hai, aur multiple yeh tay karta hai ki future ki kitni kamai aaj hi chuki ja rahi hai.

Dukaan ya chhote brand ke liye iska matlab

Teen baatein yaad rakho. Pehli, valuation maths hai, taali nahi: andar aaya paisa divided by diya gaya percentage. Jo aapko valuation bataye par percentage na bataye, wo aadhi kahani bata raha hai. Doosri, bade naam ka chhota cheque sirf cash ke liye nahi hota. Google ko India ka short-form entertainment kaise ban raha hai, yeh dekhne ko milta hai, aur Rigi ko aisa shareholder milta hai jiska naam darwaze kholta hai. Agar koi bada customer ya platform aapke business mein chhota hissa maange, to poocho ki paise ke alawa kya mil raha hai.

Teesri, advertising ka badlav sabse zyada local business ko chhoota hai. Micro-dramas logon ka dhyan chhote chhote hisson mein pakadte hain, aur wo dhyan ad space ban kar bikta hai. Agar aapke customer shaam ko short video dekh rahe hain, to us format ka budget aapki dukaan ke bahar lage hoarding se muqabla karta hai. Hisaab wahi rakho: kitna kharcha, kitne customer, kitni sale. ₹10,000 ka test spend ₹40,000 ki sale laata hai to 4x return hai; ₹8,000 ki sale laata hai to nuksan hai, chahe format kitna bhi naya lage.
