SEBI's SME IPO Plan: ₹3 Crore Profit Bar

SEBI wants SME IPOs judged by market value rather than paid-up capital, with a ₹3 crore average profit bar. Here is what the plan means for a founder.

FundingNovember 1, 2026

If you are building a company toward a stock-market listing, the rulebook you were planning around may be about to change. On 14 October 2026 the market regulator, SEBI, released a consultation paper proposing a broad overhaul of the rules for small and medium enterprise (SME) IPOs. Public comments are open until 4 November 2026. These are proposals, not yet law, but they point clearly at where the SME platform is heading.

Why SEBI is reviewing the SME IPO rules

The SME platform has grown far beyond its original size. As of 31 July 2026, 1,496 entities had listed on SME platforms since inception, including 365 that later migrated to the main board. SME IPOs have raised a cumulative ₹40,222 crore, and the market capitalisation of these entities stands at about ₹4.71 trillion (₹4,70,984 crore).

SEBI's argument is simple. When the platform was small, its rules were written for small companies. Today it holds issuers of very different size, maturity and valuation, so some SME-specific rules are no longer proportionate.

Eligibility would move from paid-up capital to market value

Today an SME listing is judged on post-issue paid-up capital, currently up to ₹10 crore and extendable to ₹25 crore. SEBI calls paid-up capital nominal capital: it often says little about how big a business really is. In 735 main board and SME IPOs it studied over three financial years, issuers with low paid-up capital often carried much higher market values.

The proposal replaces that test with post-issue market capitalisation, in three bands:

  • Up to ₹1,000 crore: the issuer must list on the SME platform.
  • Between ₹1,000 crore and ₹4,000 crore: the issuer may choose the SME platform or the main board. If it picks SME, it must present restated consolidated financials under Indian Accounting Standards for the last three years.
  • Above ₹4,000 crore: the issuer must use the main board framework.

The test is applied again when the red herring prospectus is filed, so a company that drifts out of its band may have to refile under the other framework.

The profit bar would rise from ₹1 crore to ₹3 crore

The current test asks for ₹1 crore of operating profit in at least two of the past three years. SEBI proposes replacing it with an average operating profit of at least ₹3 crore a year over the preceding three financial years, using restated consolidated statements.

Do the arithmetic on a simple case. A company that earned ₹1 crore, ₹2 crore and ₹6 crore across three years averages ₹3 crore — it clears the new bar. A company that earned ₹3 crore, ₹3 crore and ₹1 crore also averages about ₹2.33 crore — and misses it, even though two of its years were strong. That is the point of an average: it rewards steady profit over a single lucky year.

Issuers that miss the bar are not shut out. A new route would let them list if they meet track-record and promoter-continuity rules, use book-building, and give at least 75% of the net offer to qualified institutional buyers, with the entire subscription refunded if that fails. Retail would be capped at 10% and non-institutional investors at 15% on that route.

Smaller tickets, one-share lots

Several proposals are aimed at the investor's side of the table. The minimum application value would be halved to ₹1 lakh from ₹2 lakh. SEBI cut the working group's proposed ₹50,000 lot to ₹25,000, so an investor would apply for at least four lots. The minimum number of allottees would rise to 500 from 200, and the standardised trading lot of 100 to 10,000 shares would become a single share.

On cost, SEBI proposes dropping the rule that every SME IPO be fully underwritten, along with the requirement that lead managers underwrite 15% on their own account. Only the unsubscribed portion would need underwriting, as on the main board. Underwriting fees averaged about 49.5% and 38.4% of issue expenses for NSE SME IPOs in FY25 and FY26, and about 32.2% and 34% for BSE. Compulsory market making, which runs for three years with a 5% inventory, would also go; market makers accounted for only 0% to 10% of turnover in 361 NSE and 268 BSE SME scrips between August 2025 and July 2026.

What it means for a founder

Two signals matter most. First, size and profitability are being pulled up: a ₹3 crore average profit is a real business, not a promising one. Second, the decision of where to list is shifting from a technical capital figure to what the market thinks you are worth.

If a listing is on your roadmap, work backwards from valuation. Estimate the operating profit you can average over three years, multiply it by the multiple investors pay in your sector, and see whether the answer lands below ₹1,000 crore, between ₹1,000 and ₹4,000 crore, or above ₹4,000 crore. That number, not your paid-up capital, is the one the new framework would test.

Agar aap apni company ko stock market mein list karane ki taraf bana rahe hain, to jis rulebook ke hisaab se aap plan kar rahe the woh badal sakta hai. 14 October 2026 ko market regulator SEBI ne SME IPOs ke rules mein bade badlav ka consultation paper jaari kiya. Public comments 4 November 2026 tak khule hain. Yeh sirf proposals hain, kanoon nahi, lekin inse saaf pata chalta hai ki SME platform kahan ja raha hai.

SEBI SME IPO rules ki review kyun kar raha hai

SME platform apne shuruaati size se kaafi bada ho gaya hai. 31 July 2026 tak 1,496 entities SME platforms par list ho chuki thi, jinmein 365 baad mein main board par gayi. SME IPOs ne kul ₹40,222 crore jama kiye, aur in entities ka market capitalisation lagbhag ₹4.71 trillion (₹4,70,984 crore) hai.

SEBI ki daleel saaf hai. Jab platform chhota tha, uske rules chhoti companies ke liye likhe gaye the. Aaj usme alag-alag size, maturity aur valuation ke issuers hain, isliye kuch SME-specific rules ab theek nahi baithte.

Eligibility paid-up capital se market value ki taraf

Aaj SME listing post-issue paid-up capital par tay hoti hai, filhaal ₹10 crore tak aur ₹25 crore tak badhayi ja sakti hai. SEBI paid-up capital ko nominal capital kehta hai: isse pata nahi chalta ki business asli mein kitna bada hai. Teen financial years mein 735 main board aur SME IPOs ka study karke SEBI ne dekha ki kam paid-up capital wale issuers ki market value aksar kaafi zyada thi.

Yeh proposal us test ki jagah post-issue market capitalisation laata hai, teen bands mein:

  • ₹1,000 crore tak: issuer ko SME platform par hi list karna hoga.
  • ₹1,000 crore se ₹4,000 crore ke beech: issuer SME ya main board chun sakta hai. SME chune to usse teen saal ke restated consolidated financials Indian Accounting Standards ke hisaab se dene honge.
  • ₹4,000 crore se upar: issuer ko main board framework use karna hoga.

Yeh test red herring prospectus file hone par dobara lagta hai, isliye jo company apne band se bahar nikal jaaye use doosre framework mein dobara file karna pad sakta hai.

Profit ki shart ₹1 crore se ₹3 crore ki taraf

Aaj ka test teen saal mein kam se kam do saal ₹1 crore operating profit maangta hai. SEBI iski jagah pichhle teen financial years mein saalana kam se kam ₹3 crore ka average operating profit chahta hai, restated consolidated statements par.

Ek aasaan case par hisaab lagaiye. Jis company ne teen saal mein ₹1 crore, ₹2 crore aur ₹6 crore kamaye, uska average ₹3 crore hai — woh naya bar paar kar leti hai. Jisne ₹3 crore, ₹3 crore aur ₹1 crore kamaye, uska average lagbhag ₹2.33 crore hai — aur woh bar se chook jaati hai, chahe do saal mazboot the. Average ka yahi matlab hai: ek achhe saal ki jagah sthir munafe ko inaam dena.

Bar se chookne wale band nahi hote. Ek naya route unhe list karne dega agar woh track-record aur promoter-continuity ke rules pura karein, book-building karein, aur net offer ka kam se kam 75% qualified institutional buyers ko dein, aur woh na ho to pura subscription refund ho. Us route par retail 10% aur non-institutional investors 15% tak seemit honge.

Chhote ticket, ek-share lot

Kuch proposals investor ki taraf dekhte hain. Minimum application value ₹2 lakh se aadha hokar ₹1 lakh ho jayegi. SEBI ne working group ke proposed ₹50,000 lot ko ₹25,000 kar diya, isliye investor ko kam se kam chaar lots ke liye apply karna hoga. Minimum allottees 200 se badhkar 500 honge, aur 100 se 10,000 shares ka standardised trading lot ek share ho jayega.

Kharch par, SEBI har SME IPO ko pura underwritten karane ka rule hata raha hai, saath hi lead managers ke apne account par 15% underwrite karne ki shart bhi. Sirf bina subscribe bacha hissa underwritten karna hoga, jaise main board par. NSE SME IPOs mein FY25 aur FY26 mein underwriting fees issue expenses ka lagbhag 49.5% aur 38.4% the, aur BSE par lagbhag 32.2% aur 34%. Compulsory market making, jo teen saal 5% inventory ke saath chalti hai, woh bhi hategi; August 2025 se July 2026 ke beech 361 NSE aur 268 BSE SME scrips mein market makers ka turnover sirf 0% se 10% tha.

Founder ke liye iska matlab

Do signals sabse zyada maayne rakhte hain. Pehla, size aur profitability ki shart badh rahi hai: ₹3 crore average profit ek asli business hai, sirf ek umeed wala nahi. Doosra, list karne ki jagah ka faisla ek technical capital figure se hokar is taraf shift ho raha hai ki market aapko kitna moolya deta hai.

Agar listing aapke roadmap par hai, to valuation se peeche chaliye. Teen saal mein jitna average operating profit la sakte hain uska andaaza lagaiye, usse apne sector ke multiple se guna kijiye, aur dekhiye jawab ₹1,000 crore se neeche aata hai, ₹1,000 aur ₹4,000 crore ke beech, ya ₹4,000 crore se upar. Yahi number, aapka paid-up capital nahi, naye framework mein test hoga.
