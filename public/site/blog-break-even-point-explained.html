Break-Even Point: How Many Sales Before You Make Money?

The one number that tells you when your business stops losing money — with a tiffin kitchen example you can copy in a minute.

Growth & CashOctober 22, 2026

Every business has a magic number: the point where it stops losing money and starts making it. That is your break-even point, and it is the single most useful number a small business owner can know.

This guide gives you the formula, walks through a real example with a tiffin kitchen, and shows exactly what changes your break-even and by how much.

What is the break-even point?

The break-even point is the level of sales where your total revenue exactly equals your total costs. Sell one unit less and you make a loss. Sell one unit more and every extra rupee of contribution is profit.

It is not a target to celebrate. It is the floor you have to clear before anything you do pays you, because rent, salaries and your own time all come out of the gap above it.

The break-even formula

Break-even units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price − Variable Cost per Unit)

The part in brackets is the contribution per unit: what each sale leaves behind to pay for your fixed costs. Once you have the units, multiply by the selling price to get the break-even revenue.

  • Fixed costs — rent, salaries, software subscriptions. Anything that stays the same whether you sell 10 units or 1,000.
  • Variable cost per unit — raw material, packaging, delivery, payment gateway fees. Anything that rises with each sale.

A worked example: the tiffin kitchen

Say you run a tiffin service from home. Your monthly fixed costs are ₹3,000 — ₹2,000 rent for a small kitchen and ₹1,000 for the licence and app subscriptions.

You charge ₹40 per tiffin. Ingredients and packaging cost ₹20 per tiffin, so each tiffin contributes ₹20.

Break-even = ₹3,000 ÷ ₹20 = 150 tiffins a month.

In rupees that is 150 × ₹40 = ₹6,000 of revenue. Below ₹6,000 you are losing money. Above it, you are earning.

Contribution margin: the number behind break-even

Contribution per unit tells you how fast each sale closes the gap to break-even. Write it as a share of the selling price and you get the contribution margin: ₹20 ÷ ₹40 = 50%.

That means half of every rupee you collect goes towards fixed costs until they are covered, and into your pocket after that. A fat contribution margin brings break-even close. A thin one needs volume, and volume needs more staff, more stock and more cash.

What happens after break-even

Keep selling past 150 tiffins and the fixed costs are already paid, so each extra tiffin adds ₹20 of profit. Sell 200 tiffins in a month and the numbers look like this:

  • Revenue: 200 × ₹40 = ₹8,000
  • Variable cost: 200 × ₹20 = ₹4,000
  • Contribution: ₹8,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹4,000
  • Fixed costs: ₹3,000
  • Profit: ₹4,000 − ₹3,000 = ₹1,000

Look at the shape of that. The 50 tiffins above break-even produced ₹1,000 of profit, which is exactly 50 × ₹20. Above break-even, profit climbs in a straight line.

Break-even vs the numbers people confuse it with

  • Break-even — the sales level where profit is zero. A quantity, or the revenue that quantity produces.
  • Contribution margin — the share of each sale left after variable costs. A percentage, not a target.
  • Margin of safety — how far your actual sales sit above break-even. Sell 200 tiffins with a break-even of 150 and your cushion is 50 tiffins.

People often quote a break-even revenue without saying which month it covers. Always attach a period, because fixed costs are a monthly number.

Three ways to lower your break-even point

Break-even is a ratio, so anything that lifts contribution or cuts fixed costs pushes it down.

  1. Raise the price. Move from ₹40 to ₹60 with the variable cost still at ₹20 and contribution becomes ₹40, so break-even drops to ₹3,000 ÷ ₹40 = 75 tiffins.
  2. Cut the variable cost. Buy ingredients at ₹15 instead of ₹20 and contribution rises to ₹25, pulling break-even down to 120 tiffins.
  3. Cut the fixed costs. Drop the ₹1,000 of app subscriptions and fixed costs fall to ₹2,000, which brings break-even to ₹2,000 ÷ ₹20 = 100 tiffins.

Mistakes that make your break-even wrong

  1. Leaving your own salary out of fixed costs. If the business cannot pay you, it is not a business yet. Put your salary in, or the number lies to you.
  2. Treating a one-off cost as fixed. A machine or a deposit is not a monthly expense. Spread it through depreciation instead.
  3. Under-counting variable cost. Packaging, delivery and gateway fees are easy to forget, and forgetting them flatters the break-even badly.
  4. Forgetting when the customer pays. A sale that pays you 60 days later is not cash this month, however good the break-even looks.

The short version

The break-even point tells you how much you have to sell before your effort starts paying you. The formula is one line: fixed costs divided by contribution per unit.

Work it out every month. When your price, your costs or your rent change, this number changes too, and it is the number that decides whether the business is running or just looking busy.

Break-even point kya hai?

Break-even point bikri ka woh level hai jahaan aapka kul revenue aapke kul kharch ke baraabar ho jaata hai. Ek unit kam bechen to nuksan, ek unit zyada bechen to har extra rupaya munafa.

Ise jeet ka tamaga na samjhein. Yeh woh zameen hai jise pehle paar karna hai, kyunki kiraya, salary aur aapka apna samay sab isake upar wale hisse se nikalte hain.

Break-even ka formula

Break-even units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price − Variable Cost per Unit)

Koshthak wala hissa contribution per unit hai: har bikri se jo bachta hai aur aapke fixed costs chukaata hai. Units mil jayein to selling price se guna karein aur break-even revenue mil jaata hai.

  • Fixed costs — kiraya, salary, software subscription. Jo 10 unit bechen ya 1,000, baraabar rehte hain.
  • Variable cost per unit — kachcha maal, packaging, delivery, payment gateway fees. Jo har bikri ke saath badhte hain.

Ek udaharan: tiffin kitchen

Maan lijiye aap ghar se tiffin service chalate hain. Mahine ke fixed costs ₹3,000 hain — chhoti kitchen ka kiraya ₹2,000 aur licence tatha app subscription ₹1,000.

Aap har tiffin ke ₹40 lete hain. Saamaan aur packaging par ₹20 lagta hai, yani har tiffin se ₹20 bachta hai.

Break-even = ₹3,000 ÷ ₹20 = mahine ke 150 tiffin.

Rupayon mein yeh 150 × ₹40 = ₹6,000 revenue hai. ₹6,000 se neeche nuksan, upar munafa.

Contribution margin: break-even ke peechhe ka number

Contribution per unit batata hai ki har bikri break-even ki doore kitni jaldi poore karti hai. Ise selling price ke hisse ke roop mein likhen to contribution margin milta hai: ₹20 ÷ ₹40 = 50%.

Yani aapke har rupaye ka aadha hissa fixed costs poore hone tak unheen mein jaata hai, aur usake baad aapki jeb mein. Mota contribution margin break-even paas le aata hai. Patla margin ko volume chahiye, aur volume ko zyada staff, zyada stok aur zyada nakad.

Break-even ke baad kya hota hai

150 tiffin se upar bechate rahein to fixed costs pehle hi chuk chuke hain, isliye har extra tiffin ₹20 munafa jodata hai. Mahine mein 200 tiffin bechen to aankde aise dikhte hain:

  • Revenue: 200 × ₹40 = ₹8,000
  • Variable cost: 200 × ₹20 = ₹4,000
  • Contribution: ₹8,000 − ₹4,000 = ₹4,000
  • Fixed costs: ₹3,000
  • Munafa: ₹4,000 − ₹3,000 = ₹1,000

Isaka dhaancha dekhie. Break-even se upar ke 50 tiffin ne ₹1,000 munafa diya, yani theek 50 × ₹20. Break-even ke upar munafa seedhe lakeer mein badhta hai.

Break-even aur ve numbers jinase log ise mila dete hain

  • Break-even — bikri ka woh level jahaan munafa shoony hai. Ek quantity, ya us quantity se banane wala revenue.
  • Contribution margin — variable costs ke baad har bikri ka bacha hissa. Ek percentage, koi lakshya nahi.
  • Margin of safety — aapki asli bikri break-even se kitni upar hai. 150 ke break-even par 200 tiffin bechen to gunjaish 50 tiffin ki hai.

Log aksar break-even revenue batate hain par yeh nahi kahte ki woh kis mahine ka hai. Hamesha avadhi likhen, kyunki fixed costs mahine ka number hota hai.

Break-even ghataane ke teen tarike

Break-even ek anupaat hai, isliye jo cheez contribution badhaae ya fixed costs ghataye, woh ise neeche le aate hai.

  1. Keemat badhayein. ₹40 se ₹60 par jayein aur variable cost ₹20 hi rahe to contribution ₹40 banata hai, isliye break-even ₹3,000 ÷ ₹40 = 75 tiffin rah jaata hai.
  2. Variable cost ghatayein. Saamaan ₹20 ki jagah ₹15 mein len to contribution ₹25 ho jaata hai aur break-even 120 tiffin par aa jaata hai.
  3. Fixed costs ghatayein. ₹1,000 ke app subscription hataaen to fixed costs ₹2,000 rah jaate hain, aur break-even ₹2,000 ÷ ₹20 = 100 tiffin banata hai.

Ve galatiyaan jo break-even galat kar dete hain

  1. Apni salary fixed costs se baahar rakhana. Agar karobar aapko paisa nahi de sakta, to woh abhe karobar nahi hai. Salary jodein, varana number aapase jhooth bolega.
  2. Ek baar ke kharch ko fixed maan lena. Machine ya deposit mahine ka kharch nahi hai. Use depreciation se phailaaen.
  3. Variable cost kam ginana. Packaging, delivery aur gateway fees bhoolana aasan hai, aur bhoolane se break-even bahut achha dikhane lagta hai.
  4. Yeh bhool jaana ki graahak kab paisa dega. 60 din baad milne wali bikri is mahine nakad nahi hai, chaahe break-even kitna bhi badhiya lage.

Chhoti baat

Break-even point batata hai ki aapki mehnat paisa dene se pehle aapko kitna bechana hai. Formula ek laain ka hai: fixed costs bata contribution per unit.

Ise har mahine nikalein. Keemat, lagat ya kiraya badle to yeh number bhi badalta hai, aur yahi number tay karta hai ki karobar chal raha hai ya sirf vyast dikh raha hai.
