What this calculator is for

DSCR, the debt service coverage ratio, answers the one question a lender asks before it sanctions a term loan: does this business earn enough, from its normal operations, to pay the loan back on schedule? Net operating income is the profit before interest and tax; total debt service is every rupee of principal and interest due in the same year. Divide the first by the second and you have the number the credit officer writes on the file. A ratio of 1.25 or better is the usual comfort level, because it means operations cover the instalments with a quarter left over for the bad months. Below 1.0 the business is paying the bank out of reserves or fresh borrowing, which is the point at which a loan application stalls.

How the maths works

DSCR = Net Operating Income / Total Debt Service, both for the same year. A business earning 18,00,000 of operating income against 12,00,000 of principal and interest due has a DSCR of 1.5x, comfortably above the 1.25 most banks want. The same 18,00,000 against 20,00,000 of repayments gives 0.9x, and the loan is not being serviced from earnings at all. Run it across three years, not one: a single strong year flatters a business that is actually shrinking.

When to reach for it

  • A bank or NBFC has asked for a DSCR before it processes your term loan.
  • You are deciding whether the business can safely take on another EMI.
  • You are comparing two years and want to see whether the cover is thinning.

Common mistakes

  • Using net profit instead of net operating income. Interest and tax are added back because the loan is paid from profit before them.
  • Leaving a balloon or bullet repayment out of the debt service in the year it falls due. That is the year the ratio collapses.
  • Reading one good year as the trend. Lenders average the last three for exactly this reason.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

DSCR, yaani debt service coverage ratio, us ek sawaal ka jawab deta hai jo bank term loan sanction karne se pehle poochti hai: kya ye business apne normal operations se itna kama raha hai ki loan samay par chuka sake? Net operating income matlab interest aur tax se pehle ka profit; total debt service matlab us saal me dene wala poora principal aur interest. Pehle ko doosre se divide karo, aur wahi number milta hai jo credit officer file par likhta hai. 1.25 ya usse zyada aam taur par comfortable maana jaata hai, kyunki iska matlab hai ki operations instalments cover kar lete hain aur kharab mahinon ke liye thoda bach bhi jaata hai. 1.0 se neeche business bank ko reserves ya naye loan se pay kar raha hai, aur loan application wahin atak jaati hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

DSCR = Net Operating Income / Total Debt Service, dono usi saal ke. Ek business jo 18,00,000 operating income kamata hai aur jise 12,00,000 principal aur interest dena hai, uska DSCR 1.5x hai, jo zyadatar banks ke 1.25 se aaram se upar hai. Wahi 18,00,000 agar 20,00,000 ke repayment ke saamne ho to 0.9x banta hai, aur loan earnings se service hi nahi ho raha. Ise teen saal ka dekho, ek ka nahi: ek strong saal us business ko achha dikha deta hai jo asli me sikud raha hai.

Kab use karein

  • Jab bank ya NBFC ne term loan process karne se pehle DSCR maanga ho.
  • Jab aap decide kar rahe ho ki business ek aur EMI safely le sakta hai ya nahi.
  • Jab aap do saal compare kar rahe ho ye dekhne ke liye ki cover patla ho raha hai ya nahi.

Aam galtiyan

  • Net profit use karna, net operating income ke bajaye. Interest aur tax wapas jodte hain kyunki loan unse pehle ke profit se chukta hai.
  • Jis saal balloon ya bullet repayment due ho us saal usse debt service me chhod dena. Wahi saal ratio gir jaata hai.
  • Ek achhe saal ko trend maan lena. Banks isi wajah se pichhle teen saal ka average lete hain.
