What this calculator is for

A stamp duty calculator works out the money you pay the government on the day you register a property, and it is almost always more than a buyer has budgeted. Stamp duty is a state tax on the transaction and registration is the fee to record it in your name; together they add roughly 6% to 8% of the property value in most states, and in a few they cross 10%. On a ₹80 lakh flat that is ₹5 lakh to ₹8 lakh, paid in one go, and it cannot be added to the loan. This calculator shows the two charges and the total you have to carry to the sub-registrar's office.

How the maths works

Stamp Duty = Property Value × Stamp Duty Rate, and Registration = Property Value × Registration Rate. Total cost of buying = Property Value + Stamp Duty + Registration. A ₹80 lakh flat in a state charging 6% stamp duty and 1% registration costs ₹80 lakh + ₹4.8 lakh + ₹80,000, so ₹85.6 lakh on the day. Some states charge stamp duty on the higher of the agreement value and the circle rate, so the government's own floor price can push the bill up.

When to reach for it

  • You are about to register a property and want the exact cash you must arrange on that day.
  • You are comparing two states or two cities and the transaction cost changes your budget.
  • You are working out the true all-in cost of a purchase, not just the price the seller quoted.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting for the price and forgetting the duty, which on a ₹80 lakh flat is several lakh more.
  • Assuming stamp duty is the same everywhere. It is a state subject and varies by state, by buyer (women sometimes pay less) and by property type.
  • Forgetting that stamp duty cannot be financed in the home loan, so it has to be your own cash.

Ganit kaise chalta hai
