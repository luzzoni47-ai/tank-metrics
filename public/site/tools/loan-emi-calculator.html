How the Loan EMI Calculator works

Work out the fixed monthly payment for a loan, based on amount, rate and duration. Enter Loan Amount, Interest Rate and Loan Tenure above. Nothing you type for the Loan EMI Calculator is sent anywhere.

Good to know: A lower EMI usually means a longer loan — and more total interest paid.

Worked example

An ₹8,00,000 car loan at 9% over sixty months — the sort of quote a dealership prints on a single page and asks you to sign.

What you enter

  • Loan Amount: 800000 ₹
  • Interest Rate: 9 % per year
  • Loan Tenure: 60 months

What the Loan EMI Calculator returns

  • Monthly EMI: ₹16,606.68
  • Total Amount Paid: ₹9,96,401.05
  • Total Interest: ₹1,96,401.05

Sixty instalments of ₹16,607 add up to ₹9,96,401, of which ₹1,96,401 is interest. Stretch the same loan to eighty-four months and the EMI falls to ₹12,871 while the interest climbs to ₹2,81,186.

What the Loan EMI Calculator is for

This is the instalment-loan pricer for every borrowing that is not a house: the car or two-wheeler being financed at the dealership, a personal loan for a wedding or a hospital bill, gold and education loans, and the working-capital loan a shop takes for stock. You type the loan amount, the interest rate per year and the tenure in months, and the tool returns the fixed monthly EMI, the total paid by the last instalment, and the interest hidden inside that total. On ₹8,00,000 at 9% over 60 months the EMI is ₹16,607, the total is ₹9,96,401 and ₹1,96,401 of it is interest — about a quarter of the amount borrowed, paid for the convenience of spreading it out. The decision this page settles is whether an instalment plan is a cheap way to buy something or an expensive way to delay paying for it. Two quotes for the same car rarely arrive in the same language: one dealer offers 9% on the outstanding balance, another advertises a flat 10% over three years, and a card offers zero-cost EMI with a processing fee attached. This tool turns any of them into the same two numbers — the monthly outgo and the rupees of interest — so the offers can finally be compared. Tenure is the lever it makes visible: the same ₹8,00,000 at 9% costs ₹1,15,832 of interest over 36 months, ₹1,96,401 over 60 and ₹2,81,186 over 84, for a monthly saving that always looks smaller than the extra interest.

How the Loan EMI Calculator does the maths

The three fields are read as p (Loan Amount), r (Interest Rate per year) and m (Loan Tenure in months). The annual rate becomes a monthly decimal, i = r ÷ 12 ÷ 100, and the instalment is the annuity payment EMI = p × i × (1+i)^m ÷ ((1+i)^m − 1). Nothing is rounded part-way: Total Amount Paid is that unrounded EMI × m, and Total Interest is the total minus p. The amortisation behind the formula is simpler than the formula itself — each month the lender charges the outstanding balance × i, the remainder of the EMI reduces the balance, and the next month's interest is charged on the smaller balance. On ₹8,00,000 at 9% the first month's interest is ₹6,000 of the ₹16,607 instalment, and the final instalment is almost entirely principal.

When to reach for the Loan EMI Calculator

  • You are financing a car, two-wheeler, phone, furniture or hospital bill and a dealer has printed a monthly figure you want to check before signing it.
  • One lender quotes a reducing-balance rate and another a flat rate, and both have to be in the same unit before either can be called cheaper.
  • You are choosing between a three-year, five-year and seven-year term on the same amount and want the monthly saving set against the extra interest in rupees.

Mistakes the Loan EMI Calculator avoids

  • Typing years into the Loan Tenure field. It is in months, so a five-year loan is 60; enter 5 and the tool prices a five-month loan with an EMI of ₹1,63,618 instead of ₹16,607.
  • Reading a flat rate as an annual one. A flat 10% over three years on ₹5,00,000 charges ₹1,50,000 of interest on the whole amount from day one, an EMI of ₹18,056 — the same as a reducing-balance loan at roughly 18% a year.
  • Deciding on the EMI and ignoring the charges outside it. A 1-2% processing fee and a 2-4% foreclosure charge can add more than a percentage point to the real cost, and neither appears in the EMI or in the total this tool shows.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Tool aapke teen fields ko p (Loan Amount), r (Interest Rate per year) aur m (Loan Tenure in months) maanta hai, saal ke rate ko mahine ka decimal banata hai — i = r ÷ 12 ÷ 100 — aur phir annuity formula lagata hai: EMI = p × i × (1+i)^m ÷ ((1+i)^m − 1). Beech me koi rounding nahi hoti: Total Amount Paid unrounded EMI × m hai, aur Total Interest us total me se p ghata kar. Formula se amortisation aasan hai: har mahine interest = bachi hui balance × i, EMI ka baaki hissa principal ghataata hai, aur agle mahine interest chhoti balance par lagta hai. ₹8,00,000 par 9% me pehle mahine EMI ₹16,607 me se ₹6,000 interest hai, aur aakhri instalment lagbhag poora principal.
