How the Implied Valuation works

When someone says "50 lakh for 5%", find out what that says the whole company is worth. Enter Investment Amount and Equity Offered above. Nothing you type for the Implied Valuation is sent anywhere.

Good to know: Formula: Valuation = Investment ÷ (Equity % ÷ 100)

Worked example

An investor offers ₹10 lakh for 10% of the company. The cheque and the stake are two views of one price; divide the first by the second and the sentence turns into a ₹1 crore valuation of the whole business.

What you enter

  • Investment Amount: 1000000 ₹
  • Equity Offered: 10 %

What the Implied Valuation returns

  • Implied Valuation: ₹1,00,00,000

₹10 lakh for 10% prices the company at ₹1 crore - those are the two fields above. Ask for the same ₹10 lakh at 20% instead and the implied price drops to ₹50 lakh; change either field and the whole-company number follows.

What the Implied Valuation is for

A quote always arrives as a slice, never as a price: an angel says ₹10 lakh for 10%, an accelerator says ₹40 lakh for 8%, a forwarded message says the company is "raising at 5 crore". Implied Valuation converts any one of those slices into the single number both sides can argue about - the price of the whole company - by dividing the cheque by the fraction of the business it buys. ₹10 lakh for 10% is a ₹1 crore company; ₹40 lakh for 8% is a ₹5 crore company. The decision it settles is narrower than "what am I worth" and far more useful: is this particular quote cheap, fair or expensive, using only the two numbers that were actually spoken. Its real job is to kill the habit of comparing cheques. A larger cheque at a larger stake can put a smaller price on the business - ₹50 lakh for 25% values it at ₹2 crore, well below the ₹3 crore implied by a ₹30 lakh cheque at 10% - so offers are ranked by price and never by size. Treat the answer as the value of the company once this cheque is in, because the amount you divided is already inside the total; and treat it as arithmetic on one sentence rather than a valuation, since changing the stake, splitting the cheque into two tranches or carving an ESOP pool out of the same round makes the same cheque imply a different price.

How the Implied Valuation does the maths

Implied Valuation = amt ÷ (eq ÷ 100) - the cheque divided by the stake written as a fraction. With amt = ₹10,00,000 and eq = 10 the divisor is 0.10, so 10,00,000 ÷ 0.10 = ₹1,00,00,000, a ₹1 crore company. Reverse it to audit a claim: the stake a cheque buys is amt ÷ implied valuation × 100, so ₹10 lakh inside a ₹1 crore company is 10% and the same ₹10 lakh inside a ₹2 crore company is only 5%. Doubling eq while leaving amt fixed halves the implied price, which is exactly why two quotes must both pass through this division before they can be compared.

When to reach for the Implied Valuation

  • Someone speaks a cheque and a stake in the same breath and you want the whole-company price hiding inside the sentence.
  • Two offers arrive with different sizes and different stakes and you need them ranked by price rather than by cheque.
  • A deck, a pitch or a forwarded message quotes a slice and you want to test whether the arithmetic behind it holds up.
  • You are about to repeat a valuation to your team and want to trace it back to the quote it came from.

Mistakes the Implied Valuation avoids

  • Typing the stake as 10 instead of the fraction 10% stands for. The calculator divides eq by 100 first, so a quote entered as a multiplier comes out a hundred times too small.
  • Ranking offers by the size of the cheque. ₹50 lakh for 25% implies ₹2 crore and ₹30 lakh for 10% implies ₹3 crore; the bigger cheque is the worse price, and only the division shows it.
  • Reading the answer as the founder's price before the money. The cheque sits inside the implied total, so quoting ₹1 crore from a ₹10 lakh-for-10% offer as the pre-money overstates what the business was worth the day before the round.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Quote hamesha ek hisse me aata hai, poore price me nahi: koi angel kehta hai ₹10 lakh for 10%, accelerator kehta hai ₹40 lakh for 8%, aur ek forwarded message kehta hai company "5 crore par raise" kar rahi hai. Implied Valuation in sab hisson ko us ek number me badal deta hai jis par dono side baat kar sakti hain - poore company ka price - cheque ko us fraction se divide karke jo woh kharidta hai. ₹10 lakh for 10% matlab ₹1 crore ki company; ₹40 lakh for 8% matlab ₹5 crore ki company. Ye tool "main kitne ki hoon" se chhota par kaafi zyada kaam ka decision settle karta hai: yahi quote sasta hai, theek hai ya mehnga, sirf un do numbers se jo asli me bole gaye the. Iska asli kaam cheque compare karne ki aadat todna hai. Bada cheque bade stake par chhota price bhi de sakta hai - ₹50 lakh for 25% company ko ₹2 crore par rakhta hai, jo ₹30 lakh for 10% ke ₹3 crore se kam hai - isliye offers price se rank hote hain, size se kabhi nahi. Answer ko aise padho jaise cheque aane ke baad company ki value, kyunki jo amount divide kiya woh total me pehle se hai; aur ise ek sentence ka hisaab samjho, valuation nahi: stake badlo, cheque do tranche me todo, ya usi round me ESOP pool nikaalo, aur wahi cheque alag price imply karne lagta hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Implied Valuation = amt ÷ (eq ÷ 100) - cheque ko stake se divide karo, stake fraction me. amt = ₹10,00,000 aur eq = 10 par divisor 0.10 hai, isliye 10,00,000 ÷ 0.10 = ₹1,00,00,000, yani ₹1 crore ki company. Kisi claim ko check karne ke liye ulta chalao: ek cheque jitna stake kharidta hai woh amt ÷ implied valuation × 100 hai, isliye ₹10 lakh ₹1 crore company me 10% hai, aur wahi ₹10 lakh ₹2 crore company me sirf 5%. amt wahi rakh kar eq double karo to implied price aadha ho jaata hai - isi wajah se do quotes ko compare karne se pehle dono ko is division se guzarna padta hai.

Kab use karein

  • Koi ek hi saans me cheque aur stake bolta hai aur aap us sentence ke andar chhupa poora company price jaanna chahte ho.
  • Do offers alag size aur alag stake ke saath aate hain aur aapko unhe cheque ki jagah price se rank karna hai.
  • Deck, pitch ya forwarded message ek hissa quote karta hai aur aap uska ganit parakhna chahte ho.
  • Team ko valuation dohraane se pehle aap us aakhri baar check karna chahte ho ki quote kahan se aaya tha.

Aam galtiyan

  • Stake ko 10 likhna, us fraction ki jagah jo 10% darshata hai. Calculator eq ko pehle 100 se divide karta hai, isliye multiplier ki tarah type kiya gaya same quote sau guna chhota jawab deta hai.
  • Offers ko cheque ke size se rank karna. ₹50 lakh for 25% ₹2 crore imply karta hai aur ₹30 lakh for 10% ₹3 crore; bada cheque kharab price hai, aur ye sirf division se dikhta hai.
  • Answer ko paisa aane se pehle ka founder price samajhna. Cheque implied total ke andar hai, isliye ₹10 lakh for 10% se nikla ₹1 crore pre-money bata dena galat hai - round se theek pehle business usse kam ki thi.
