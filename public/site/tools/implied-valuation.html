How this calculator works

When someone says "50 lakh for 5%", find out what that says the whole company is worth. Enter Investment Amount and Equity Offered above and the Implied Valuation result appears instantly — nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

Good to know: Formula: Valuation = Investment ÷ (Equity % ÷ 100)

Worked example

An investor offers ₹50 lakh for 5% of the company. Neither number says what the business is worth; divide one by the other and the offer turns into a ₹10 crore price for the whole company.

What you enter

  • Investment Amount: 1000000 ₹
  • Equity Offered: 10 %

What you get

  • Implied Valuation: ₹1,00,00,000

At ₹50 lakh for 5% the cheque prices the company at ₹10 crore. Offer the same ₹50 lakh for 10% and the implied price halves to ₹5 crore - change either field and the whole-company value moves with it.

What this calculator is for

An implied valuation is a back-solve. You are handed one slice of a company - a cheque of ₹50 lakh for 5% - and the tool works out what the whole company must be worth for that slice to be a fair price: ₹10 crore. The decision it settles is whether the number quoted in a WhatsApp message, a term sheet or a pitch is a fair price for the business or only a flattering price for a small piece of it. Founders talk in stakes and investors talk in cheques, so each side quotes the figure the other side never says out loud, and this division is what puts them on the same page. It is also the quickest way to catch a mismatched round. If one angel offers ₹50 lakh for 5% (₹10 crore implied) and another offers ₹1 crore for 20% (₹5 crore implied), the second deal is half the price for four times the stake - a bigger cheque and a worse deal. Compare implied valuations, never cheque sizes. And read the answer as a post-money figure, because the cheque being priced is already inside it.

How the maths works

Implied Valuation = Investment Amount ÷ (Equity Offered ÷ 100). In the calculator's own variables that is amt divided by eq expressed as a fraction: ₹50,00,000 for 5% gives 50,00,000 ÷ 0.05 = ₹10,00,00,000, or ₹10 crore. Turned around, the stake a cheque buys is amt ÷ implied valuation × 100, so ₹50 lakh into a ₹10 crore company is 5%. The answer is a post-money valuation because the new money is already counted; the pre-money is that answer minus the cheque, ₹9.5 crore in this example.

When to reach for it

  • An investor names a cheque and a stake in one sentence and you want the whole-company price hiding behind them.
  • Two offers arrive with different cheque amounts and you need to rank them by the price each one implies, not by the headline figure.
  • You are repeating a pitch valuation to your team and want to check that the 'X for Y%' adds up first.
  • You are advising a friend who has been offered a stake and wants to know what price that offer really puts on their company.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the implied number as pre-money. The cheque is already inside the price, so ₹50 lakh for 5% implies a ₹10 crore company once the money lands, not before it.
  • Comparing cheque amounts instead of implied prices. ₹1 crore for 20% implies ₹5 crore, a worse price than ₹50 lakh for 5% even though the cheque is twice as big.
  • Treating the number as a promise. It is only the arithmetic of one offer, and an ESOP top-up or liquidation preference can change what the quoted 5% is worth on the day the company sells.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Implied valuation ek back-solve hai. Aapko company ka ek chhota hissa diya jaata hai - ₹50 lakh ka cheque 5% ke liye - aur tool nikaalta hai ki poora business kitne ka hona chahiye tabhi ye hissa theek price par ho: ₹10 crore. Ye tool us decision ko settle karta hai ki WhatsApp message, term sheet ya pitch me jo number bola gaya hai woh business ka sachcha price hai ya sirf ek chhote hisse ka chikna price. Founders stake me baat karte hain aur investors cheque me, isliye dono apne side ka woh figure bolte hain jo saamne wala kabhi nahi kehta, aur yahi division dono ko ek page par laata hai. Ye mismatched round pakadne ka sabse tez tareeka bhi hai. Agar ek angel ₹50 lakh de 5% ke liye (₹10 crore implied) aur doosra ₹1 crore de 20% ke liye (₹5 crore implied), to doosra deal aadhe price par chaar guna stake maangta hai - bada cheque aur kharab deal. Implied valuation compare karo, cheque size kabhi nahi. Aur answer ko post-money samjho, kyunki jo cheque price kar raha hai woh usme pehle se hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Implied Valuation = Investment Amount ÷ (Equity Offered ÷ 100). Calculator ke variables me ye amt ko eq ke fraction se divide karna hai: ₹50,00,000 for 5% se 50,00,000 ÷ 0.05 = ₹10,00,00,000, yani ₹10 crore. Ulta karke, ek cheque jitna stake kharidta hai woh amt ÷ implied valuation × 100 hai, isliye ₹10 crore company me ₹50 lakh 5% hai. Answer post-money valuation hai kyunki naya paisa usme shamil hai; pre-money is answer se cheque ghata kar milta hai, is example me ₹9.5 crore.

  • Do offers alag cheque amounts ke saath aate hain aur aapko headline figure ki jagah har ek ke implied price se rank karna hai.
  • Team ko pitch valuation repeat karne se pehle aap check karna chahte ho ki 'X for Y%' ka hisaab sahi baithta hai.
  • Aapka koi dost stake ka offer le kar aaya hai aur aapko batana hai ki us offer me company ka asli price kya ban raha hai.

Aam galtiyan

  • Implied number ko pre-money samajhna. Cheque price me pehle se hai, isliye ₹50 lakh for 5% paisa aane ke baad ₹10 crore ki company imply karta hai, pehle nahi.
  • Cheque amount compare karna implied price ki jagah. ₹1 crore for 20% ₹5 crore imply karta hai, jo ₹50 lakh for 5% se kharab price hai chahe cheque do guna bada ho.
  • Number ko promise maan lena. Ye sirf ek offer ka hisaab hai, aur ESOP top-up ya liquidation preference sale ke din us 5% ki value badal sakte hain.
