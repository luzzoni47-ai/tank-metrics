What this calculator is for

Implied Valuation turns one short line in a pitch — “I am putting in ₹50 lakh for 5%” — into the number it actually implies about the whole company. Divide the investment by the equity offered and you have the price the deal is putting on the business. It is the fastest sanity check in a funding conversation: the moment a stake is named, the valuation is decided, whether or not anyone says it out loud. The figure is only as good as the stake behind it, which is why the tool asks for both inputs. ₹50 lakh for 5% implies a ₹10 crore company; the same ₹50 lakh for 20% implies only a ₹2.5 crore company. Founders quote the first number and forget the second, and investors do the reverse. Having the implied valuation in front of you ends the roundabout talk and moves the discussion to the assumption underneath — the stake itself.

How the maths works

Implied Valuation = Investment Amount ÷ (Equity Offered ÷ 100). Type the amount (amt) and the equity percentage (eq); the tool divides the equity by 100 to make it a fraction, then divides the investment by that fraction. ₹10,00,000 for 10% implies ₹1,00,00,000. The arithmetic is a single division, so the value is not the maths but the discipline of stating the amount and the stake together.

When to reach for it

  • An investor or acquirer names a stake and you want the price it implies before you reply.
  • You are the investor and want to check whether the slice you are asking for is fair for the money.
  • You are writing a term-sheet summary and need the valuation behind each offer on one line.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the valuation without the stake. ₹50 lakh for 5% and ₹50 lakh for 20% are two very different companies.
  • Treating the implied valuation as agreed. It is what the offer implies, not what the company is worth; the argument is usually about the stake, not the price.
  • Forgetting that money normally goes in on a post-money basis, so the implied price already includes the new cheque and the founder's own stake is diluted by it.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Implied Valuation pitch ke ek chhote se vaakya — “main ₹50 lakh lagata hoon 5% ke liye” — ko us number me badal deta hai jo wo poori company ke baare me keh raha hota hai. Investment ko equity se divide karo aur deal ki price saamne aa jaati hai. Funding baat-cheet me ye sabse tez sanity check hai: jis pal stake boli jaati hai, valuation decide ho jaati hai, chahe koi usko zubaan par na laaye. Ye number utna hi accha hota hai jitna uske peeche ka stake, isliye tool dono input maangta hai. ₹50 lakh 5% ke liye matlab ₹10 crore ki company; wahi ₹50 lakh 20% ke liye matlab sirf ₹2.5 crore ki company. Founders pehla number bolte hain aur doosra bhool jaate hain, investors ulta karte hain. Implied valuation saamne hone se ghumaav ki baat khatam ho jaati hai aur baat asli assumption — stake — par aa jaati hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Implied Valuation = Investment Amount ÷ (Equity Offered ÷ 100). Amount (amt) aur equity percentage (eq) daalo; tool equity ko 100 se divide karke fraction banata hai aur investment ko usse divide karta hai. ₹10,00,000 for 10% ka matlab ₹1,00,00,000. Ganit ek hi division hai, isliye tool ki value maths me nahi, amount aur stake ko saath likhne ki aadat me hai.

Kab use karein

  • Jab investor ya acquirer stake bata de aur aapko reply se pehle uski implied price jaanni ho.
  • Jab aap khud investor ho aur dekhna chahte ho ki maanga gaya stake amount ke hisaab se theek hai ya nahi.
  • Jab aap term-sheet summary bana rahe ho aur har offer ke peeche ki valuation ek line me chahiye.

Aam galtiyan

  • Valuation ko stake ke bina padhna. ₹50 lakh for 5% aur ₹50 lakh for 20% do bilkul alag company hain.
  • Implied valuation ko final maan lena. Ye offer jo dikha raha hai wahi hai, company ki asli keemat nahi; bahas aksar stake par hoti hai, price par nahi.
  • Ye bhoolna ki paisa aksar post-money basis par aata hai, isliye implied price me naya cheque shamil hota hai aur founder ka stake dilute hota hai.
