How this calculator works

Two plain numbers in — how much of the market is sold, and how much of it is yours. Out comes your share, simple. Enter your numbers above and the result appears instantly — nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

Good to know: A share of 10% or less usually means you are a price-taker. The bigger your share, the more room you have to set your own price.

Worked example

Suppose 10,000 units were sold in your market and 6,000 of them were yours. Here is your share.

What you enter

  • Total market (units sold): 10000 units
  • Your units sold: 6000 units

What you get

  • Your market share: 60.0%
  • Total market: 10,000 units
  • You sold: 6,000 units
  • Others sold: 4,000 units

6,000 out of 10,000 is 60%. Change either number above and the share updates as you type — the arithmetic stays that simple.

What this calculator is for

The Market Share Calculator answers one question in plain numbers: out of everything sold in your market, how much is yours? Enter the total units sold in the market and the units you sold. That is it. You get your share as a percentage, plus how many units everyone else sold.

How the maths works

Your market share is your units divided by the total units, times 100. If 10,000 units were sold in the market and 6,000 of them were yours, your share is 6,000 / 10,000 = 60%. There is no compounding and no hidden step — it is one division. The tool also shows the other side: 4,000 units, or 40%, went to everyone else.

When to reach for it

  • You want to know how big you are in your own market, not in revenue terms.
  • You are checking whether a claim like 'we are the market leader' is actually true.
  • You are setting a target and want a starting share to grow from.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units with rupees. Count units against units, or money against money, never one of each.
  • Using a market figure that is bigger than the market you actually compete in, which flatters the share.
  • Reading a big share as automatically good — a 60% share of a tiny market is still a tiny business.
