What this calculator is for

Simple Interest settles the cost of money that is never reinvested: the interest is charged on the original Amount (Principal) alone, every year, and the interest already paid never enters the sum. Enter ₹10,000 at 8% for three years and it earns ₹800 a year, ₹2,400 in total — the same ₹800 whether you are in year one or year three. The tool is deliberately linear, and that linearity is the answer: a flat-rate loan, a bond coupon, a friendly loan carrying a fixed annual charge. The decision this page settles is which kind of interest a quote actually is. Many Indian lenders advertise a flat rate — “10% per annum” — and a flat rate means simple interest on the full principal for the whole term, not a reducing-balance rate. A flat 10% over three years costs roughly 17-18% a year on the money you still actually owe, and the way to see that is to put the flat figure through this tool first and set it against what a reducing-balance loan really charges. Where nothing compounds, this is the exact figure; where the balance falls as you repay, it is not, and the gap is the point.

How the maths works

With p the Amount (Principal), r the Interest Rate in per cent per year and t the Time in years, Interest Earned = p × r × t ÷ 100, and Total Amount = p + Interest Earned. Nothing is raised to a power and nothing is reinvested, so doubling t doubles the interest exactly. For p = 10,000, r = 8 and t = 3 the interest is 10,000 × 8 × 3 ÷ 100 = ₹2,400 and the total is ₹12,400. For a part-year, enter Time as a fraction of a year — six months is 0.5 — and the same multiplication still holds.

When to reach for it

  • A lender or a chit fund quotes a flat rate and you want the plain interest figure to set against a reducing-balance loan carrying the same headline rate.
  • You are lending a round sum to a friend or relative and want the rupees, not a compounding schedule, for a short agreed period.
  • You are checking a bond coupon or a short-term note that pays simple interest on its face value for the term.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a lender’s “flat 10%” as a 10% annual cost. Flat interest is charged on the full principal for the whole term, so on a three-year loan it works out to roughly 17-18% a year on the balance you actually still owe.
  • Using this total for anything that compounds. A fixed deposit, a recurring deposit and most savings accounts add the interest back, so the simple-interest figure understates the maturity value — by ₹3,100 on ₹1,00,000 at 10% over three years, and far more over longer terms.
  • Entering months in the Time field. The field is in years, so six months has to go in as 0.5; typing 6 quietly multiplies the interest by twelve.

Simple Interest us paise ki cost nipta deta hai jo kabhi reinvest nahi hota: interest sirf original Amount (Principal) par lagta hai, har saal, aur jo interest chuka diya gaya wo sum me kabhi wapas nahi aata. ₹10,000 par 8% aur teen saal me har saal ₹800, total ₹2,400 — wahi ₹800, chahe pehla saal ho ya teesra. Tool jaan-boojh kar linear hai, aur yahi uski answer hai: flat-rate loan, bond coupon, ya fixed annual charge wala udhaar. Is page ka decision ye hai ki quote kaunse type ka interest hai. Teen saal ka flat 10% us paise par lagbhag 17-18% saalana padta hai jise aap waqai abhi bhi owe karte ho, aur ye dekhne ka tareeka hai ki pehle flat figure is tool me daalo aur uski tulna kisi reducing-balance loan se karo. Jahan kuch compound nahi hota, ye exact figure hai; jahan balance repay karte-karte girta hai, ye nahi, aur wahi gap asli baat hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

p = Amount (Principal), r = Interest Rate (per cent per year) aur t = Time (years) lete hue Interest Earned = p × r × t ÷ 100, aur Total Amount = p + Interest Earned. Kuch bhi power par nahi uthaya jaata aur kuch reinvest nahi hota, isliye t double karne par interest bilkul double ho jaata hai. p = 10,000, r = 8 aur t = 3 par interest 10,000 × 8 × 3 ÷ 100 = ₹2,400 aur total ₹12,400. Saal se kam ke liye Time ko saal ka fraction daalo — chhe mahine 0.5 — aur wahi multiplication chalta rehta hai.

Kab use karein

  • Koi lender ya chit fund flat rate quote karta hai aur aap same headline rate wale reducing-balance loan se tulna karne ke liye plain interest figure chahte ho.
  • Aap kisi dost ya rishtedaar ko round sum udhaar de rahe ho aur chhoti tay period ke liye rupees chahiye, compounding schedule nahi.
  • Aap kisi bond coupon ya short-term note ko check kar rahe ho jo face value par term ke liye simple interest deta hai.

  • Time field me mahine daal dena. Field saal me hai, isliye chhe mahine 0.5 likhna hoga; 6 type karne se interest chupchaap baarah guna ho jaata hai.
