What this calculator is for

Break-Even Point answers the single question a new product has to clear before it can be called a business: how many units must we sell each month just to stop losing money? You give it three figures — the fixed costs you pay whether you sell one unit or a thousand (rent, salaries, software, the EMI on the machine), the price of one unit, and the variable cost of making or buying that one unit. It returns the number of units, and the revenue those units bring, at which profit is exactly zero. Everything below that line is a loss you are funding; everything above it is profit. What separates this tool from a margin calculator is that it works in quantities, not percentages. Gross margin tells you a ₹1,000 sale keeps ₹400; break-even tells you that 250 of those sales a month are the floor under the whole operation. That floor is the honest test of a business idea: a plan that needs 4,000 units a month from a market that buys 900 is not a pricing problem, it is a broken plan, and no amount of marketing fixes the arithmetic. Because it is built on a single contribution per unit, it assumes one product at one price; a shop selling fifty items with fifty different margins is really asking a mix question, and the answer only holds for the one product you typed in.

How the maths works

Contribution per unit is the selling price minus the variable cost of that unit: sp − vc. Break-Even Units is fixed costs divided by that contribution: fixed ÷ (sp − vc). Break-Even Revenue is those units at the selling price: units × sp. The tool calls the three inputs fixed, sp and vc, and shows Break-Even Units and Break-Even Revenue. With fixed ₹1,00,000, sp ₹1,000 and vc ₹600, contribution is ₹400, break-even is 1,00,000 ÷ 400 = 250 units, and break-even revenue is 250 × ₹1,000 = ₹2,50,000. Every lever sits in the denominator: shave ₹100 off vc and break-even falls to 200 units; add ₹100 to the price and it falls to the same 200, but one costs you a supplier negotiation and the other costs you a customer.

When to reach for it

  • You are about to sign a lease or hire, and you want the monthly sales figure that has to follow before those fixed costs are covered.
  • A lender or investor asks how much you must sell before the business stops burning money, and you need a number rather than a story.
  • You are choosing between two price-and-cost combinations and want to see which one reaches the floor with fewer units.

Common mistakes

  • Using a per-unit variable cost that has quietly become a total. If vc carries the whole month's material bill instead of one unit's, break-even comes out a hundred times too small and the plan looks easy.
  • Mixing the periods. fixed is a monthly figure, so the units and revenue it produces are monthly too; compare them with annual sales and every business looks safely past break-even.
  • Calling the revenue figure cash break-even. GST collected on top, a deposit, or a loan principal repayment are real outflows the contribution does not touch, so a business can be past break-even and still short of cash.
  • Averaging several products into one. A ₹400 contribution on the flagship and ₹40 on the accessory do not average into a plan — run the one you are actually betting on.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Break-Even Point wo sawaal solve karta hai jo har naye product ko pehle clear karna padta hai: mahine mein kitni units bikni chahiye taaki nuksaan band ho? Tool ko teen numbers do — fixed costs jo ek unit bhi na bike to bhi dene padte hain (rent, salary, software, machine ki EMI), ek unit ka selling price, aur usi ek unit ka variable cost. Jawab mein aata hai wo units, aur un units ki revenue, jahan profit exactly zero hai. Is line ke neeche jo bhi bikta hai wo aapki jeb se loss hai; is line ke upar sab profit hai. Margin calculator se ye tool alag isliye hai ki ye percentage ki jagah quantity mein kaam karta hai. Gross margin batata hai ₹1,000 ki sale se ₹400 bache; break-even batata hai ki poore business ke liye mahine mein 250 aisi sales kam se kam chahiye. Yahi floor kisi bhi idea ka imaandaar test hai: agar plan ko mahine ke 4,000 units chahiye aur market mein 900 bikte hain, to ye pricing ki dikkat nahi, ye galat plan hai — marketing se arithmetic theek nahi hoti. Kyunki ye ek hi contribution per unit par bana hai, ye ek product, ek price maanta hai; ek dukaan jisme pachaas alag margins ke saaman hain, wo asli mein mix ka sawaal pooch rahi hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Contribution per unit = selling price − variable cost, yaani sp − vc. Break-Even Units = fixed costs ÷ contribution = fixed ÷ (sp − vc). Break-Even Revenue = units × sp. Tool ke inputs fixed, sp aur vc hain, aur output Break-Even Units aur Break-Even Revenue. fixed ₹1,00,000, sp ₹1,000 aur vc ₹600 par contribution ₹400 hai, break-even 1,00,000 ÷ 400 = 250 units, aur break-even revenue 250 × ₹1,000 = ₹2,50,000. Har lever denominator mein hai: vc se ₹100 ghatane par break-even 200 units ho jaata hai; price mein ₹100 badhane par bhi wahi 200 — par ek ke liye supplier se baat karni hai, doosre ke liye customer ko manana hai.

Kab use karein

  • Lease ya hiring sign karne se pehle, ye dekhna hai ki wo fixed cost mahine ki kitni sales maangti hai.
  • Investor ya lender poochta hai ki paisa jalna band karne ke liye kitna bechna padega, aur aapko kahani ki jagah ek number chahiye.
  • Do price aur cost ke options mein se chunna hai ki kaunsa kam units mein floor tak pahunchta hai.

Aam galtiyan

  • Variable cost per unit ki jagah total daal dena. Agar vc mein poore mahine ka material bill hai, to break-even sau guna chhota aa jaata hai aur plan aasaan lagta hai.
  • Periods mix karna. fixed mahine ka hai, to units aur revenue bhi mahine ke hain; saal ki sales se compare karo to har business break-even ke paar lagta hai.
  • Revenue break-even ko cash break-even samajhna. GST, deposit ya loan ki principal repayment contribution se bahar hain, to break-even ke baad bhi cash ki kami reh sakti hai.
  • Kai products ko average kar dena. ₹400 contribution wale flagship aur ₹40 wale accessory ka average plan nahi banta — jis product par daanv laga rahe ho, wahi chalao.
