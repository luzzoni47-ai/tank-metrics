What this calculator is for

Inventory turnover counts how many times in a year a business sells through its entire stock and replaces it. It is the clearest single read on whether cash is working or sitting still: stock on a shelf earns nothing, ties up money you have already spent, and can go out of date or out of season before it sells. A high turnover means the goods move fast and cash comes back quickly; a low one means the warehouse is holding money the business could be using elsewhere. Dividing 365 by the ratio converts it into the more intuitive number: the average days a unit waits before it is sold.

How the maths works

Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory. Average inventory is the opening and closing stock added together and halved, so one stock-take does not distort the picture. A business with 48,00,000 of yearly COGS and 8,00,000 of average stock turns its inventory 6 times a year, which is 365 / 6 = about 61 days to sell through. Compare that against the terms your supplier gives you: if you get 30 days to pay and take 61 to sell, you are funding the gap yourself.

When to reach for it

  • You want to know whether stock levels are too high for the sales they generate.
  • You are negotiating supplier credit terms and need to know how long the goods really take to move.
  • You are comparing a fast line against a slow one to decide what to keep stocking.

Common mistakes

  • Using sales instead of cost of goods sold. Sales are marked up, so the ratio comes out too high and the stock looks faster than it is.
  • Taking one closing stock figure as the average. A seasonal peak or a clearance sale makes a single snapshot misleading.
  • Chasing a high ratio by under-stocking, then losing sales to stock-outs. Turnover is a means, not the goal.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Inventory turnover ginta hai ki ek saal me business apna poora stock kitni baar bech kar badalta hai. Ye sabse saaf ishara hai ki cash kaam kar raha hai ya pada hua hai: shelf par pada stock kuch kamata nahi, wo paisa band kar deta hai jo aap kharch kar chuke ho, aur bechne se pehle kharab ya season se bahar ho sakta hai. Zyada turnover matlab goods tez chalte hain aur cash jaldi wapas aata hai; kam turnover matlab warehouse wo paisa pakde hue hai jo business kahin aur use kar sakta tha. 365 ko ratio se divide karke wo number milta hai jo samajhna aasan hai: ek unit bechne se pehle average kitne din wait karti hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory. Average inventory matlab opening aur closing stock jod kar aadha karo, taki ek hi stock-take tasveer bigaad na de. Ek business jiska yearly COGS 48,00,000 hai aur average stock 8,00,000, wo apna inventory saal me 6 baar ghumata hai, yaani 365 / 6 = takriban 61 din me bech jaata hai. Ise apne supplier ki credit terms se compare karo: agar aapko pay karne ke 30 din milte hain aur bechne me 61 lagte hain, to wo gap aap khud fund kar rahe ho.

Kab use karein

  • Jab aap jaanna chahte ho ki jitni sale hoti hai uske hisaab se stock zyada hai ya nahi.
  • Jab aap supplier credit terms par baat kar rahe ho aur jaanna chahte ho ki goods asli me kitne din me chalte hain.
  • Jab aap ek tez line ko ek slow line se compare kar rahe ho ye tay karne ke liye ki kya stock karna hai.

Aam galtiyan

  • Sales use karna, cost of goods sold ke bajaye. Sales par markup hota hai, isliye ratio zyada aata hai aur stock asli se tez dikhta hai.
  • Ek closing stock figure ko average maan lena. Seasonal peak ya clearance sale ek snapshot ko galat bana deta hai.
  • Under-stocking se zyada ratio ke peeche bhaagna, aur phir stock-outs me sale khona. Turnover ek zariya hai, lakshya nahi.
