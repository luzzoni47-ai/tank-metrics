What this calculator is for

Return on Capital Employed answers a question ROI cannot: how hard is every rupee inside this business working, whether it came from you or from a lender? Capital employed is shareholders' equity plus long-term debt, so ROCE puts a debt-free shop and a heavily borrowed one on the same ruler. Two businesses can post the same ROI and very different ROCE, and the gap is almost always the borrowing. Reach for it when you are weighing an acquisition, a new machine or a second outlet against the money already tied up in the first one. It is a before-interest number: the tool divides operating profit by capital employed and stops there, because how the capital was financed is exactly the thing you are trying to see past.

How the maths works

ROCE is operating profit (EBIT) divided by capital employed, times 100. In this tool 'Operating Profit' is the ebit field and 'Capital Employed' is the ce field. Capital employed is total assets minus current liabilities, which is the same as shareholders' equity plus long-term debt. Rs 4,50,000 of operating profit on Rs 18,00,000 of capital employed gives 25%. Because the numerator is struck before interest and tax, ROCE does not move when the debt does, and that is what makes two differently financed companies comparable.

When to reach for it

  • You are comparing two companies with different debt levels and want a like-for-like return.
  • You are deciding whether a new machine or outlet earns more than the capital already sitting in the business.
  • An investor asks how well the company uses the whole balance sheet, not just the founders' money.

Common mistakes

  • Putting profit after interest in the numerator. ROCE takes EBIT on purpose so the debt neither flatters nor punishes the number; net profit belongs in ROE, not here.
  • Tinkering with capital employed - subtracting short-term creditors twice, or dropping cash entirely - so the ratio jumps while nothing real has changed.
  • Cheering a high ROCE on a business with almost no capital employed. A services firm that rents everything can show 200% and still own nothing worth buying.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Return on Capital Employed wahi sawaal uthata hai jo ROI nahi uthata: is business me jo bhi paisa laga hai - aapka ho ya bank ka - wo kitni mehnat kar raha hai? Capital employed matlab shareholders' equity plus long-term debt, isliye ek bina-karz ki dukaan aur ek bhaari loan wali dukaan ek hi scale par aa jaati hain. Do business ka ROI same ho sakta hai aur ROCE bilkul alag, aur wo gap aam taur par udhaar ka hota hai. Ganit kaise chalta hai

ROCE = operating profit (EBIT) / capital employed x 100. Is tool me 'Operating Profit' ebit field hai aur 'Capital Employed' ce field. Capital employed matlab total assets minus current liabilities, jo shareholders' equity plus long-term debt ke barabar hai. Rs 4,50,000 operating profit par Rs 18,00,000 capital employed = 25%. Kyunki upar wala number interest aur tax se pehle nikalta hai, ROCE udhaar badalne se nahi hilta - isi wajah se alag-alag funded companies compare ho paati hain.

Kab use karein

  • Jab do companies ka karz alag ho aur aap like-for-like return dekhna chahte ho.
  • Jab aap tay kar rahe ho ki nayi machine ya nayi branch business me lage paise se zyada kamayegi ya nahi.
  • Jab investor poochta hai ki company poori balance sheet ka kitna acha use kar rahi hai, sirf founders ke paise ka nahi.

Aam galtiyan

  • Numerator me interest ke baad ka profit daal dena. ROCE jaan-boojhkar EBIT leta hai taki karz number ko na chamkaye na dabaye; net profit ROE ka kaam hai.
  • Capital employed ke saath chhedchhad - short-term creditors do baar ghata dena, ya cash poori tarah hata dena - number uchhal jaata hai par asli me kuch nahi badalta.
  • Bahut kam capital employed wale business ka 200% ROCE dekh kar khush ho jaana. Jo firm sab kuch kiraye par leti hai wo 200% dikha sakti hai aur phir bhi kuch kharidne layak nahi hota.
