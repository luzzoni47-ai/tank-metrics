How the maths works

Every one of these tools is one arithmetic rule applied to the numbers you type. A percentage is the part divided by the whole, multiplied by 100. Simple interest is principal x rate x time. Compound interest adds the interest back to the principal at each period, so the base grows. The calculator applies the rule and shows you the result, the intermediate steps and, where it matters, the total you will actually pay or receive.

When to reach for it

  • You are checking a shop bill or an online discount before you pay.
  • You are working out a share of a total — a partner's cut, a deposit, a commission.
  • You want a quick sanity check before you commit to a number on a form.

Common mistakes

  • Adding percentages together. A 10% rise followed by a 10% fall is not back to where you started — it is 99% of it.
  • Using simple interest for anything longer than a year. Banks compound, so a simple-interest answer will understate what you owe.
  • Rounding too early. Keep the full figure and round only the answer you write down.
