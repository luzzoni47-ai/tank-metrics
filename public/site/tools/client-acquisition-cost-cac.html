What this calculator is for

Client acquisition cost is the service firm's version of the same arithmetic, and it answers a different question: what does one signed retainer cost the agency in business development? The calculator adds Marketing Spend and Sales Cost - the BD salary, the conferences, the CRM subscription and the unpaid hours spent writing proposals that were never accepted - then divides by New Clients actually signed. ₹3,00,000 of marketing plus ₹1,50,000 of sales cost that lands 9 signed clients is ₹4,50,000 ÷ 9 = ₹50,000 a client, and if those 9 came out of 45 pitches, each pitch cost ₹10,000 to put in front of a buyer. That pair of numbers is what decides how much prospecting the firm can afford. A retainer billed at ₹25,000 a month for a year is a ₹3,00,000 fee, so ₹50,000 per client returns roughly six rupees in the first year alone - room to hire another BD person. Spread the same ₹4,50,000 over 3 signed clients instead and each one cost ₹1,50,000; at that point the agency is buying revenue at a loss and has to repair its win rate before spending another rupee on lead generation.

How the maths works

Client CAC = (Marketing Spend + Sales Cost) ÷ New Clients. Marketing Spend covers campaigns, events and content; Sales Cost covers the business development salary, commission and pitch expenses; New Clients counts only signed contracts - not leads, discovery calls or proposals sent. Marketing ₹3,00,000 and sales ₹1,50,000 over a quarter make ₹4,50,000, which across 9 signed clients is ₹50,000 each. Divide the client's first-year fee by that figure for the return, and keep the proposal count separately so the win rate behind it stays visible.

When to reach for it

  • You are setting next quarter's business development budget and need the ceiling on what one signed client may cost.
  • Referrals bring clients free while outbound burns salary, and you want one cost-per-client figure that compares them fairly.
  • A retainer client is about to renew, and you need to confirm the fee still covers what it cost to win them.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by proposals sent or discovery calls held rather than contracts signed, which turns a one-in-five win rate into a flattering number.
  • Leaving the partners' unpaid pitch hours out of Sales Cost, so a firm that wins one pitch in five looks far cheaper than it is.
  • Comparing the client CAC with a single month's retainer instead of the first-year fee, so a healthy channel looks unaffordable.

Kab use karein

  • Agle quarter ka business development budget set karte waqt, ek signed client par kitna kharch ho sakta hai woh ceiling chahiye.
  • Referrals muft client laate hain aur outbound salary jalaata hai - dono ko ek cost-per-client figure par naapna hai.
  • Ek retainer client renew hone wala hai, aur check karna hai ki fee aaj bhi jeetne ke kharch ko cover karti hai.

Aam galtiyan

  • Bheje proposals ya hui discovery calls par divide karna, signed contracts par nahi - isse paanch me ek jeetne ka win rate accha dikhne lagta hai.
  • Partners ke unpaid pitch ghante Sales Cost se bahar rakhna, jisse paanch me ek pitch jeetne wali firm asli se kaafi sasti dikhti hai.
  • Client CAC ko ek mahine ke retainer se compare karna, pehle saal ki fee se nahi - tab healthy channel bhi afford se bahar lagta hai.
