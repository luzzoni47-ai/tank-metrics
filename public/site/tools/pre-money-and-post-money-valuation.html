What this calculator is for

Pre-money and post-money are the two prices in every funding round, and the distance between them is exactly the cheque. Pre-money is what the company was worth the instant before the new money landed; post-money is that figure plus the investment. Agree a ₹8 crore pre-money, take a ₹2 crore cheque, and the company is priced at ₹10 crore once the round closes, with the cheque buying 20% of it. The decision this settles is how much of the company a cheque actually costs - the number that goes into the term sheet and onto the cap table. Founders usually negotiate the pre-money and then get surprised by the stake, because the stake is measured against a bigger number. Dividing the cheque by the pre-money overstates it every time; divide by the post-money and the real share appears. Every rupee added to the cheque at a fixed pre-money buys a little more of the company, which is why a round is agreed on the pre-money and audited on the post-money. The Implied Valuation tool runs the same arithmetic from the other end, starting from a stake and returning a price.

How the maths works

Post-money Valuation = Pre-money Valuation + Investment Amount. Equity Given = Investment Amount ÷ Post-money Valuation × 100. With an ₹8,00,00,000 pre-money and a ₹2,00,00,000 cheque the post-money is ₹10,00,00,000 and the cheque buys 2,00,00,000 ÷ 10,00,00,000 = 20%. Work backwards to test a term sheet: if an investor says ₹2 crore should buy 20%, the pre-money being agreed is ₹8 crore, not ₹10 crore. The gap between the two prices is always the cheque itself.

When to reach for it

  • A term sheet names a pre-money and a cheque and you want the post-money and the exact stake it costs.
  • The same pre-money is on the table with a bigger cheque and a smaller one, and you need the dilution each causes.
  • You want to test an investor's '₹X for Y%' against the pre-money they claim to be paying.
  • You are drawing a simple cap table and need the post-money value before you can split it into shareholder stakes.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing the cheque by the pre-money. ₹2 crore into an ₹8 crore pre-money is 20% of the ₹10 crore post-money, not the 25% that 2 ÷ 8 suggests.
  • Quoting a post-money as the founder's price. It includes the new money, so it is always the larger figure and the one the stake is measured against.
  • Forgetting the ESOP pool, which is usually carved out of the pre-money and leaves founders with less than 100% minus the investor's slice.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Pre-money aur post-money har funding round ke do price hain, aur dono ke beech ka gap bilkul cheque ke barabar hota hai. Pre-money woh value hai jo company ki thi naya paisa aane se theek pehle; post-money woh figure plus investment hai. ₹8 crore pre-money tay karo, ₹2 crore ka cheque lo, to round band hone par company ₹10 crore ki price par hai aur cheque uske 20% kharidta hai. Ye tool ye decision settle karta hai ki ek cheque company ka kitna hissa asli me kharidta hai - wahi number jo term sheet aur cap table me jaata hai. Founders aksar pre-money par negotiate karte hain aur phir stake dekh kar chauk jaate hain, kyunki stake ek bade number par naapa jaata hai. Cheque ko pre-money se divide karna har baar stake badha kar dikhata hai; post-money se divide karo to asli hissa milta hai. Fixed pre-money par cheque me har rupaya company ka thoda zyada hissa kharidta hai, isliye round pre-money par tay hota hai aur post-money par check hota hai. Implied Valuation tool yahi hisaab doosre sire se karta hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Post-money Valuation = Pre-money Valuation + Investment Amount. Equity Given = Investment Amount ÷ Post-money Valuation × 100. ₹8,00,00,000 pre-money aur ₹2,00,00,000 cheque par post-money ₹10,00,00,000 hai aur cheque 2,00,00,000 ÷ 10,00,00,000 = 20% kharidta hai. Term sheet test karne ke liye ulta chalao: agar investor kehta hai ₹2 crore se 20% milna chahiye, to tay hone wala pre-money ₹8 crore hai, ₹10 crore nahi. Dono price ke beech ka gap hamesha cheque hi hota hai.

Kab use karein

  • Term sheet me pre-money aur cheque likha hai aur aapko post-money aur uska asli stake chahiye.
  • Wahi pre-money table par hai par cheque bada aur chhota do options me hai, aur aapko dono ka dilution chahiye.
  • Aap investor ke '₹X for Y%' ko us pre-money se check karna chahte ho jo woh kehta hai ki de raha hai.
  • Aap ek simple cap table bana rahe ho aur shareholders me baantne se pehle post-money value chahiye.

Aam galtiyan

  • Cheque ko pre-money se divide karna. ₹8 crore pre-money me ₹2 crore ₹10 crore post-money ka 20% hai, woh 25% nahi jo 2 ÷ 8 se lagta hai.
  • Post-money ko founder ka price samajhna. Isme naya paisa shamil hai, isliye ye hamesha bada figure hota hai aur stake isi par naapa jaata hai.
  • ESOP pool bhoolna, jo aksar pre-money me se nikaala jaata hai aur founders ko 100% minus investor ka hissa se kam chhod deta hai.
