What this calculator is for

The Home Loan EMI Calculator prices a housing loan, and a housing loan is a different animal from any other instalment loan: it is the largest borrowing most families ever take, it runs for twenty to thirty years, and it is secured against the roof over your head. You enter the Loan Amount, the Annual Interest Rate and the Loan Tenure in years, and the tool returns the fixed monthly EMI and the total repayment. On ₹30,00,000 at 8% over 20 years the EMI is ₹25,093 and the total repayment is ₹60,22,368 — you pay back roughly double what you borrowed, and almost none of that doubling is visible in the monthly figure. What makes a home loan its own calculation is the tenure. Stretch that same ₹30,00,000 loan from 20 years to 30 and the EMI drops only to ₹22,013 — about ₹3,000 a month — while the total repayment climbs from ₹60,22,368 to ₹79,24,657, nearly ₹19 lakh more. Lenders price the longer tenure as comfort, and it is bought at a heavy price, which is why this page exists to show the two numbers side by side. The other home-loan-specific decision it serves is prepayment: in the first five years most of the EMI is interest, so a lump sum paid then cuts the term and the total far more than the same money paid in year fifteen. Add the costs the EMI does not include — stamp duty and registration on the agreement, processing fees and insurance — and the real cost of the house is higher again.

How the maths works

EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the Loan Amount, r the monthly rate (Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the term in months (Loan Tenure in years × 12). Total Repayment = EMI × n. For ₹30,00,000 at 8% over 20 years: r = 0.00667, n = 240, the EMI is ₹25,093 and the total is ₹60,22,368. Over 30 years the same loan gives n = 360, an EMI of ₹22,013 and a total of ₹79,24,657. The rate is annual and the tenure is in years, so a 20-year loan is entered as 20, not 240.

When to reach for it

  • You are house-hunting and need to know whether the monthly EMI fits your income before you make an offer, not after the bank sanctions it.
  • You are choosing between a 20-year and a 30-year tenure and want to see the monthly saving against the extra lakhs of total interest.
  • You are deciding whether to prepay a lump sum now or invest it, and want to see how much of the early EMI is interest that a prepayment would cut.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the 30-year tenure for the lower EMI. On ₹30,00,000 at 8% it saves about ₹3,000 a month but adds nearly ₹19 lakh of interest over the life of the loan.
  • Budgeting only for the EMI. Stamp duty, registration, processing fees and home-loan insurance are paid outside the EMI and are not in the total repayment the tool shows.
  • Prepaying late instead of early. In the first five years most of each EMI is interest, so a prepayment then removes far more future interest than the same amount paid in year fifteen.
