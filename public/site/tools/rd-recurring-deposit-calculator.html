What this calculator is for

The RD Calculator answers what a fixed monthly deposit becomes by maturity. You commit the same amount on the same date every month, the bank adds interest to each instalment from the day it lands, and at the end you get back every rupee you deposited plus the interest each instalment earned. The tool takes Monthly Deposit, Annual Interest Rate and Tenure in months, and returns Total Deposits, Maturity Amount and Interest Earned as three separate figures - so you see the return, not just a headline maturity. An RD is the deposit-side twin of a SIP: the same monthly discipline, the opposite risk. The rate is fixed the day you open it, so the Maturity Amount is knowable in advance, but that certainty is also the ceiling - an RD will not outgrow a good equity fund over a long horizon. Where this calculator earns its keep is in showing Interest Earned next to Total Deposits: at ₹5,000 a month for 24 months, only ₹9,154 of the ₹1,29,154 maturity is interest, which is the honest measure of what the bank is paying you. That split is exactly what an advertised 7% a year hides.

How the maths works

Each monthly instalment earns interest for the months it remains in the account, so the first deposit compounds the longest and the last one for a single month. The tool sets a = rate / 1200 (the monthly rate) and n = months, and Maturity Amount = Monthly Deposit x ((1 + a)^n - 1) / a x (1 + a); Total Deposits = Monthly Deposit x n; Interest Earned = Maturity Amount - Total Deposits. At ₹5,000 a month, 7% and 24 months, a = 0.005833 and n = 24, giving a factor of 25.83, so Maturity Amount = ₹1,29,154, Total Deposits = ₹1,20,000 and Interest Earned = ₹9,154. The (1 + a) at the end is the tool crediting each instalment at the start of its month, which is why its figure sits at the generous end of what banks quote.

When to reach for it

  • You are about to open an RD and want the exact maturity and interest before you commit, rather than the approximately figure a bank app shows.
  • You are comparing an RD with a debt mutual fund or a short-term FD for a goal two or three years out, and want the guaranteed number to weigh against the fund's indicative one.
  • You want to see how the interest changes when you stretch the Tenure - the same Monthly Deposit over 36 months rather than 24 - to judge whether the extra lock-in is worth it.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the Annual Interest Rate applies to the whole balance for the whole term. Only the earliest instalments earn for the full Tenure; the last ones earn for a month, which is why Interest Earned is a small slice of the Maturity Amount.
  • Comparing the RD's Maturity Amount with a lump-sum FD's, when the RD only locks the full balance near the end. The RD ties up far less capital on average, so its effective yield is higher than the raw interest figure suggests.
  • Planning an RD for a ten-year goal. The rate is fixed and modest, so over a long horizon the compounding equity gives up in certainty it more than recovers in return; size a short-term goal with this tool, not a retirement one.

  • RD kholne wale ho aur commit karne se pehle exact maturity aur interest jaanna chahte ho, bank app ka approximately figure nahi.
  • Do-teen saal door ke goal ke liye RD ko debt mutual fund ya short-term FD se compare kar rahe ho, aur guaranteed number ko fund ke indicative number se tolna chahte ho.

Aam galtiyan

  • Annual Interest Rate ko poore balance par poore term ke liye apply hone ka maan lena. Sirf shuruaati instalments poora Tenure kamate hain; aakhri wale ek mahina, isliye Interest Earned Maturity Amount ka chhota hissa hai.
  • RD ke Maturity Amount ko lump-sum FD se compare karna, jabki RD poora balance sirf ant mein lock karta hai. RD average par kaafi kam capital bandhta hai, isliye uska effective yield raw interest figure se zyada hai.
  • RD ko das saal ke goal ke liye plan karna. Rate fixed aur modest hai, isliye lambe horizon par compounding equity certainty jo chhodti hai usse zyada return de deti hai; calculator ko short-term goal ke liye use karo, retirement ke liye nahi.
