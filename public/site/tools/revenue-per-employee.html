What this calculator is for

Revenue per employee divides what the business earns in a year by the number of people it employs, giving a rough read on how productive the team is and whether headcount has outrun the work. It is a blunt instrument, but it is the fastest sanity check there is: if revenue per head is falling while the team grows, the new hires are not yet paying for themselves. The number is only meaningful against a like-for-like comparison, because a software firm and a trading business will never share a figure. Used honestly, over several years, it shows whether growth is being bought with people faster than it is being earned from customers.

How the maths works

Revenue per Employee = Yearly Revenue / Number of Employees. A business turning over 1,20,00,000 with 12 people earns 10,00,000 per head. The same revenue with 20 people is 6,00,000 per head, which may be fine if the extra eight are building something that pays next year, and may be a warning if they are not. Track the ratio year on year rather than against a single industry benchmark, because the trend inside your own business is the signal that matters.

When to reach for it

  • You are about to add headcount and want to see the revenue each new hire has to carry.
  • Revenue is growing but profit is not, and you suspect the team has grown faster than the work.
  • You are comparing your productivity against another business in the same line.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing across industries. A distribution business will always look thinner than a software one.
  • Counting only full-time staff while ignoring a large contractor bench, which understates the real team.
  • Cutting headcount to lift the ratio when the people were building next year's revenue.
