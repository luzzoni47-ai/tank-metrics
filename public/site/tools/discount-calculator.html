What this calculator is for

The Discount Calculator works out what a percentage off actually takes off a price. Enter the original price and the discount, and it shows two things: the rupees you save and the price you finally pay. That is the number that decides whether a sale is worth acting on — not the percentage, which is the part shops advertise because it sounds bigger than it is.

How the maths works

The saving is the original price multiplied by the discount, divided by 100: `price` × `disc` ÷ 100. The final price is the original minus that saving: `price` − saving. At ₹2,000 with a 25% discount the saving is ₹500 and the final price is ₹1,500. Two discounts applied one after another are not added together — 30% then 20% leaves you paying 0.7 × 0.8 = 0.56 of the price, which is 44% off, not 50%.

When to reach for it

  • A marketplace or shop shows a flat percentage off and you want the rupee amount before you decide.
  • You are comparing two sale prices where the discounts and the original prices are both different.
  • A coupon stacks on top of an existing sale and you want to know the real final price.

Common mistakes

  • Adding two discounts together. A 30% discount followed by a 20% one is 44% off the original, not 50%, because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price.
  • Reading the discount as a share of the final price. The percentage is taken off the original marked price; once the price has dropped, the same rupees are a larger percentage of what is left.
  • Comparing discount percentages across different prices. 40% off ₹2,000 saves ₹800, which is more than 50% off ₹1,000 at ₹500.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Discount Calculator batata hai ki kisi price par diya gaya percentage off asli me kitna paisa bachata hai. Original price aur discount daalo, aur tool do cheezein dikhata hai: kitne rupaye bache aur final kitna dena padega. Yahi number decide karta hai ki sale sach me faydemand hai ya nahi — sirf percentage nahi, kyunki dukaan wahi dikhati hai jo bada lagta hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Bachat = original price × discount ÷ 100 (`price` × `disc` ÷ 100). Final price = original minus bachat (`price` − saving). ₹2,000 par 25% discount me bachat ₹500 aur final price ₹1,500 hota hai. Do discount ek ke baad ek lagte hain to unhe jodte nahi — 30% phir 20% me aap price ka 0.7 × 0.8 = 0.56 dete ho, matlab 44% off, 50% nahi.

Kab use karein

  • Marketplace ya dukaan flat percentage off dikhaye aur aap decide karne se pehle rupaye me amount jaanna chahein.
  • Do sale prices compare kar rahe ho jahan discount aur original price dono alag hain.
  • Coupon kisi chalti sale ke upar lage aur aapko asli final price jaanna ho.

Aam galtiyan

  • Do discounts ko jod dena. 30% discount ke baad 20% matlab 44% off, 50% nahi, kyunki doosra discount pehle se kam hue price par lagta hai.
  • Discount ko final price ka hissa maan lena. Percentage original marked price se ghata jaata hai; price girne ke baad wahi rupaye bacha hua price ka bada percentage ban jaate hain.
  • Alag alag prices par sirf discount percentage compare karna. ₹2,000 par 40% se ₹800 bachte hain, jo ₹1,000 par 50% ke ₹500 se zyada hai.
