What this calculator is for

The Revenue Multiple answers the fastest question in any valuation: for every rupee of sales the business rings up in a year, how many rupees is the buyer offering? It needs only two figures, the price and the annual revenue, and neither is an accounting judgement call - which is why it is the first multiple a broker quotes and the one a founder can sanity-check in a minute. When a business is early, unprofitable, or growing fast enough that profit is deliberately spent on growth, revenue is often the only figure both sides trust, and this multiple is the only price language left. It is a yardstick, not a verdict. Revenue says nothing about whether the sales are profitable, recurring or one-off, so the number is only meaningful next to a margin. Two businesses can both trade at 3x revenue while one is worth the price and the other is not, because the first converts 30% of each rupee to profit and the second converts 5%. Use the multiple to place the price in a range, then use margin and growth to decide where in that range the business deserves to sit.

How the maths works

The tool divides the valuation you enter by the annual revenue you enter and shows the result in times. In the calculator's variables that is val divided by rev: a 4,50,000 valuation over 1,80,000 of annual revenue gives 2.50x. A 3x multiple means the buyer pays three rupees for every rupee of yearly sales. It is always the smaller of the two multiples for a profitable business - revenue is bigger than EBITDA, so dividing by it gives a smaller multiple - and the gap between the revenue and EBITDA multiples is exactly the profit margin. Hold the two side by side and the margin falls out.

When to reach for it

  • You have an offer or a term sheet and want to know, in one number, whether the price is in the normal range for your sector.
  • You are comparing two companies and revenue is the only figure you have for both - common for early-stage or unprofitable businesses.
  • You are setting your own asking price and want to work back from the multiple buyers in your category actually pay.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing recurring and one-off revenue. Subscription or retainer revenue earns a far higher multiple than one-off project or product sales, so a blended figure overstates the value of the recurring part and understates the rest. Split the two before you apply a multiple to either.
  • Reading a high multiple as good news. A 10x revenue price on a business keeping 5% of each rupee is worse than a 3x price on one keeping 30%; the multiple is only as good as the margin behind it.
  • Choosing the wrong twelve months. A buyer means the last twelve months or the next twelve, and in a fast-growing business those can differ by a third. The multiple swings with whichever you pick, so fix the period before you compare two offers.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Revenue Multiple kisi bhi valuation ka sabse tez sawaal hai: business saal me jitni sales karta hai, uske har ek rupaye ke badle buyer kitne rupaye de raha hai? Isme sirf do figures chahiye, price aur saalana revenue, aur dono me koi accounting judgement nahi hota - isliye broker isi multiple ko sabse pehle batata hai aur founder ise ek minute me khud check kar leta hai. Jab business early ho, profitable na ho, ya itni tez badh raha ho ki profit jaan-boojh kar growth par kharch ho raha ho, to revenue hi ek aisa figure hota hai jispe dono taraf bharosa karti hain, aur yahi multiple bacha hua ek price language hota hai. Ye ek yardstick hai, verdict nahi. Revenue ye nahi batata ki sales profitable hain, recurring hain ya one-off, isliye ye number sirf margin ke saath hi matlab rakhta hai. Do business dono 3x revenue par chal sakti hain, phir bhi ek price ke laayak hai aur doosri nahi, kyunki pehli har rupaye ka 30% profit me badalti hai aur doosri 5%. Multiple se price ko ek range me rakho, phir margin aur growth se tay karo ki business us range me kahan deserve karti hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Tool aapki daali valuation ko daale hue saalana revenue se divide karta hai aur jawab times me dikhata hai. Calculator ke variables me ye val divided by rev hai: 4,50,000 ki valuation 1,80,000 ke saalana revenue par 2.50x deti hai. 3x ka matlab hai buyer saalana bikri ke har rupaye par teen rupaye de raha hai. Profitable business ke liye ye do multiples me se hamesha chhota hota hai - revenue EBITDA se bada hota hai, isliye usse divide karne par multiple chhota aata hai - aur revenue aur EBITDA multiples ka gap hi profit margin hota hai. Dono ko saath rakho aur margin khud nikal aata hai.

Kab use karein

  • Jab aapke paas offer ya term sheet ho aur ek number me jaanna ho ki price aapke sector ki normal range me hai ya nahi.
  • Jab aap do companies compare kar rahe ho aur dono ka sirf revenue figure available ho - early-stage ya unprofitable businesses me aam baat hai.
  • Jab aap apna asking price tay kar rahe ho aur chahte ho ki category me buyers jo multiple asli me dete hain usse peeche chalo.

Aam galtiyan

  • Recurring aur one-off revenue mila dena. Subscription ya retainer revenue ko one-off project ya product sales se kaafi zyada multiple milta hai, isliye blended figure recurring part ki value badha deta hai aur baaki ki ghatta deta hai. Kisi bhi hisse par multiple lagane se pehle dono alag karo.
  • High multiple ko achhi khabar maan lena. 5% margin wali business par 10x revenue ka price, 30% margin wali par 3x se kharab hai; multiple utna hi achha jitna uske peeche margin hai.
  • Galat barah mahine chunna. Buyer ka matlab pichhle barah mahine ya agle barah hota hai, aur tez badhti business me dono me ek-tihai ka farak ho sakta hai. Multiple usi ke saath hilta hai jo aap chunte ho, isliye do offers compare karne se pehle period fix karo.
