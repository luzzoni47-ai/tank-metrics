What this calculator is for

CPC, CPL, CPA & Conversion Rate measures the economics of a single customer. See your cost at every step of the marketing funnel — click, lead, and sale. These numbers decide whether growth makes money or burns it, and they are the first thing a serious investor checks.

How the maths works

The cost to acquire a customer (CAC) is everything you spend on sales and marketing divided by the customers it won. Lifetime value (LTV) is what one customer brings in over the whole relationship — average order value, times how often they buy, times how many years they stay, times your gross margin. Divide LTV by CAC and you have the ratio. Around 3:1 is the common rule of thumb; below 1:1 you lose money on every customer you win.

When to reach for it

  • You are about to increase your ad budget and want to know if it will pay back.
  • A channel is winning customers cheaply and you want to know if that will last.
  • You are preparing a pitch and need the unit-economics slide.

Common mistakes

  • Using revenue for LTV instead of gross margin. The customer brings in sales, not profit.
  • Counting only ad spend as CAC and leaving out salaries, tools and agency fees.
  • Assuming a customer stays for years when your data shows most leave after a few months.
