What this calculator is for

Compound Interest settles what a lump sum actually becomes once the interest it throws off is left in to earn interest of its own. You enter the principal, the rate, the years, and — the field that decides the answer — how many times a year the interest is added back (n). ₹10,000 at 8% over five years with n = 1 becomes ₹14,693.28; set n = 12 and the same headline 8% becomes ₹14,898.46. The rate never moved; only the compounding frequency did. That is the decision this page is built around: not “what is 8% of ₹10,000”, but “what does ₹10,000 grow to when the interest is reinvested this often”. It is the compound twin of Simple Interest, and the reason to keep both is the gap between them. Over a single year the two agree almost to the rupee; over thirty years, on the same principal and the same rate, this figure can be more than triple the simple one. So this is the long-horizon tool — what a fixed deposit rolls up to, what a lump sum left untouched turns into, and what a multi-year loan really costs once unpaid interest starts earning interest too. If the term is short and nothing is reinvested, Simple Interest is the honest tool; the moment interest is left in, this one is.

How the maths works

With p the Amount (Principal), r the Interest Rate in per cent per year, t the Time in years and n the Times compounded per year, the balance is p × (1 + r ÷ 100 ÷ n) ^ (n × t). The Interest Earned line is that total minus p. n is the only field that changes the shape of the curve rather than its scale: n = 1 adds the interest once a year, n = 4 quarterly, n = 12 monthly. For p = 10,000, r = 8, t = 5 and n = 1 the total is 10,000 × 1.08^5 = ₹14,693.28, so Interest Earned is ₹4,693.28; the same figures with n = 12 give ₹14,898.46. Leaving n blank, or at 0, is read as 1 — annual compounding.

When to reach for it

  • You are choosing between two fixed deposits that quote the same rate but compound at different frequencies, and you want to see in rupees whether quarterly really beats annual.
  • You are leaving a lump sum untouched for a long horizon — a child’s education corpus, a retirement amount — and want the rolled-up figure rather than the simple-interest one.
  • You are working out the true cost of a multi-year loan or a credit-card balance where unpaid interest starts earning interest, and the headline rate understates what you will repay.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the “Times compounded per year” field as decoration and leaving it at 1 on a deposit that compounds monthly. Over five years at 8% that quietly drops about ₹205 off the answer, and the understatement grows with every extra year.
  • Entering a monthly rate (say 0.67%) in the Interest Rate field while also setting n = 12. The rate is annual, and the ÷ n inside the formula already turns it into a period rate — doing both double-counts and overstates the total.
  • Comparing a compound total with a simple-interest quote without checking the term first. At one year the two are within a few rupees and the comparison looks like a wash; at twenty years the same numbers differ by more than the entire original principal.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Compound Interest ye sawaal nipta deta hai ki ek lump sum actually kitna ban jaata hai jab uska interest hi aage interest kamane lagta hai. Principal, rate, saal, aur — sabse zaroori field — saal me kitni baar interest wapas jama hota hai (n) daal do. ₹10,000 par 8% aur paanch saal me n = 1 par ₹14,693.28 milta hai; wahi 8% par n = 12 kar do to ₹14,898.46. Rate bilkul nahi badla, sirf compounding ki frequency badli. Yahi is page ka poora decision hai: “8% of ₹10,000 kitna hai” nahi, balki “₹10,000 is frequency par reinvest hone ke baad kitna banta hai”. Ye Simple Interest ka compound jodidaar hai, aur dono rakhne ka poora point inke beech ka gap hai. Ek saal me dono lagbhag rupee-rupee barabar rehte hain; tees saal me usi principal aur usi rate par ye figure simple se teen guna se bhi zyada ho sakta hai. Isliye ye lambi horizon ka tool hai — FD roll ho kar kitni banti hai, chhoda hua lump sum kitna banta hai, aur multi-year loan ki asli cost kya hai jab bina chukaya interest khud interest kamane lagta hai. Agar term chhoti hai aur kuch reinvest nahi hota, to Simple Interest hi imaandar tool hai; jaise hi interest andar chhod rahe ho, ye.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

p = Amount (Principal), r = Interest Rate (per cent per year), t = Time (years) aur n = Times compounded per year lete hue balance = p × (1 + r ÷ 100 ÷ n) ^ (n × t). Interest Earned line is total me se p ghata kar aati hai. n hi wo field hai jo curve ka shape badalta hai, sirf scale nahi: n = 1 par saal me ek baar, n = 4 par quarterly, n = 12 par monthly. p = 10,000, r = 8, t = 5 aur n = 1 par total 10,000 × 1.08^5 = ₹14,693.28, yaani Interest Earned ₹4,693.28; wahi numbers n = 12 par ₹14,898.46 dete hain. n khaali ya 0 chhodne par use 1 maana jaata hai, yaani annual compounding.

Kab use karein

  • Do FD ke beech choose kar rahe ho jo same rate quote karte hain par alag frequency par compound karte hain, aur dekhna hai quarterly annual ko rupaye me sach me haraata hai ya nahi.
  • Ek lump sum lambi horizon ke liye chhod rahe ho — bachche ki education corpus, retirement ka amount — aur simple-interest ke bajaye rolled-up figure chahiye.
  • Multi-year loan ya credit-card balance ki asli cost nikal rahe ho jahan bina chukaya interest khud interest kamata hai, aur headline rate asli repayment se kam dikhata hai.

Aam galtiyan

  • “Times compounded per year” field ko decoration samajh kar monthly-compounding deposit par bhi 1 chhod dena. Paanch saal aur 8% par ye chupchaap lagbhag ₹205 kam kar deta hai, aur saal badhne ke saath understatement badhta jaata hai.
  • Interest Rate field me monthly rate (jaise 0.67%) daalna aur saath me n = 12 set karna. Rate annual hai; formula ke andar ka ÷ n khud use period rate bana deta hai, isliye dono karne se double-count hota hai aur total zyada dikhta hai.
  • Term dekhe bina compound total ko simple-interest quote se compare karna. Ek saal par dono me kuch rupaye ka farak hota hai aur comparison barabar lagta hai; bees saal me wahi numbers poore original principal se bhi zyada alag ho jaate hain.
