What this calculator is for

The Net Profit Margin Calculator turns two figures — revenue and net profit — into the one percentage that says how much of every rupee of sales the business actually keeps. Net profit is the last line: after the cost of goods, after salaries and rent, after interest on any loan, and after tax. So this is the smallest and the most honest of the margin figures.

How the maths works

Net margin is net profit divided by revenue, multiplied by 100: `np` ÷ `rev` × 100. If a business earns ₹4,50,000 of revenue and keeps ₹1,80,000 after every cost and tax, the net margin is 1,80,000 ÷ 4,50,000 = 40%. Net profit is what remains after cost of goods, operating expenses, interest and tax; subtract any one of those later and the percentage rises.

When to reach for it

  • You want to know how much of each rupee of sales the business keeps after everything is paid.
  • You are comparing your margin with others selling the same kind of product or service.
  • You are preparing accounts or a pitch and need the bottom-line percentage investors will ask for.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing the margin against a business in another industry. A grocery chain at 3% can be healthy while a software company at 3% is in trouble; the fair comparison is within the same trade.
  • Using profit before tax and calling it net. Net means after tax and after interest, which is why it is lower than the operating margin on the same revenue.
  • Reading a high percentage on a small revenue as a strong business. 40% of ₹4,50,000 is ₹1,80,000 — a large margin on a tiny turnover is still a small profit.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Net Profit Margin Calculator do figure — revenue aur net profit — ko us ek percentage me badal deta hai jo batata hai ki sales ke har rupaye me se business asli me kitna rakhta hai. Net profit last line hai: cost of goods ke baad, salary aur rent ke baad, loan ke interest ke baad, aur tax ke baad. Isliye ye teen margin numbers me sabse chhota aur sabse sachcha hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Net margin = net profit ÷ revenue × 100 (`np` ÷ `rev` × 100). Agar business ₹4,50,000 revenue kamaye aur har cost aur tax ke baad ₹1,80,000 bachaye, to net margin 1,80,000 ÷ 4,50,000 = 40% hai. Net profit wo bachta hai jo cost of goods, operating expenses, interest aur tax ke baad rehta hai; inme se koi bhi kam ho to percentage badh jaata hai.

Kab use karein

  • Aap jaanna chahein ki sales ke har rupaye me se sab kuch dene ke baad business kitna rakhta hai.
  • Apne margin ko usi tarah ka product ya service bechne walon se compare kar rahe ho.
  • Accounts ya pitch taiyaar kar rahe ho aur bottom-line percentage chahiye jo investor poochta hai.

Aam galtiyan

  • Margin ko doosri industry ke business se compare karna. Grocery chain ka 3% theek ho sakta hai jabki software company ka 3% mushkil me hai; sahi comparison apne hi trade me hai.
  • Profit before tax ko net keh dena. Net matlab tax aur interest ke baad, isliye wahi revenue par operating margin se kam hota hai.
  • Chhoti revenue par bada percentage dekh kar business ko strong maan lena. ₹4,50,000 ka 40% matlab ₹1,80,000 — chhoti turnover par bada margin bhi chhota profit hi hai.
