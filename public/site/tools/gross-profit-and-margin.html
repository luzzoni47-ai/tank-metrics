What this calculator is for

Gross Profit & Margin answers the question a trading month asks in the middle rather than at the end: on the goods that actually left the shop, did the business make money, or did it just move stock from a supplier to a customer? rev is everything invoiced in the period, net of returns and of GST. cogs is what those same goods cost to get sold — the supplier bill, freight in, packaging, the marketplace's commission, the payment gateway's cut and a provision for what comes back. The tool returns Gross Profit in rupees and Gross Margin as a percentage of revenue, and it is deliberately the first line of the profit-and-loss statement, ahead of rent, salaries, ads and tax. Use the rupees to see whether the month produced anything at all, and the percentage to see whether the business can carry its own overheads: ₹2,70,000 of gross profit sounds strong until you notice it is 60% of ₹4,50,000 in sales, and every salary and every ad has to fit inside that 60%. Kept at the level of one product it becomes a pricing tool; averaged across the whole catalogue it hides the item that is quietly sold at a loss to keep the till busy. This is also where markup is confused with margin — bought at ₹600, sold at ₹1,000 is a 40% margin on the price but a 67% markup on the cost, and quoting the wrong one to a marketplace moves the price by hundreds of rupees a unit.

How the maths works

Gross Profit = rev − cogs. Gross Margin = (rev − cogs) ÷ rev × 100. The tool takes rev and cogs and returns Gross Profit in rupees and Gross Margin as a percentage. With rev ₹4,50,000 and cogs ₹1,80,000, gross profit is ₹2,70,000 and the margin is 2,70,000 ÷ 4,50,000 = 60%. Run it the other way and a target margin sets the price: divide the landed cost by (1 − margin), so a 40% margin on ₹600 needs 600 ÷ 0.6 = ₹1,000. A 10% discount then takes revenue to ₹900 and the margin to (900 − 600) ÷ 900 = 33% — a tenth off the price costs a quarter of the margin, which is why discount calls belong here and not in a gut feel.

When to reach for it

  • You are pricing a new product and need to know whether the margin survives the real landed cost — freight, commission and returns included — rather than the supplier's list price.
  • A month was busy but the bank balance barely moved, and you want to know whether the leak is in the goods or further down the profit-and-loss statement.
  • Two suppliers or two products are on the table and you have to decide which one deserves the next rupee of effort, not merely which one looks cheaper per kilo.

Common mistakes

  • Reading gross profit as profit. Rent, salaries, ads, interest and tax have not been paid out of it yet; the gross margin is the pool they all drink from, not the money the owner keeps.
  • Leaving the sale's own direct costs outside cogs. Courier, packaging, marketplace commission and gateway fees vanish when a sale does not happen, so they belong in cogs — excluded, the margin is flattered by several points.
  • Comparing the percentage with a business that is not yours. 8-15% is ordinary for a kirana, 40-55% for apparel and 70-90% for software; a 60% margin means nothing until it stands beside its own industry.
  • Averaging the whole catalogue into one figure. A healthy company-level margin can be hiding a best-seller that is subsidising a product which loses money on every unit sold.

Ye calculator kis kaam ka hai

Gross Profit & Margin wo sawaal poochta hai jo trading mahina beech mein poochta hai, aakhir mein nahi: jo saaman asli mein dukaan se nikla, us par business ne paisa banaya ya sirf stock supplier se customer tak pahunchaya? rev period ka poora invoice hai, returns aur GST nikaal kar. cogs wahi saaman bechne layak banane ka kharcha hai — supplier ka bill, freight in, packing, marketplace ka commission, payment gateway ka cut aur wapas aane wale saaman ka provision. Tool Gross Profit rupees mein aur Gross Margin revenue ke percentage mein deta hai, aur ye jaan-boojh kar profit-and-loss ki pehli line hai — rent, salary, ads aur tax se pehle. Rupees se dekho ki mahine mein kuch bana ya nahi, aur percentage se dekho ki business apne overheads utha sakta hai ya nahi: ₹2,70,000 gross profit strong lagta hai jab tak dikh na jaye ki wo ₹4,50,000 ki sales ka 60% hai, aur har salary aur ad usi 60% ke andar aani hai. Ek product ke level par rakho to ye pricing tool ban jaata hai; poore catalogue ka average banao to wo item chhup jaata hai jo till chalta rahe isliye chupchap loss par bik raha hai. Yahin markup aur margin bhi mix hote hain — ₹600 mein kharida, ₹1,000 mein becha price ka 40% margin hai par cost ka 67% markup, aur marketplace ko galat wala bata diya to price per unit sau rupaye hil jaata hai.

Ganit kaise chalta hai

Us par 10% discount revenue ko ₹900 karta hai aur margin (900 − 600) ÷ 900 = 33% — price ka dasva hissa margin ka chauthai kha jaata hai, isliye discount ka faisla yahan lena chahiye, gut feel par nahi.

Kab use karein

  • Naya product price kar rahe ho aur dekhna hai ki margin asli landed cost — freight, commission aur returns ke saath — par bachta hai ya nahi, supplier ke list price par nahi.
  • Mahina busy tha par bank balance hila nahi, aur jaanna hai leak maal mein hai ya profit-and-loss ke neeche.
  • Do suppliers ya do products saamne hain aur tay karna hai agli rupaye ki mehnat kaun deserve karta hai, sirf kaun per kilo sasta dikhta hai wo nahi.

Aam galtiyan

  • Gross profit ko profit samajhna. Rent, salary, ads, interest aur tax abhi isme se nahi gaye; gross margin wo pool hai jisme se sab peete hain, wo paisa nahi jo owner rakhta hai.
  • Sale ki apni direct cost cogs se bahar rakhna. Courier, packing, marketplace commission aur gateway fee sale na hone par nahi lagti, isliye wo cogs mein aati hain; bahar rakhne se margin kai point zyada dikhta hai.
  • Apne percentage ko doosre business se compare karna. Kirana ke liye 8-15% normal hai, apparel ke liye 40-55% aur software ke liye 70-90%; 60% ka koi matlab nahi jab tak apni industry ke saath na rakha jaye.
  • Poore catalogue ko ek figure mein average kar dena. Healthy company-level margin us best-seller ko chhupa sakta hai jo aise product ko subsidise kar raha hai jo har unit par paisa khota hai.
