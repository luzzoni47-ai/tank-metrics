Growth figures are only useful next to a cash figure. A business can grow quickly and still run out of money, which is what the runway number is there to catch.

What this calculator is for

Working Capital tracks the two things that kill young businesses: running out of cash, and growing faster than the cash can follow. How much cash you actually have available to run day-to-day operations. Growth is a headline; cash is the oxygen.

How the maths works

Burn is the cash a business spends each month beyond what it earns. Runway is the cash you hold divided by that monthly burn — the number of months before the money is gone. Run rate takes one period's revenue and scales it to a year, which is useful for comparison but assumes the period repeats. Break-even is the sales level at which contribution covers your fixed costs, so profit is zero and every extra sale is profit.

When to reach for it

  • You want to know how many months you have before you must raise or cut.
  • You are deciding between hiring now and waiting a quarter.
  • A month was unusually strong and you want to compare it fairly with the rest of the year.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring runway from revenue instead of cash in the bank.
  • Treating run rate as a forecast. It is a snapshot, not a promise.
  • Cutting the spending that wins customers first. That shortens runway now and shrinks the business later.
