Wine Shop Profit Margin: What You Keep

A thin margin on every bottle — here is what the shop actually keeps.

Profit & MarginsNovember 14, 2026

A wine shop sells at a fixed MRP and buys at a trade price. The gap between the two is the only money the shop keeps, and it is thinner than most people guess. This guide works through one bottle, the monthly costs a shop carries, and the number of bottles it must sell before it earns anything.

Every figure below is small and round on purpose. A single bottle is easy to check, and the same arithmetic works whether you sell 60 bottles a month or 600.

A wine shop earns two margins, not one

The first margin is on the bottle: the gap between what the customer pays and what the shop paid the distributor. The second is what is left of that gap after the rent, the electricity and the staff are paid. A shop can look busy on the first and still lose money on the second.

A ₹600 bottle, worked through

Take a bottle that sells at ₹600. The shop bought it for ₹480. The sale earns ₹120 before any of the shop's own costs are counted.

  1. Selling price: ₹600
  2. Cost of the bottle: ₹480
  3. Gross profit: ₹600 − ₹480 = ₹120
  4. Gross margin: ₹120 ÷ ₹600 = 20%

That ₹120 is the whole of what the shop keeps from the sale. The ₹480 was never the shop's money — it went to the distributor the day the stock was bought.

The fixed costs that arrive every month

The rent, the electricity and the staff come whether the shop sells one bottle or none. A small wine shop's month might look like this.

  • Shop rent: ₹7,000
  • Electricity and water: ₹1,000
  • Helper's salary: ₹1,000
  • Total fixed cost per month: ₹7,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹1,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 a month is the hurdle. Until the sales cover it, every rupee of gross profit is only paying the shop back, not the owner.

The break-even: how many bottles a month

Divide the fixed cost by the gross profit on one bottle and you get the break-even.

  1. Fixed cost per month: ₹9,000
  2. Gross profit per bottle: ₹120
  3. Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹120 = 75 bottles a month

Seventy-five bottles is about two and a half a day. Sell 80 and the month works: 80 × ₹120 = ₹9,600 of gross profit, and ₹9,600 − ₹9,000 leaves ₹600. Sell 70 instead — a quiet month, a wet week — and the same shop brings ₹8,400 of gross profit against ₹9,000 of fixed costs. That is a loss of ₹600, and the rent was still paid in full.

A slow month, and what it costs

Notice how small the cushion is. The shop earns ₹600 in a good month and loses ₹600 in a slow one — a swing of just ten bottles. The fixed cost does not move with sales, so a small drop in bottles goes straight to the bottom line.

Three things that quietly cut the margin

  1. Breakage and shrinkage. A bottle that falls or walks off the shelf is ₹120 of profit gone, not ₹600 — you still paid ₹480 for it.
  2. Licence and renewal costs. The annual fee and any renewal charges are real, and they are fixed. Spread them across the year and add them to the monthly hurdle.
  3. Selling on credit to regulars. A customer who pays next week ties up cash you have already spent on stock, and a written-off bill takes ten bottles of profit to recover.

Wine shop do margin kamaate hai, ek nahi

Pehla margin botal par hai: graahak jo deta hai aur dukaan ne distributor ko jo diya, unaka antar. Doosara woh hai jo kiraya, bijli aur salary nikalne ke baad bachta hai. Dukaan pehle mein vyast dikh sakate hai aur doosare mein ghaata kar sakate hai.

₹600 ki ek botal, pura hisaab

₹600 mein bikne wali ek botal lijiye. Dukaan ne use ₹480 mein khareeda tha. Bikri par dukaan ke apne kharch se pehle ₹120 banate hain.

  1. Bikri keemat: ₹600
  2. Botal ki lagat: ₹480
  3. Gross profit: ₹600 − ₹480 = ₹120
  4. Gross margin: ₹120 ÷ ₹600 = 20%

Woh ₹120 hi bikri se dukaan ki poore kamai hai. ₹480 kabhi dukaan ka paisa tha hi nahi — woh maal khareedane ke din distributor ko chala gaya.

Har mahine aane wale tay kharch

Kiraya, bijli aur staff har mahine aate hain, chaahe dukaan ek botal beche ya ek bhi nahi. Ek chhoti wine shop ka mahina aisa dikh sakta hai.

  • Dukaan ka kiraya: ₹7,000
  • Bijli aur paani: ₹1,000
  • Helper ki salary: ₹1,000
  • Mahine ka kul tay kharch: ₹7,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹1,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 mahine ki rukaavat hai. Jab tak bikri ise nahi nikaalate, har rupaya dukaan ko hi vaapas ja raha hai, maalik ko nahi.

Break-even: mahine mein kitni botalen

Tay kharch ko ek botal ke gross profit se bhaag deejie, break-even nikal aata hai.

  1. Mahine ka tay kharch: ₹9,000
  2. Ek botal par gross profit: ₹120
  3. Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹120 = mahine ki 75 botalen

Pachahattar botalen yani roz kareeb dhaaee. 80 bechen to mahina chal jaata hai: 80 × ₹120 = ₹9,600 gross profit, aur ₹9,600 − ₹9,000 se ₹600 bachate hain. Isake bajaay 70 bechen — shaant mahina, barasaat ka hafta — to wahi dukaan ₹8,400 gross profit par ₹9,000 tay kharch laate hai. Yeh ₹600 ka ghaata hai, aur kiraya pura dena pada.

Dheema mahina, aur uski keemat

Dekhie gadde kitni patale hai. Achhe mahine mein dukaan ₹600 kamaate hai aur dheeme mahine mein ₹600 khote hai — sirf das botalon ka farq. Tay kharch bikri ke saath nahi hilata, isliye botalon ki chhoti giraavat seedhe munafe par padti hai.

Teen cheezein jo chupchaap margin kaatate hain

  1. Toot-phoot aur chore. Gire ya gaayab huee botal par ₹120 ka munafa jaata hai, ₹600 ka nahi — ₹480 aapne phir bhi diye the.
  2. Licence aur naveeneekaran ka kharch. Saalana fees aur naveeneekaran asli hain, aur tay hain. Inhein saal bhar mein baantie aur mahine ki rukaavat mein jodie.
  3. Niyamit graahakon ko udhaar. Jo graahak agle hafte paisa deta hai, woh us nakad ko rokata hai jo aapne maal par kharch kar diya, aur dooba hua bil vasoolane mein das botalon ka munafa lagta hai.
