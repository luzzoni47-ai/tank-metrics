Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate profit margin in rupees?

Divide net profit by revenue and multiply by 100. ₹10 lakh of profit on ₹1 crore of revenue is (10,00,000 ÷ 1,00,00,000) × 100 = a 10% margin.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit divided by revenue; markup is profit divided by cost. A 25% margin is a 33% markup. Confusing the two is the most common mistake Indian founders make in a pitch.

Is a 10% profit margin good in India?

It depends on the business. A trading firm can survive on 5-10%; a D2C brand usually needs 20%+ to fund ads and returns. Compare against your own industry, not a generic number.

In Shark Tank India Season 2, a D2C brand walked in with ₹12 crore of revenue and walked out without a deal - because its margin was barely 4%. The sharks said the same thing every time: revenue is vanity, margin is sanity. A business that sells ₹1 crore and keeps ₹10 lakh is a very different animal from one that keeps ₹25 lakh on the same sales. This calculator turns your revenue and profit into the margin in rupees, so you know which animal you are.

Margin is profit divided by revenue. ₹1 crore of sales with ₹10 lakh of profit is a 10% margin. The same ₹1 crore with ₹25 lakh of profit is 25%. Revenue alone hides the difference; the margin exposes it.

Founders mix up margin and markup constantly. A 25% margin is a 33% markup on cost, and quoting the wrong one in a pitch is how a good business looks dishonest. This calculator shows both, so you never mix them again.

Formula

The formulaNet Profit Margin = (Net Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100

Net profit is what is left after every cost - goods, salaries, rent, ads, tax. ₹10 lakh of profit on ₹1 crore of revenue is a 10% margin. Markup is a different number: profit divided by cost, not by revenue.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

American margin tools report in dollars and talk about 'gross margin' in a US accounting sense that does not match how an Indian small business keeps its books. An Indian founder thinks in ₹ Lakhs, adds GST separately, and wants the margin on the number they actually bill.

In India a trader, a D2C brand and a services firm all measure margin differently - the trader on landed cost, the brand on cost of goods plus marketplace fees, the agency on billable hours. The one thing they share is that the answer has to be in rupees, not in a spreadsheet column labelled in dollars.

Indian lenders and investors ask for the margin on the last twelve months, not a single month. Keep feeding this calculator each month's revenue and profit, and you will have the number ready before anyone asks for it.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing margin with markup. A 25% margin is a 33% markup on cost; quoting the wrong one in a pitch makes a good business look dishonest.
  • Calculating margin on revenue that still includes GST. Strip the tax out first or the margin is understated.
  • Looking at one good month. Margin swings with season, discounts and one-off costs, so judge it on a rolling twelve months.
