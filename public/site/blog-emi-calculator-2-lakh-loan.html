Frequently asked questions

What is the EMI for a 2 lakh loan?

It depends on the rate and tenure. At 10% for 24 months the EMI is about ₹9,229; at 10% for 12 months it is about ₹17,584. Use the calculator above for your exact rate and tenure.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan, r the monthly rate and n the number of months. The same formula is used by every Indian bank.

Does a longer tenure reduce the total cost?

No. A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but raises the total interest you pay. On a ₹2 lakh loan at 10%, moving from 12 to 24 months roughly doubles the interest.

What is emi calculator 2 lakh loan?

An EMI - Equated Monthly Instalment - is a fixed amount you pay every month that covers both the interest on a loan and a part of the principal. It stays the same each month even though the split between interest and principal keeps shifting in favour of the principal as the loan runs down.

For a ₹2 lakh loan the EMI depends on two things: the annual interest rate and the tenure. At 10% over one year the EMI is about ₹17,584; stretch the same loan to two years and the EMI drops to ₹9,229, but the total interest almost doubles to about ₹21,500. A longer tenure means a smaller monthly bite and a bigger total bill.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

Indian lenders quote an annual reducing-balance rate, but many borrowers still compare offers on the flat rate, which understates the true cost. The EMI formula here uses the reducing-balance method that banks actually charge, so the total interest is the real one.

Indian loans often carry processing fees, insurance and GST on the fee, which are not part of the EMI but do raise the effective cost. Add those to the total payment to see what the loan truly costs.

A 2 lakh loan is small enough that the rate difference between a bank, an NBFC and a fintech app can be several percentage points. On a short tenure that difference is small; on a longer one it is a real amount of money, which is why comparing the total matters.

Common Mistakes

  • Comparing loans on the flat rate instead of the reducing-balance rate - the flat rate hides a much higher true cost.
  • Choosing the longest tenure just because the EMI is smallest, and paying far more interest in total.
  • Forgetting processing fees, insurance and GST, which raise the effective cost above the quoted rate.
