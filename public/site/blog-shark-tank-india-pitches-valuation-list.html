Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the valuation of a Shark Tank India pitch?

Divide the amount invested by the equity given, then multiply by one hundred. Skippi Ice Pops gave 15% for ₹1 crore, so the valuation was ₹6.67 crore, while Revamp Moto gave 1.5% for ₹1 crore, a ₹66.7 crore valuation.

Why do two ₹1 crore deals have such different valuations?

Because the valuation depends on the equity, not the cheque. A founder who gives 15% for ₹1 crore is valuing the company at ₹6.67 crore, while one who gives 1.5% is valuing it at about ₹66.7 crore.

What is shark tank india all pitches valuation list?

A Shark Tank India pitches valuation list is a running record of what each founder asked for, what the sharks finally agreed to, and what that deal says the company is worth. The valuation is almost never printed on screen, but it is easy to recover. Take the cheque, divide it by the equity handed over, and multiply by one hundred. That one line turns any pitch into a number you can line up against every other pitch on the show, across all three seasons.

The reason such a list is useful is that it shows how wide the range really is. On the same stage, in the same season, some founders gave 1.5% for ₹1 crore and others gave far more equity for a much smaller cheque. The cheque size on its own tells you very little. What matters is the equity attached to it, because that is the only figure that reveals the valuation the sharks accepted. Two deals can both be described as a one crore round and still sit forty times apart in value.

For an Indian founder preparing to pitch, the list works as a benchmark. You can see where your revenue, margin and growth would place you, then decide whether your own ask is reasonable or optimistic. Everything here stays in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores, the way Indian investors actually talk.

Formula

The formulaValuation = (Investment ÷ Equity) × 100

Investment in ₹ crore or lakh, and equity as a percentage of the company. The answer is the post-money valuation, the value after the cheque is in.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

A US valuation template counts in millions and billions, while India counts in lakh and crore, and the same company can look like a very different number depending on the unit. That unit gap is exactly where founders get confused when they copy a foreign calculator, and it is why every figure here is written in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores.

Indian equity rounds also come with more than a percentage. Along with the stake come board seats, veto rights, liquidation preference and sometimes a debt component, as Bamboo India's ₹30 lakh loan shows. A plain percentage-only tool hides all of that, and it is often the layer that decides whether a deal is genuinely good.

On the tax and compliance side, a fresh issue of shares is not a taxable event for the founder, but the valuation behind it may still need certification by a chartered accountant or a SEBI-registered merchant banker. So a number you quote publicly should be one you can defend on paper.

Common Mistakes

  • Reading a headline cheque as the valuation. ₹1 crore is not the value of the company, the equity attached to it is.
  • Mixing up pre-money and post-money. The valuation you get from the division is post-money, and pre-money is that figure minus the cheque.
  • Comparing your deal to a US pitch that was valued on a completely different method and a different currency.
