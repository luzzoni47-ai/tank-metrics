Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate break-even in rupees?

Divide your fixed costs by the contribution per unit, which is the selling price minus variable cost. Multiply the break-even units by the price to get break-even revenue in ₹.

What is contribution per unit?

It is the selling price minus the variable cost of one unit. A ₹45 product with a ₹15 cost contributes ₹30 toward covering fixed costs.

Why is break-even revenue useful?

It tells you the minimum turnover you must hit each month to avoid a loss. Anything below it is a loss and anything above it is profit.

Bamboo India walked into Shark Tank India selling a bamboo toothbrush at an MRP of ₹45 with a manufacturing cost of ₹15, so every unit carried ₹30 of gross profit. That single number is what makes break-even possible to work out: you divide your fixed monthly costs by the profit each unit brings in. Sharks ask 'kab tak profitable ho jaoge?' for exactly this reason. This free break even calculator works entirely in rupees, so you can see how many units and how many ₹ of sales you need before the business stops losing money.

What is break even calculator in rupees?

A break even calculator in rupees finds the exact sales level at which your total revenue equals your total costs, so profit is zero. Below that level you lose money, and above it every extra sale adds profit. The number you need is the contribution per unit, which is the selling price minus the variable cost of making or buying that unit. Divide your monthly fixed costs by the contribution and you have your break-even units.

The reason contribution matters more than price is that variable costs scale with every unit you sell. A ₹250 product with a ₹150 variable cost contributes ₹100 toward fixed costs, while a ₹45 product with a ₹15 cost contributes only ₹30. The cheaper product may feel easier to sell, but it takes far more volume to cover the same rent and salaries.

Indian small businesses often price by gut feel, comparing only to the neighbour's rate, and then wonder why the month ends in loss. Turning the price, the variable cost and the fixed cost into a single break-even number removes the guesswork, and it is the fastest way to see whether a discount actually makes sense. Run the same numbers again with a lower price and you can watch the break-even units climb, which is the clearest possible argument against a careless festive discount.

Formula

The formulaBreak-Even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price - Variable Cost)

Fixed costs, price and variable cost all in ₹. Selling price minus variable cost is the contribution each unit makes.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

American break-even tools assume clean per-unit costs, but an Indian seller usually has GST, marketplace commission and shipping folded into the price. Each of those behaves like a variable cost, so the honest contribution per unit is lower than the invoice suggests. Feed those into the calculation or your break-even will be optimistic.

Indian small businesses also carry fixed costs that a US template ignores, such as a shop deposit, a local licence fee or a monthly distributor retainer. These do not change with volume, which is exactly what makes them fixed costs, and they must be added to the monthly total before you divide.

Seasonality is a real factor in India. A festive quarter can carry a weak quarter, so a break-even that looks easy in October may be impossible in July. Running the same calculation for a slow month is the honest test of whether the business is genuinely viable.

Common Mistakes

  • Leaving GST and marketplace commission out of the variable cost, which makes the contribution per unit look larger and the break-even look easier than it is.
  • Treating a one-off cost as fixed forever. Rent or a licence fee that ends next quarter should not be spread across the whole year as a permanent fixed cost.
  • Forgetting that break-even is a monthly target, not a yearly one. A business that only breaks even over twelve months may still run out of cash in a weak month.
