How to Set Your Freelancer Hourly Rate

Your rate is a price, not a wage — here is how to set it.

Pricing & CostsNovember 17, 2026

Most freelancers set a rate by taking the income they want and dividing it by the hours they work. It feels fair. It is almost always too low, because it quietly pays you for hours you never get to bill. A freelance rate is not a wage. It is a price, and it has to carry your own costs, your unpaid time and your slow months.

This guide gives you the one-line formula, works a full example from ₹9,000 a month, and lists the costs to fold in before you quote your next client.

Why dividing by every hour gives a low rate

When you work for an employer, someone else pays for your tea breaks, your training and the weeks when the phone does not ring. When you work for yourself, all of that comes out of your own rate. The hours you sit at the desk and the hours a client actually pays for are two different numbers, and mixing them up is the single most common pricing mistake a freelancer makes.

The gap is bigger than most people expect. Email, quotes, invoices, learning a new tool and chasing payments are all real work. They are also all unbillable. A freelancer who bills 60% of their desk hours is doing well; a busy first year often sits closer to 40%.

The formula in one line

The honest rate is your target income divided by the hours you can actually bill, not the hours you work:

  • Billable hours = working hours a week × weeks a month × billable share
  • Hourly rate = target monthly income ÷ billable hours

Two inputs decide everything. The first is how many hours you really put in. The second, and the one people skip, is the share of those hours a client pays for. Get that share wrong and every quote that follows is wrong with it.

A worked example: ₹9,000 a month

Say you want to take home ₹9,000 a month, you work 25 hours a week, and a month is 4 weeks. That is 100 hours at the desk. The naive rate is ₹9,000 ÷ 100 = ₹90 an hour.

Now be honest about billable time. At a 60% billable share, only 60 of those hours earn. The same ₹9,000 spread over 60 hours is ₹150 an hour:

  • Target income: ₹9,000
  • Desk hours: 25 × 4 = 100
  • Billable share: 60%
  • Billable hours: 60
  • Naive rate: ₹9,000 ÷ 100 = ₹90
  • Honest rate: ₹9,000 ÷ 60 = ₹150

Nothing about the work changed. Only the number you divided by did, and it moved your price by two thirds.

Add your costs before you set the rate

The ₹150 above is what you take home, before the business pays for itself. Your software, internet, a laptop that wears out and a cushion for the months a client is late all have to come out of the same rate.

Suppose your tools and internet come to ₹3,000 a month. Spread over 60 billable hours, that is another ₹50 an hour:

  • Rate to take home ₹9,000: ₹150
  • Tools and internet: ₹3,000 ÷ 60 = ₹50
  • Rate you actually charge: ₹200

This is why two freelancers with the same skills can charge very different rates and still earn the same. The one with the higher rate is simply counting the costs the other is absorbing in silence.

A short checklist before you quote

Run these five checks before you send a number to a client:

  1. Start from the income you want to take home, not from a competitor's rate.
  2. Count billable hours, not desk hours.
  3. Add your tools, internet and software as a per-hour cost.
  4. Build in a small cushion for late payments and slow months.
  5. Round the final figure up, never down.

A rate is a decision you make once and defend many times. Set it from your own numbers, and you stop guessing every time a client asks what you charge.

Zyadatar freelancer apni ret aise tay karte hain: jo kamai chahiye, use kaam ke ghanton par bhaag de dete hain. Yeh theek lagta hai. Par yeh lagabhag hamesha kam hota hai, kyunki isamen aapko un ghanton ka bhi paisa mil jaata hai jo kabhi bil nahi hote. Freelaans ret salary nahi hai. Yeh ek keemat hai, aur ise aapki lagat, aapka bina-bil wala samay aur sust mahine sab uthaane padate hain.

Yeh guide ek laain ka formula dete hai, ₹9,000 mahine se pura hisaab karti hai, aur agli baar keemat bataane se pehle jodne wali laagaten ginaate hai.

Har ghante se bhaag dene par ret kam kyon banate hai

Jab aap kisi ke liye kaam karte hain, to aapki chaay ka brek, aapki trening aur ve hafte jab fon nahi bajata — yeh sab koi aur uthaata hai. Khud ke liye kaam karte samay yeh sab aapki hi ret se nikalta hai. Desk par baithe ghante aur ve ghante jinaka graahak sachamuch paisa deta hai, do alag sankhyaaen hain, aur inhein mila dena freelancer ki sabse aam keemat wali galat hai.

Yeh farq jitna lagta hai usse bada hota hai. Eemel, koteshan, invoice, naya tool seekhana aur paise ke peechhe bhaagana — yeh sab asli kaam hai. Aur yeh sab bina bil ka hai. Jo freelancer apne desk ghanton ka 60% bil kar paata hai woh achha kar raha hai; pehle vyast saal mein yeh aam taur par 40% ke aasapaas rehta hai.

Formula, ek laain mein

Imandar ret woh hai jo aapki lakshya kamai ko un ghanton par bhaag dete hai jo sachamuch bil ho sakte hain, na ki un ghanton par jo aap kaam karte hain:

  • Bil-yogy ghante = hafte ke kaam ke ghante × mahine ke hafte × bil-yogy hissa
  • Prati ghanta ret = lakshya maasik kamai ÷ bil-yogy ghante

Do cheezein sab tay karti hain. Pehli, aap asal mein kitne ghante dete hain. Doosare, aur yahi jo log chhod dete hain, un ghanton ka kitna hissa graahak ka paisa banata hai. Yeh hissa ghalat liya, to har agla koteshan bhi ghalat hoga.

Ek udaharan: ₹9,000 mahina

Maan lijiye aap ₹9,000 mahina kamaana chahte hain, hafte mein 25 ghante kaam karte hain, aur mahine mein 4 hafte hain. Yani desk par 100 ghante. Seedha ret banata hai ₹9,000 ÷ 100 = ₹90 prati ghanta.

Ab bil-yogy samay par imandar rahie. 60% bil-yogy hisse par in 100 mein se sirf 60 ghante kamaate hain. Wahi ₹9,000 ab 60 ghanton par baanten to ret ₹150 prati ghanta banata hai:

  • Lakshya kamai: ₹9,000
  • Desk ghante: 25 × 4 = 100
  • Bil-yogy hissa: 60%
  • Bil-yogy ghante: 60
  • Seedha ret: ₹9,000 ÷ 100 = ₹90
  • Imandar ret: ₹9,000 ÷ 60 = ₹150

Kaam mein kuch nahi badala. Sirf woh sankhya badale jis par aapne bhaag diya, aur usse aapki keemat do-tihaaee badh gayi.

Ret tay karne se pehle lagat jodie

Upar ka ₹150 woh hai jo aap ghar le jaate hain, isase pehle ki karobar apna kharch khud uthaae. Aapka software, intaranet, ghisata hua laipatop aur graahak ke der se paise dene ke liye ek gunjaish — yeh sab isi ret se nikalana hai.

Maan lijiye aapke tool aur intaranet mahine ke ₹3,000 padate hain. 60 bil-yogy ghanton par baanten to yeh ₹50 prati ghanta aur jud jaata hai:

  • ₹9,000 ghar le jaane ki ret: ₹150
  • Tool aur intaranet: ₹3,000 ÷ 60 = ₹50
  • Asal mein lee jaane wali ret: ₹200

Iseelie ek jaise hunar wale do freelancer bahut alag ret lekar bhi baraabar kama sakte hain. Zyada ret wala bas ve laagaten gin raha hai jo doosara chupchaap khud jhel raha hai.

Keemat bataane se pehle ek chhoti jaanch

Graahak ko koi aankada bhejane se pehle ye paanch baatein jaanchie:

  1. Shuruaat us kamai se keejie jo aap ghar le jaana chahte hain, kisi mukaabale ki ret se nahi.
  2. Desk ghante nahi, bil-yogy ghante ginie.
  3. Apne tool, intaranet aur software ko prati ghanta lagat ke roop mein jodie.
  4. Der se milne wale paise aur sust mahinon ke liye thode gunjaish rakhie.
  5. Aakhire aankada upar ki or pura keejie, neeche ki or kabhi nahi.

Ret ek faisala hai jo aap ek baar lete hain aur kai baar us par tike rehte hain. Ise apne aankadon se tay keejie, aur har baar graahak ke poochhane par andaza lagaana band ho jaaega.
