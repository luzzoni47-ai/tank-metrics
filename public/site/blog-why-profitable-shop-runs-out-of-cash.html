Why a Profitable Shop Still Runs Out of Cash

Profit is what the month earned. Cash is what you can actually spend — and growth widens the gap between them.

Profit & MarginsNovember 1, 2026

You can have a good month — customers bought, the till rang, and the profit column is positive — and still not have the money to pay the electricity bill. For a growing shop that is not bad luck. It is the ordinary shape of the problem.

The reason is a gap with a name: working capital. It is the money tied up in the business between the moment you pay for something and the moment a customer pays you for it. Growth widens that gap, which is why a shop can double its sales and still run out of cash.

Profit is not cash

Profit and cash answer two different questions. Profit asks: did the month earn more than it spent? Cash asks: is there money in the bank today?

An invoice you have raised but not been paid for is profit on paper and nothing in the bank. Stock on the shelf was paid for in cash and is profit to nobody until it sells. A shop can be profitable on paper every month and still be short of money every week.

The working-capital cycle, in five steps

Follow one rupee through a small shop and the gap appears on its own:

  1. You pay a supplier for stock — cash leaves.
  2. The stock sits on the shelf — cash is stuck.
  3. A customer buys and you raise an invoice — still no cash.
  4. The customer pays, often 30 days later — cash finally returns.
  5. You order the next, larger lot — and the cycle starts again, with more money needed.

The longer steps 2 and 4 take, the more cash you must carry. Growth makes step 5 bigger every time.

A worked example

Say a small shop starts the month with ₹5,000 in the bank. It buys stock for ₹4,000 in cash and sells it during the month for ₹6,000, on 30-day credit. Rent and power cost ₹1,000, paid at the start.

The month's profit is ₹6,000 − ₹4,000 − ₹1,000 = ₹1,000. A good month.

Now follow the cash. The ₹4,000 for stock and the ₹1,000 for rent leave at once, so the bank drops from ₹5,000 to ₹0. The sales are on credit, so nothing comes in for 30 days. For that month the shop is profitable and has no cash at all.

When the customer pays, ₹6,000 arrives. To grow, the shop wants to buy ₹8,000 of stock next month. Against a ₹6,000 bank balance it is ₹2,000 short — even though it made a profit.

The cash-conversion cycle

You can measure the gap in days. The cash-conversion cycle is how long your money stays locked up: the days stock sits before it sells, plus the days customers take to pay, minus the days your suppliers let you wait.

In the example, stock turns over in 30 days and customers pay in 30, while suppliers are paid on delivery (0 days). The cycle is 30 + 30 − 0 = 60 days. Sixty days is how long a rupee is unavailable after you spend it.

Three ways to close the gap

  • Get paid sooner. A small discount for payment within 7 days, or a UPI request the moment the bill is raised, shortens the customer's 30 days to almost nothing.
  • Pay later, within reason. Negotiating 15 or 30 days of supplier credit pushes the gap the other way, without costing you a rupee of margin.
  • Hold less stock. Ordering smaller lots more often keeps cash on the shelf only briefly instead of sitting in unsold boxes.

Each of these shortens the cycle by days, and days are cash.

What it means for your shop

Before you celebrate a profitable month, ask where the money is. If it is sitting in stock or in an unpaid invoice, the profit is real but not yet spendable. Plan the next, larger order against the bank balance, not against the profit figure.

A shop that understands its working-capital gap can grow on its own cash. A shop that ignores it grows straight into a shortage.

The short version

Profit is what the month earned. Cash is what you can spend. Working capital is the money trapped between paying for stock and being paid for it, and growth makes that trap bigger.

Measure the cycle in days, shorten it where you can, and match the size of your next order to the money actually in the bank.

Achha mahina raha ho sakta hai — graahak khareedate rahe, galla bajata raha, aur munafe ka kolam hara hai — aur phir bhi bijli ka bil bharane ke liye paisa na ho. Badhti dukaan ke liye yeh badakismate nahi hai. Yeh is samasya ka samanya roop hai.

Vajah ek naam wala antar hai: working capital. Yeh woh paisa hai jo karobar mein phansa rehta hai — jab aap kisi cheez ka bhugataan karte hain aur jab graahak aapko bhugataan karta hai, usake beech. Badhotri is antar ko chauda karti hai, iseelie dukaan bikri dugune karke bhi nakade se khaale ho sakate hai.

Munafa nakade nahi hai

Munafa aur nakade do alag savaalon ke jawab hain. Munafa poochhta hai: mahine ne kamaaya zyada ya kharch kiya zyada? Nakade poochhate hai: aaj bank mein paisa hai ya nahi?

Jo bil aapne banaaya par bhugataan nahi mila, woh kaagaz par munafa hai aur bank mein kuch nahi. Shelph par rakha maal nakade mein khareeda gaya aur bikne tak kisi ka munafa nahi. Dukaan har mahine kaagaz par munafe mein rah sakate hai aur har hafte paise ki tange mein bhi.

Working capital ka chakr, paanch kadamon mein

Ek rupaye ka peechha chhoti dukaan mein keejie aur antar khud dikh jaaega:

  1. Aap saplaayar ko maal ke paise dete hain — nakade baahar.
  2. Maal shelph par pada rehta hai — nakade atak jaati hai.
  3. Graahak khareedata hai aur aap bil banate hain — ab bhi nakade nahi.
  4. Graahak bhugataan karta hai, aksar 30 din baad — nakade aakhirakaar lautate hai.
  5. Aap agla, bada order dete hain — aur chakr phir shuru, zyada paise ki zaroorat ke saath.

Kadam 2 aur 4 jitne lambe, utni zyada nakade dhone padti hai. Badhotri har baar kadam 5 ko bada karti hai.

Ek udaharan

Maan lijiye ek chhoti dukaan mahine ki shuruaat mein bank mein ₹5,000 ke saath hai. Yeh ₹4,000 ka maal nakad mein khareedate hai aur mahine mein ₹6,000 mein bechti hai, 30 din ke udhaar par. Kiraya aur bijli ₹1,000, shuruaat mein bhare jaate hain.

Mahine ka munafa hai ₹6,000 − ₹4,000 − ₹1,000 = ₹1,000. Achha mahina.

Ab nakade ka peechha keejie. Maal ke ₹4,000 aur kiraae ke ₹1,000 turant nikal jaate hain, isliye bank ₹5,000 se ₹0 par aa jaata hai. Bikri udhaar par hai, isliye 30 din tak kuch nahi aata. Us mahine dukaan munafe mein hai aur usake paas nakade bilakul nahi.

Jab graahak bhugataan karta hai, ₹6,000 aate hain. Badhne ke liye dukaan agle mahine ₹8,000 ka maal khareedna chahti hai. ₹6,000 ke bank balance ke saamane ₹2,000 kam padate hain — haalaanki usane munafa kamaaya.

Cash-conversion cycle

Antar ko dinon mein naapa ja sakta hai. Cash-conversion cycle yani aapka paisa kitne din phansa rehta hai: maal bikne se pehle pade rahane ke din, jama graahak ke bhugataan ke din, ghata saplaayar ke diye intazaar ke din.

Udaharan mein maal 30 din mein bikta hai aur graahak 30 din mein bharate hain, jabaki saplaayar ko dileevare par bhugataan hota hai (0 din). Chakr hai 30 + 30 − 0 = 60 din. Saath din yani kharch karne ke baad ek rupaya itane samay tak upalabdh nahi.

Antar paatane ke teen tarike

  • Jaldi bhugataan len. 7 din mein bhugataan par thoda discount, ya bil banate hi UPI rikvest, graahak ke 30 din ko lagabhag shoony kar dete hai.
  • Der se bhugataan karein, had mein. 15 ya 30 din ka saplaayar kredit maangane se antar doosare taraf khisak jaata hai, bina margin ka ek rupaya ganvaae.
  • Kam maal rakhein. Chhote order baar-baar dene se nakade shelph par thode der rahate hai, bina bike dibbon mein nahi.

Inmein se har ek chakr ko dinon se chhota karta hai, aur din yani nakade.

Aapki dukaan ke liye isaka matlab

Munafe wale mahine ka jashn manaane se pehle poochhie ki paisa kahan hai. Agar woh maal mein ya bina bhare bil mein pada hai, to munafa asli hai par abhe kharch karne layak nahi. Agla, bada order bank balance ke hisaab se tay keejie, munafe ke aankade ke hisaab se nahi.

Jo dukaan apna working-capital antar samajhate hai, woh apni nakade par badh sakate hai. Jo ise nazarandaz karti hai, woh seedhe kame mein badhti hai.

Chhoti baat

Munafa woh hai jo mahine ne kamaaya. Nakade woh hai jo aap kharch kar sakte hain. Working capital woh paisa hai jo maal ke bhugataan aur usake bhugataan milne ke beech phansa hai, aur badhotri us phansaav ko bada karti hai.

Chakr ko dinon mein naapie, jahaan ho sake chhota keejie, aur agle order ka aakaar bank mein sachamuch maujood paise se milaaie.
