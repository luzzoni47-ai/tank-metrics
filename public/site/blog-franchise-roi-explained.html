Franchise ROI: What a Store Really Earns

What a franchise outlet keeps each month, what it costs to open, and how long until you get your money back.

Profit & MarginsOctober 29, 2026

A franchise is not a shop you open and then count the profit on. It is a shop you buy the right to open, and the money you pay for that right is part of the cost. That is the whole reason a franchise needs its own return figure — a plain profit margin tells you what the counter earns, but not whether the money you put in was worth it.

This guide works one small franchise outlet through the full calculation: what it keeps in a month, what it cost to open, the payback period, and the return on the money invested. Every figure is in rupees and every one reconciles with the next.

Franchise ROI is two numbers, not one

The first number is the monthly profit — what the outlet keeps after every cost. The second is the investment — the money you put in on day one to open it. Neither is interesting alone. An outlet that earns ₹600 a month is excellent if it cost ₹3,000 to open, and poor if it cost ₹9,000.

ROI puts the two together: the yearly profit divided by the investment. Payback does the same job from the other side: how many months the profit takes to return the investment. They are the same fact stated two ways.

What the outlet earns in a month

Say the outlet sells ₹9,000 of goods in a month. The goods cost ₹5,400 to buy, so the gross profit is ₹3,600 — a 40% gross margin.

  • Monthly sales: ₹9,000
  • Cost of the goods sold: ₹5,400
  • Gross profit: ₹9,000 − ₹5,400 = ₹3,600
  • Gross margin: ₹3,600 ÷ ₹9,000 = 40%

That ₹3,600 is real, but none of it is yours yet. The outlet's own monthly costs still have to come out of it:

  • Rent: ₹1,500
  • Staff salary: ₹1,000
  • Electricity and other running costs: ₹500
  • Total monthly cost: ₹1,500 + ₹1,000 + ₹500 = ₹3,000

So the outlet keeps ₹3,600 − ₹3,000 = ₹600 a month. That ₹600 is the number the whole ROI calculation rests on.

What it cost to open

Opening the outlet was not free, and this is the number owners most often forget. The investment is everything spent before the shop sold its first rupee:

  • Franchise fee paid to the brand: ₹2,000
  • Deposit on the shop: ₹1,000
  • Fittings, signage and equipment: ₹1,500
  • Opening stock: ₹1,500
  • Total money in: ₹2,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹1,500 + ₹1,500 = ₹6,000

Only ₹6,000, which sounds small — but it is the whole basis of the return. The outlet earns ₹600 a month on ₹6,000 of investment.

The payback period

Payback is the investment divided by the monthly profit:

  1. Money in: ₹6,000
  2. Profit a month: ₹600
  3. Payback: ₹6,000 ÷ ₹600 = 10 months

Ten months is the answer the owner actually feels, and it is why the calculation matters: the outlet's first months of profit are not profit at all. The shop is paying you back.

The return on investment

ROI states the same fact as a yearly percentage. Twelve months of ₹600 is ₹7,200 of profit on a ₹6,000 investment:

  1. Yearly profit: ₹600 × 12 = ₹7,200
  2. ROI: ₹7,200 ÷ ₹6,000 = 1.2, or 120%

Read the 120% next to the ten-month payback and the two agree: money that comes back in ten months earns about 120% in a year. When a franchise pitch quotes one of these numbers and not the other, the missing one is usually the unflattering one.

Where the numbers really break

  1. The royalty is not in the ₹600. Most franchises charge a monthly royalty, often a share of sales. At 5% of ₹9,000 that is ₹450 a month, and it comes straight off the ₹600 — the profit falls to ₹150 and the payback stretches past three years.
  2. The owner's own salary is missing. If the owner runs the counter and takes nothing, the ₹600 looks like profit. Pay a real salary out of it and the return disappears.
  3. Opening stock is not a cost that disappears. It is money sitting on a shelf. If it sells slowly, the cash is not back yet even though the books show profit.
  4. Month one is never month twelve. Sales ramp up. A payback calculated on the best month will always look better than the one the owner lives through.

The short version

Franchise ROI is the monthly profit divided into the money it took to open. Here the outlet keeps ₹600 a month on ₹6,000 of investment: a ten-month payback and a yearly return of 120%. Add the royalty and the owner's salary and both figures soften, which is exactly why a franchise pitch quotes sales and not this number. Franchise woh dukaan nahi hai jise kholakar aap sirf profit ginate hain. Yeh woh dukaan hai jise kholane ka haq aap khareedate hain, aur us haq ki jo keemat dee jaati hai woh bhi lagat ka hissa hai. Isi vajah se franchise ko apna return figure chahiye — saada profit margin batata hai ki counter kitna kamata hai, par yeh nahi ki aapka lagaaya paisa vasool hua ya nahi.

Yeh guide ek chhoti franchise outlet ka pura hisaab dikhati hai: mahine mein kitna bachta hai, kholane mein kitna laga, payback period kya hai, aur lagaae paise par return kitna hai. Har aankda rupees mein hai aur har aankda agle se mel khaata hai.

Franchise ROI do aankde hain, ek nahi

Pehla aankda hai mahine ka profit — har kharch ke baad outlet jo bachaata hai. Doosara hai investment — pehle din kholane mein lagaaya gaya paisa. Akele mein koi bhi aankda kaam ka nahi. Jo outlet mahine ke ₹600 kamata hai woh shaanadaar hai agar kholane mein ₹3,000 lage, aur kharaab hai agar ₹9,000 lage.

ROI dono ko jodata hai: saal ka profit bhaag investment. Payback wahi baat doosare taraf se kahta hai: profit ko investment vaapas karne mein kitne mahine lagte hain. Dono ek hi sach ke do roop hain.

Outlet mahine mein kitna kamata hai

Maan lijiye outlet mahine ke ₹9,000 ka maal bechata hai. Maal khareedane mein ₹5,400 lage, to gross profit ₹3,600 hua — 40% ka gross margin.

  • Mahine ki bikri: ₹9,000
  • Beche gaye maal ki lagat: ₹5,400
  • Gross profit: ₹9,000 − ₹5,400 = ₹3,600
  • Gross margin: ₹3,600 ÷ ₹9,000 = 40%

Woh ₹3,600 asli hai, par abhe aapka nahi hai. Outlet ke apne mahine ke kharch bhi usamen se nikalane hain:

  • Kiraya: ₹1,500
  • Staff ki salary: ₹1,000
  • Bijli aur baaki running kharch: ₹500
  • Kul mahine ka kharch: ₹1,500 + ₹1,000 + ₹500 = ₹3,000

Yani outlet bachaata hai ₹3,600 − ₹3,000 = ₹600 mahine. Wahi ₹600 poore ROI hisaab ki neenv hai.

Kholane mein kitna laga

Outlet kholana muft nahi tha, aur yahi woh aankda hai jo maalik sabse zyada bhoolate hain. Investment woh sab hai jo dukaan ke pehle rupaye ki kamai se pehle kharch hua:

  • Brand ko dee franchise fee: ₹2,000
  • Dukaan ki deposit: ₹1,000
  • Fittings, signage aur equipment: ₹1,500
  • Shuruaati stock: ₹1,500
  • Kul laga paisa: ₹2,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹1,500 + ₹1,500 = ₹6,000

Sirf ₹6,000, jo kam lagta hai — par pura return isi par tika hai. Outlet ₹6,000 ke investment par mahine ke ₹600 kamata hai.

Payback period

Payback yani investment bhaag mahine ka profit:

  1. Laga paisa: ₹6,000
  2. Mahine ka profit: ₹600
  3. Payback: ₹6,000 ÷ ₹600 = 10 mahine

Das mahine wahi jawab hai jo maalik asal mein mahasoos karta hai, aur iseelie yeh hisaab zaroore hai: outlet ke shuruaati mahinon ka profit profit nahi hota. Dukaan aapko paisa vaapas kar rahi hoti hai.

Return on investment

ROI wahi baat saal ke pratishat mein kahta hai. ₹600 ke baarah mahine yani ₹7,200 ka profit, ₹6,000 ke investment par:

  1. Saal ka profit: ₹600 × 12 = ₹7,200
  2. ROI: ₹7,200 ÷ ₹6,000 = 1.2, yani 120%

120% ko das mahine ke payback ke saath padhie to dono mel khaate hain: jo paisa das mahine mein vaapas aata hai woh saal mein lagabhag 120% kamata hai. Jab koi franchise pitch inmein se ek aankda batata hai aur doosara nahi, to jo chhoota hai woh aam taur par kam achha wala hota hai.

Asal mein aankde kahan tootate hain

  1. Royalty us ₹600 mein nahi hai. Zyadatar franchises mahine ki royalty lete hain, aksar bikri ka ek hissa. ₹9,000 ke 5% yani ₹450 mahine, aur woh seedhe ₹600 se jaata hai — profit ₹150 rah jaata hai aur payback teen saal se upar khinch jaata hai.
  2. Maalik ki apni salary gaayab hai. Agar maalik khud counter chalaata hai aur kuch nahi leta, to ₹600 profit jaisa dikhta hai. Usamen se asli salary deejie to return gaayab ho jaata hai.
  3. Shuruaati stock woh lagat nahi jo gaayab ho jaye. Woh shelf par pada paisa hai. Dheere bike to cash abhe vaapas nahi aaya, chaahe kitaaben profit dikhayein.
  4. Pehla mahina kabhi baarahavaan nahi hota. Bikri dheere-dheere badhti hai. Sabse achhe mahine par nikaala payback hamesha usse behtar dikhega jo maalik asal mein jeeta hai.

Chhoti baat

Franchise ROI yani mahine ka profit, kholane mein lage paise par. Yahaan outlet ₹6,000 ke investment par mahine ke ₹600 bachaata hai: das mahine ka payback aur saal ka 120% return. Royalty aur maalik ki salary jodie to dono aankde naram pad jaate hain, aur iseelie franchise pitch sales batata hai, yeh aankda nahi. Tareeqa aapke paas rehta hai — apni bikri, kharch aur investment daalie aur calculator aapke outlet ka sach bataaega, is udaharan ka nahi.
