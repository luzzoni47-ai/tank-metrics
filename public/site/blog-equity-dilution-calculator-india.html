Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate equity dilution in India?

Add the investor's equity and the ESOP pool, subtract that total from 100, and apply the remainder to your current stake. A 20% round plus a 10% pool leaves a founder who owned 100% with 70%.

Does the ESOP pool dilute the founder?

Yes. In most Indian term sheets the ESOP pool is carved out of the founders' stake rather than the investor's, so the founder's percentage falls by the pool size as well as by the new investment.

How much equity do Indian angel investors take?

Angel rounds in India commonly take 10% to 20% for a seed cheque, with a board seat or information rights added on top of the headline percentage.

In Shark Tank India, a founder who gives 20% to a shark and then sets aside a 10% ESOP pool for future hires does not keep 80% of the company, they keep 70%. Dilution stacks, and every round takes another slice from the same table. The well known Jugaadu Kamlesh deal of ₹10 lakh for 5% equity valued his company at ₹2 crore, and the same stake arithmetic decides how much of any company the founders still own after the tank. What is equity dilution calculator india?

An equity dilution calculator for India shows what happens to a founder's stake when new shares are issued. When an investor puts money in and receives equity, the total number of shares grows and the founder's percentage falls, even though nobody takes the founder's shares away. The same thing happens when an ESOP pool is created for employees.

The trap is that dilution arrives in layers. An investor round takes one slice, an ESOP pool takes another, and a later bridge round takes a third. Founders who track only the first round are often surprised by how little they hold after two or three rounds, especially because the ESOP pool is usually carved out of the founders' side rather than the investor's.

Formula

The formulaFounder Stake = Own Stake × (1 - (Investor % + ESOP %) ÷ 100)

Own stake, investor equity and ESOP pool are all percentages. The result is the founder's stake after the round.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

In the US, a startup can issue options from a pool and expand the pool later with less friction. In India the ESOP pool is usually approved by the board and the shareholders in one go, so founders are encouraged to create a pool big enough for two or three years of hiring at the time of the round.

Indian early rounds are commonly 10% to 20% equity, and the founder's agreement often adds a board seat, information rights and a liquidation preference on top. Those terms do not change the percentage, but they change how much the stake is really worth if the company is sold.

ESOPs in India are also taxed at two points, once when the employee exercises the option and again when the shares are sold, so a pool is not just a slice of the cap table, it is a future tax event for the whole team.

Common Mistakes

  • Planning only for the investor's stake and forgetting the ESOP pool, which dilutes the founders too.
  • Assuming the pool comes out of the investor's share, when most Indian term sheets take it from the founders' side.
  • Ignoring follow-on rounds, which stack on top of the first round and can leave founders with far less than they planned.
