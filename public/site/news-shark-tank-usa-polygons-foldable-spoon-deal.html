Indian Designer's Foldable Spoon Wins $350,000 on Shark Tank USA

Rahul Agarwal sold 10% of Polygons for $350,000 to Lori Greiner and Mindy Kaling. Here is the arithmetic behind that deal, and what it says about valuing a simple product.

Shark TankOctober 21, 2026

A measuring spoon that folds flat just did what most kitchen gadgets never manage: it walked into Shark Tank USA and left with a cheque. Rahul Agarwal, founder of Polygons, took an origami-inspired design to season 18, episode 4, and closed a $350,000 deal with Lori Greiner and guest shark Mindy Kaling.

The structure matters more than the headline number, because it is the same structure Indian founders are offered every week on Shark Tank India.

What exactly did he give away?

Agarwal handed over 8% equity plus 2% in advisory shares — 10% in total — for $350,000. That puts the deal at a $3.5 million post-money valuation, or roughly ₹29 crore at the exchange rate used in reporting.

He had asked for $350,000 for 7%, which would have valued Polygons at $5 million. The sharks talked him down. That gap — $5 million asked versus $3.5 million agreed — is 30% of the company's value, and it was decided in a few minutes of conversation.

The two numbers you should always split apart

Every equity deal is two numbers wearing one coat. The amount invested tells you how much cash arrives. The percentage tells you what the company is worth. Confusing them is the most common mistake founders make when they repeat a deal on social media.

Pre-money valuation is the company's worth before the cheque. Post-money is after. Here, the post-money is $3.5 million, so the pre-money is $3.15 million. If a founder quotes only the post-money, they are quietly flattering themselves.

Why a spoon is worth $3.5 million

Because it solved a boring problem well. The product replaces a six-piece measuring spoon set with two folding tools, holds a patent, and had shipped more than 200,000 units to over 110 countries before the episode aired. Twenty-plus design awards helped too.

None of that is glamorous. All of it is the reason the deal happened. Investors do not pay for the idea of a folding spoon. They pay for units already sold, a patent that keeps rivals out, and a founder who shipped before asking for money.

What an Indian founder should take from it

First, know your numbers cold. Agarwal could name his units shipped and his country count on demand. Sharks test that, and fumbling it costs valuation.

Second, decide your floor before you walk in. The asking valuation is an opening bid, not a fact. Having a number below which you will walk is what stops a bad deal from feeling like a win.

Third, remember advisory shares are real shares. That extra 2% was not decoration; it was dilution. Founders often wave it through because it sounds like a formality. It is not.

Ek measuring spoon jo fold ho jaata hai, woh kar gaya jo zyadatar kitchen gadgets kabhi nahi kar paate: Shark Tank USA mein gaya aur cheque le kar nikla. Rahul Agarwal, Polygons ke founder, origami-inspired design le kar season 18, episode 4 mein aaye, aur Lori Greiner aur guest shark Mindy Kaling ke saath $350,000 ka deal kar liya.

Number se zyada deal ka structure important hai, kyunki yahi structure har hafte Shark Tank India mein Indian founders ko milta hai.

Aakhir usne kitna hissa diya?

Agarwal ne 8% equity aur 2% advisory shares diye — total 10% — $350,000 ke liye. Isse deal ki $3.5 million post-money valuation banti hai, yaani takriban ₹29 crore.

Unhone 7% ke liye $350,000 maanga tha, jisse Polygons ki value $5 million hoti. Sharks ne unhe neeche laaya. Yahi gap — $5 million maanga, $3.5 million tay hua — company ki 30% value hai, aur ye kuch minute ki baat-cheet mein tay hua.

Do numbers jo hamesha alag-alag dekho

Har equity deal do numbers ka joda hota hai. Amount batata hai kitna cash aayega. Percentage batata hai company kitne ki hai. In dono ko mila dena sabse aam galti hai jo founders social media par deal dohraate waqt karte hain.

Pre-money valuation cheque se pehle ki value hai. Post-money baad ki. Yahan post-money $3.5 million hai, to pre-money $3.15 million. Agar founder sirf post-money bataye, woh chupke se apni tareef kar raha hai.

Ek chammach $3.5 million ka kyun?

Kyunki usne ek boring problem achhe se solve kiya. Product chhe piece ka set hata kar do folding tools deta hai, patent hai, aur episode se pehle 200,000 se zyada units 110 se zyada deshon mein bhej chuka tha. Bees se zyada design awards ne bhi madad ki.

Ismein kuch bhi glamorous nahi hai. Phir bhi yahi wajah hai ki deal hui. Investors folding spoon ke idea ke paise nahi dete. Woh already biki units, patent jo competitor ko rokta hai, aur us founder ke paise dete hain jisne maangne se pehle ship kar diya.

Indian founder ko isse kya seekhna chahiye

Pehla, apne numbers ratlo. Agarwal turant units aur deshon ki ginti bata sakte the. Sharks yahi test karte hain, aur yahan fumble karne se valuation girti hai.

Doosra, andar ghusne se pehle apna floor tay karo. Asking valuation ek opening bid hai, sach nahi. Ek number jiske neeche aap walk karoge, wahi kharab deal ko jeet jaisa lagne se rokti hai.

Teesra, yaad rakho advisory shares asli shares hote hain. Woh extra 2% decoration nahi thi; woh dilution thi. Founders ise aksar hawa mein chhod dete hain kyunki formality lagti hai. Hai nahi.
