Rupee Falls to 95.22: What It Costs Importers

A third straight day of losses left the rupee at 95.22 against the dollar on October 26. Here is what that adds to an import bill and pays an exporter.

Small BusinessOctober 26, 2026

The rupee fell for the third straight day on Monday, October 26, 2026, settling 29 paise lower at 95.22 against the U.S. dollar. It opened the day at 94.92, touched an intra-day low of 95.26 and closed at 95.22. The fall came on top of Friday's 12 paise slide to 94.93, so the currency has now given up ground in three sessions running.

Why the rupee is falling

Forex traders put the slide down to a stronger dollar, slight gains in U.S. Treasury yields and elevated crude oil prices, with geopolitical tension in West Asia weighing on sentiment. A recovery in the domestic markets cushioned the downside, which is why the rupee finished the day above its worst level rather than at it.

What a weaker rupee costs an importer

A weaker rupee makes every dollar-denominated purchase dearer, and the effect is immediate. Take a $10,000 import order. At Friday's rate of 94.93 it cost ₹9,49,300. At Monday's 95.22 the same order costs ₹9,52,200 — ₹2,900 more for a 29-paise move, and that is before customs duty, freight or insurance are added.

The rule of thumb is easier to remember than the arithmetic: every ₹1 the rupee falls adds ₹10,000 to a $10,000 bill. A shop that imports twice a year can absorb that. A trader placing monthly orders cannot, which is why the rate belongs in the price you quote, not in a footnote.

What it pays an exporter

The same move works in reverse for anyone who earns in dollars. A $10,000 export invoice that fetched ₹9,49,300 on Friday now brings in ₹9,52,200. An exporter whose costs are in rupees and whose price is in dollars therefore sees the margin widen as the rupee weakens, provided the rate has not already been locked with a forward contract.

What to do about it

For a small business the practical answer is to plan against a range rather than a single number. Check whether your margin still holds if the rupee moves another rupee in either direction, because a rate that flatters your numbers today can turn a profitable order into a break-even one next month. Re-run your landed cost with the current rate before you quote, and treat the exchange rate as one more line item that moves, the same way you treat freight or packaging.

None of this is a forecast. The rupee closed at 95.22 on October 26 after opening at 94.92, and where it goes next depends on crude prices, U.S. yields and events no calculator can see. What a calculator can do is tell you the number your margin can survive.

Monday, October 26, 2026 ko rupee teesre din bhi gira aur dollar ke against 29 paise kam hokar 95.22 par band hua. Din ki shuruaat 94.92 par hui, din mein 95.26 tak gira aur 95.22 par band hua. Friday ko bhi 12 paise gir kar 94.93 par band hua tha, to teen session se rupee peeche ja raha hai.

Rupee kyun gir raha hai

Forex traders ke mutabik wajah hai mazboot dollar, US Treasury yields mein halki badhotri aur mehnga crude oil, aur West Asia ka geopolitical tension sentiment par bhaari pad raha hai. Domestic market mein recovery ne nuksan kam kiya, isliye rupee din ke sabse neeche level se upar band hua.

Kamzor rupee importer ko kitna mehnga padta hai

Kamzor rupee har dollar wali kharid ko mehnga kar deta hai, aur asar turant hota hai. Maan lo $10,000 ka import order hai. Friday ke 94.93 par iski cost ₹9,49,300 thi. Monday ke 95.22 par wahi order ₹9,52,200 padta hai — 29 paise ke move par ₹2,900 zyada, aur ye customs duty, freight ya insurance se pehle ki baat hai.

Yaad rakhne ka aasan rule: rupee jitna ₹1 girta hai, $10,000 ke bill par ₹10,000 jud jaata hai. Saal mein do baar import karne wala dukaan ise jhel lega. Har mahine order karne wala nahi, isliye rate ko apne quote ki price mein rakho, footnote mein nahi.

Exporter ko kitna milta hai

Jo dollar mein kamata hai, uske liye yahi move ulta kaam karta hai. Friday ko $10,000 ka export invoice ₹9,49,300 deta tha, ab ₹9,52,200 dega. Jis exporter ki cost rupee mein hai aur price dollar mein, uska margin rupee kamzor hone par badhta hai — bas rate forward contract se pehle se lock na ho.

Iska kya karna hai

Chhote business ke liye practical baat ye hai ki ek number ke bajaye ek range par plan karo. Dekho ki rupee ek rupaya idhar ya udhar move kare to bhi tumhara margin tikta hai ya nahi, kyunki aaj ka rate jo achha lag raha hai wo agle mahine order ko break-even bana sakta hai. Quote karne se pehle current rate par apna landed cost dobara chalao, aur exchange rate ko bhi ek aise line item ki tarah dekho jo move karta hai, jaise freight ya packaging.

Ye koi forecast nahi hai. October 26 ko rupee 94.92 par khula aur 95.22 par band hua, aur aage kya hoga wo crude prices, US yields aur un events par nirbhar karta hai jo koi calculator nahi dekh sakta. Calculator sirf itna bata sakta hai ki tumhara margin kaunsa number jhel sakta hai.
