CAGR: Compound Annual Growth Rate Explained

The one steady growth rate hiding behind any two of your figures.

Growth & CashOctober 31, 2026

If your revenue went from ₹4,000 a month to ₹9,000 a month in two years, how fast did you actually grow? Most owners take the total jump, divide it by the number of years, and call it a day. That answer is usually wrong, and it is wrong in the direction that flatters a shrinking business. The number that fixes it is CAGR — the compound annual growth rate.

This guide explains what CAGR measures, how to work it out from two figures you already have, and where it is the right tool and where it quietly misleads.

What CAGR actually measures

CAGR is the single, steady yearly rate that would take your starting value to your ending value over a given number of years. It answers one question: if my business had grown by the same percentage every single year, what percentage would that be?

It is not the growth you actually saw in any one year. Real growth jumps around — a good Diwali, a slow monsoon, a lost client. CAGR smooths all of that into one comparable number, which is exactly why investors and lenders ask for it.

The CAGR formula, in plain terms

CAGR = (Ending value ÷ Starting value) raised to the power of (1 ÷ number of years), minus 1.

You do not need to memorise the algebra. Three things matter: you need the starting value, the ending value, and the number of years between them. The "raised to the power" part is what turns a total change into a yearly one, and it is the step a simple average skips.

A worked example: ₹4,000 to ₹9,000 in two years

Say a small service business billed ₹4,000 a month at the start and ₹9,000 a month two years later. Here is the arithmetic:

  1. Divide the ending value by the starting value: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹4,000 = 2.25.
  2. Take the square root, because the period is two years: the square root of 2.25 is 1.5.
  3. Subtract 1: 1.5 − 1 = 0.5, which is 50%.

So the CAGR is 50% a year. Check it forward: ₹4,000 grows 50% to ₹6,000 in year one, then 50% again to ₹9,000 in year two. Both ends match, which is the whole point of the measure.

Why a simple average lies to you

Now take a business whose revenue went ₹8,000, then ₹4,000, then back to ₹8,000. It fell 50% in year one and rose 100% in year two. The simple average of those two numbers is 25% a year — a flattering figure for a business that ended exactly where it started.

CAGR gets it right. The ending value equals the starting value, so the ratio is 1, and the CAGR is 0%. Nothing grew. Whenever growth swings, the plain average overshoots and CAGR tells the truth, because it only ever looks at where you began and where you finished.

CAGR is not a promise about the future

A common mistake is to read CAGR as a forecast. It is not. It is a description of the past, and a business that grew 50% a year for two years will almost never grow 50% a year for the next two. Small bases grow fast; large ones do not. A base of a few thousand rupees can double on one good client; a base of several lakh cannot.

Use CAGR to compare, not to predict. It is fair to say "this line grew 50% a year while that one grew 12%". It is not fair to say "so it will grow 50% next year".

Where a small business should use CAGR

  • Comparing two years of sales when one year was unusually good or bad, so a raw percentage jump misleads.
  • Pitching to an investor or lender, who will ask for a growth rate that ignores a single lucky quarter.
  • Tracking one customer or one product line over several years to see which is genuinely compounding.
  • Checking a claim — "we grew 100% last year" means something very different on a small base than on a large one.

How to work out your own CAGR

You need only two numbers you already have: what the figure was at the start, and what it is now. Put them into the formula, divide by the years in between, and you have a growth rate you can compare against any other period or any other business.

One last caution: keep the period honest. Two years is two years, not "about two and a half". Stretching the period lowers the CAGR and flatters a slow year; shrinking it does the opposite. The number is only as good as the dates you feed it.

Agar aapki kamai do saal mein ₹4,000 mahine se ₹9,000 mahine ho gayi, to asal mein aap kitni tezi se badhe? Zyadatar log kul badhotri lekar saalon ki sankhya se bhaag de dete hain aur use dar maan lete hain. Yeh jawab aksar galat hota hai, aur galat bhi us taraf jo ghatate karobar ko badha-chadhaakar dikhata hai. Ise theek karne wala aankda hai CAGR — chakravriddhi saalana vriddhi dar.

Yeh guide bataate hai ki CAGR kya naapata hai, aapke paas pehle se maujood do aankdon se ise kaise nikalein, aur yeh kahan sahe hai aur kahan chupchaap bhatakaata hai.

CAGR asal mein kya naapata hai

CAGR woh ek, sthir saalana dar hai jo aapke shuruaati aankde ko tay saalon mein aapke aakhire aankde tak pahuncha dege. Yeh ek hi sawal ka jawab dete hai: agar mera karobar har saal ek hi pratishat se badhta, to woh pratishat kya hota?

Yeh aapki kisi ek saal ki asli badhotri nahi hai. Asli badhotri upar-neeche hoti rahate hai — ek achhi divaale, ek sust maanasoon, ek ganvaaya hua graahak. CAGR in sabko ek tulaneey aankde mein badal dete hai, iseelie niveshak aur bank ise maangate hain.

CAGR ka formula, aasan bhasha mein

CAGR = (aakhire aankda ÷ shuruaati aankda) ki ghaat (1 ÷ saalon ki sankhya), ghata 1.

Aapko yeh ganit yaad karne ki zaroorat nahi hai. Teen cheezein maayne rakhate hain: shuruaati aankda, aakhire aankda, aur dono ke beech ke saal. "Ghaat" wala hissa hi kul badlav ko saalana badlav banata hai, aur yahi charan saadhaaran ausat chhod deta hai.

Ek udaharan: do saal mein ₹4,000 se ₹9,000

Maan lijiye ek chhota service karobar shuru mein ₹4,000 mahine ka bil banata tha aur do saal baad ₹9,000 mahine ka. Hisaab aisa hai:

  1. Aakhire aankde ko shuruaati aankde se bhaag dein: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹4,000 = 2.25.
  2. Vargamool nikalein, kyunki avadhi do saal hai: 2.25 ka vargamool 1.5 hai.
  3. 1 ghatayein: 1.5 − 1 = 0.5, yani 50%.

To CAGR 50% saalana hai. Ise aage jodkar dekhein: ₹4,000 pehle saal 50% badhakar ₹6,000 banata hai, phir 50% badhakar doosare saal ₹9,000. Dono sire milate hain, aur yahi is naap ka pura matlab hai.

Saadhaaran ausat aapko dhokha kyon deta hai

Ab ek aisa karobar lijiye jisake kamai ₹8,000 rahi, phir ₹4,000, aur phir vaapas ₹8,000. Pehle saal yeh 50% gire aur doosare saal 100% badhe. In dono aankdon ka saadhaaran ausat 25% saalana banata hai — ek aise karobar ke liye badha-chadhaakar aankda jo asal mein wahin khada hai jahaan se shuru hua tha.

CAGR sahe jawab dete hai. Aakhire aankda shuruaati ke baraabar hai, isliye anupaat 1 hai, aur CAGR 0% hai. Kuch bhi nahi badha. Jab bhi badhotri upar-neeche hoti hai, saadhaaran ausat badha-chadhaakar batata hai aur CAGR sach kahate hai, kyunki yeh sirf dekhti hai ki aap kahan se chale aur kahan pahunche.

CAGR bhavishy ka vaada nahi hai

Ek aam galat CAGR ko bhavishyavaane maan lena hai. Yeh nahi hai. Yeh beete samay ka byaura hai, aur jo karobar do saal 50% saalana badha, woh agle do saal lagabhag kabhi 50% saalana nahi badhega. Chhota aadhaar tez badhta hai; bada nahi. Kuch hazaar rupaye ka aadhaar ek achhe graahak se dugana ho sakta hai, kai laakh ka nahi.

CAGR ko tulna ke liye istemal keejie, bhavishyavaane ke liye nahi. Yeh kehna theek hai ki "yeh laain 50% saalana badhe jabaki woh 12%". Yeh kehna theek nahi ki "to agle saal yeh 50% badhege".

Chhota karobar CAGR kahan istemal kare

  • Do saal ki bikri ki tulna karte samay, jab ek saal asaamaany roop se achha ya kharaab raha ho, taaki kachcha pratishat bhatakaae nahi.
  • Niveshak ya bank ke saamane pesh karte samay, jo aise vriddhi dar maangenge jo ek bhaagyashaale timaahe ko na gine.
  • Ek graahak ya ek product laain par nazar rakhte samay, yeh dekhane ke liye ki kaun sach mein badh raha hai.
  • Kisi daave ki jaanch karte samay — "hum pichhale saal 100% badhe" ka matlab chhote aadhaar par kuch aur hota hai, bade par kuch aur.

Apni CAGR khud kaise nikalein

Aapko sirf do aankde chahiye jo aapke paas pehle se hain: shuru mein kya tha, aur ab kya hai. Inhein formoole mein daalie, beech ke saalon se bhaag deejie, aur aapke paas ek vriddhi dar hai jisake tulna aap kisi bhi avadhi ya karobar se kar sakte hain.

Aakhire saavadhaane: avadhi imandar rakhie. Do saal ka matlab do saal hai, "lagabhag dhaaee" nahi. Avadhi kheenchane se CAGR ghatate hai aur sust saal badha-chadhaakar dikhta hai; ghataane se ulta. Aankda utna hi achha hai jitane achhi aapki taareekhen.
