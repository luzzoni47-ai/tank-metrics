Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between gross margin and net margin?

Gross margin is profit after direct costs only, while net margin is profit after all operating expenses. A brand can have a 40% gross margin and a 10% net margin at the same time.

Which margin do investors look at in India?

Serious investors look at both, but net margin decides the conversation. A strong gross margin with a negative net margin means the business loses money on every sale.

What is gross margin vs net margin calculator india?

A gross margin vs net margin calculator for India separates two numbers that founders routinely confuse. Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of making or buying what you sold, divided by revenue. Net margin is what remains after you also subtract every operating expense, such as salaries, rent, marketing, software and interest. Gross margin tells you whether the product itself is profitable. Net margin tells you whether the company is.

The reason the distinction matters is that a product can carry a beautiful gross margin and the business can still bleed. A D2C brand with a 40% gross margin sounds healthy, but if rent, salaries and ad spend eat 45% of revenue, the net margin is negative and every extra order loses money. Sharks read the second number first because it is the one that survives contact with reality.

On Tank Metrics the inputs are plain ₹ figures you already know from your profit and loss statement: revenue, cost of goods sold and operating expenses. There is no dollar conversion and no US template in the way, and the answer comes back as both a rupee amount and a percentage, which is how an Indian chartered accountant expects to see it.

Formula

The formulaGross Margin = ((Revenue - COGS) ÷ Revenue) × 100 and Net Margin = ((Revenue - COGS - Expenses) ÷ Revenue) × 100

All three inputs in ₹ for the same period. Gross margin ignores overheads while net margin includes every operating expense.

Worked Example (in ₹ Crores)

A D2C brand earns ₹10 lakh of monthly revenue and spends ₹3 lakh on operating expenses. Here is how the two margins move as the cost of goods sold changes.

₹10 lakh₹5 lakh50%20%
₹10 lakh₹7 lakh30%0%
₹10 lakh₹8 lakh20%-10%

At a 30% gross margin the brand only breaks even, and at a 20% gross margin it loses money every single month. Sharks look at the last column, not the first, because net margin is the number that decides survival.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

An American margin template usually ignores GST, because sales tax works differently there. In India the tax sits inside the price you charge, so a founder who forgets to strip it out will overstate both revenue and margin. Working from GST-exclusive figures is the Indian habit that keeps the comparison honest.

Indian D2C and food businesses often carry a gross margin that looks strong in a pitch deck but a net margin that is thin, because advertising, delivery and marketplace commissions are heavy. Investors who have watched Indian consumer brands know this pattern, which is why they ask for net margin and not just gross.

Finally, Indian founders frequently mix up profit and cash. A positive net margin can still sit locked in unpaid invoices for 60 or 90 days, so a margin calculator is only half the picture and the runway calculator is the other half.

Common Mistakes

  • Reporting revenue that still includes GST. Strip the tax out first, or both margins will look better than they really are.
  • Lumping marketing spend into cost of goods sold. Advertising is an operating expense, and mixing the two hides how thin the true net margin is.
  • Comparing your net margin to a US company in a different category. Indian consumer brands carry marketplace commissions and delivery costs that a US software firm never sees.
