What Is the Cash Conversion Cycle?

Stock days plus receivable days minus payable days — the waiting time that empties a bank account.

Growth & CashNovember 8, 2026

A shop can sell well every single day and still run out of cash to pay its bills. The reason is that the money does not come back the moment the sale is made. First you pay for the stock, then you sell it, then you wait for the customer or the marketplace to pay you. That waiting time is the whole problem, and it has a name: the cash conversion cycle.

This guide explains what the cash conversion cycle measures, works through a small example you can follow with your own numbers, and shows the two ways to shorten it.

Three waiting times, added together

The cycle adds up three stretches of time:

  • Stock days. How long your inventory sits on the shelf before it is sold.
  • Receivable days. How long after the sale you wait to actually get paid.
  • Payable days. How long you get to pay your own supplier.

The first two cost you money, because your cash is locked up. The third helps you, because your supplier is funding you for that long. The cycle is stock days plus receivable days, minus payable days.

A worked example

Say a small shop sells about ₹9,000 of goods a month. It holds ₹6,000 of stock, which at that rate is roughly 20 days of stock. It has ₹3,000 of unpaid customer invoices, which is about 10 days of sales. Its supplier gives 15 days to pay. The cycle works out as:

  1. Stock days: about 20 days.
  2. Receivable days: about 10 days.
  3. Payable days: 15 days.
  4. Cycle: 20 + 10 − 15 = 15 days.

That shop's money is tied up for 15 days on every sale. Cut the stock holding to ₹3,000, or about 10 days, and the cycle falls to 5 days. No extra sales are needed for that to happen; the same turnover simply needs less cash behind it.

Why a profitable shop still runs dry

Profit and cash are not the same number. Profit counts a sale when it is made; cash arrives weeks later. A shop that grows fast makes more sales, which means it buys more stock and carries more unpaid invoices at the same time. The faster it grows, the more cash it needs, which is why the busiest month can be the one that empties the bank account.

Two ways to shorten the cycle

The first is to speed up collection: ask for a deposit on large orders, invoice the day you ship instead of at month end, and follow up on the oldest unpaid bills first. The second is to slow down payment without damaging the relationship: negotiate longer credit with your main supplier, or buy smaller quantities more often so less money sits in stock. Every day you remove from the cycle is a day of cash you get to keep.

Watch the cycle, not just the sales

A rising sales figure hides a growing problem if the cycle is stretching at the same time. Check the three numbers once a month, and treat a lengthening cycle as a warning even when the sales line looks healthy.

Koi dukaan har din achhi bikri karke bhi apne bil chukaane ke liye paise se khaale ho sakate hai. Vajah yeh hai ki bikri hote hi paisa vaapas nahi aata. Pehle aap maal ke paise dete hain, phir maal bikta hai, aur usake baad graahak ya maarketaples ke paise aane ka intazaar karna padta hai. Yahi intazaar poore samasya hai, aur isaka naam hai kaish kanvarzan saaikal.

Yeh guide bataate hai ki yeh chakr kya naapata hai, ek chhota udaharan dikhati hai jise aap apne aankdon se dohara sakte hain, aur yeh bataate hai ki ise chhota karne ke do tarike kya hain.

Teen intazaar, jo jode jaate hain

Yeh chakr teen avadhiyon ko jodata hai:

  • Stok ke din. Maal bikne se pehle kitne din raik par pada rehta hai.
  • Udhaar ke din. Bikri ke baad paisa milne mein kitne din lagte hain.
  • Bhugataan ke din. Aapko apne saplaayar ko paise dene ke liye kitne din milate hain.

Pehle do aapka paisa rok lete hain, isliye ve mahange padate hain. Teesara aapke paksh mein hai, kyunki utane samay tak aapka saplaayar aapko paisa de raha hota hai. Chakr baraabar hota hai stok ke din jama udhaar ke din, ghata bhugataan ke din.

Ek udaharan

Maan lijiye ek chhoti dukaan mahine mein lagabhag ₹9,000 ka maal bechti hai. Usake paas ₹6,000 ka stok hai, jo is dar par kareeb 20 din ka maal hai. Usake graahakon se ₹3,000 ki bikri ka paisa aana baaki hai, yani lagabhag 10 din ki bikri. Saplaayar 15 din ka samay deta hai. Chakr is tarah banata hai:

  1. Stok ke din: kareeb 20 din.
  2. Udhaar ke din: kareeb 10 din.
  3. Bhugataan ke din: 15 din.
  4. Chakr: 20 + 10 − 15 = 15 din.

Yani har bikri par is dukaan ka paisa 15 din bandha rehta hai. Stok ghataakar ₹3,000 kar dein, yani kareeb 10 din ka, to chakr ghatakar 5 din rah jaata hai. Isake liye ek bhi atirikt bikri ki zaroorat nahi, usi karobar ko utane hi kam paise mein chalaaya ja sakta hai.

Munafa kamaane wali dukaan bhi khaale kyon ho jaati hai

Munafa aur paisa ek hi aankda nahi hain. Munafa bikri hote hi gin liya jaata hai, lekin paisa hafton baad aata hai. Tezi se badhti dukaan zyada bechti hai, yani saath hi zyada maal khareedate hai aur zyada udhaar chhodti hai. Jitane tez badhat, utane zyada paise ki zaroorat, iseelie sabse vyast mahina hi woh mahina ho sakta hai jo khaata khaale kar de.

Chakr chhota karne ke do tarike

Pehla tarika hai vasoole tez karna: bade order par kuch edavaans maangie, bil mahine ke aakhir ki jagah bhejane ke din hi bana deejie, aur sabse puraane bakaaya bilon ko pehle vasoolie. Doosara tarika hai bhugataan dheema karna, bina rishta bigaade: apne mukhya saplaayar se lamba udhaar tay keejie, ya thoda-thoda aur baar-baar khareedie taaki stok mein kam paisa phansa rahe. Chakr se aap jo har din hataate hain, woh paisa aapke paas bacha rehta hai.

Bikri nahi, chakr dekhie

Badhti bikri ke aankde ke peechhe badhti samasya chhip sakate hai, agar saath hi chakr bhi lamba ho raha ho. Mahine mein ek baar teenon aankde dekhie, aur chakr lamba hone ko chetaavane maanie, chaahe bikri ki laain kitni bhi achhi dikhe.
