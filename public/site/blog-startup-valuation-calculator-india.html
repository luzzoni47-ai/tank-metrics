Frequently asked questions

Which valuation method is best for an Indian startup?

Use a revenue or profit multiple once you have revenue, and a scorecard method before that. The best answer is a range from two methods, not a single number.

What revenue multiple do Indian startups get?

It varies by sector. Software and subscription businesses often get higher multiples than D2C, and profitable businesses are valued on profit. Treat any multiple as the start of a negotiation.

What is startup valuation calculator india?

A startup valuation calculator for India takes the numbers an Indian founder actually has, revenue, profit and growth, and turns them into a range instead of a single figure. Early-stage businesses rarely have a defensible single valuation, so the honest output is a band: a low number that a cautious investor would accept and a high number that a bullish one might.

The five methods used here cover the whole journey of a company. Pre-revenue teams use the scorecard or Berkus style approach, which scores the team, the product and the market. Once revenue exists, a revenue multiple becomes the main tool for software, D2C and services businesses. Once profit exists, a profit multiple or a discounted cash flow takes over.

On Tank Metrics all inputs and outputs are in ₹ Lakhs and ₹ Crores. There is no dollar toggle, because an Indian founder comparing a US comparable at ten million dollars needs that figure written as roughly ₹8 crore before it means anything.

Formula

The formulaValuation = Revenue × Multiple, blended with Profit × Multiple

Revenue and profit in ₹ crore and the multiple as a plain number. Blend the results to get a defensible range.

When the two methods disagree wildly, the business is either overvalued on revenue or unusually profitable, and that gap is exactly what a shark or a venture fund will question. A founder who walks in with both numbers ready, and can explain why the multiple is what it is, spends far less time defending the valuation and more time closing the deal.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

A US valuation tool usually starts from a comparable listed company and a discounted cash flow model. Indian early-stage founders rarely have a five-year forecast they trust, so Indian angel networks lean on simpler methods such as the scorecard, where the team and the market are scored against a benchmark valuation.

Indian angel rounds commonly take 10% to 20% equity, and an ESOP pool of 8% to 12% is usually created at the same time. A valuation that ignores the pool will overstate how much of the company the founders really keep.

If a company raises from a venture fund, the round may also need a valuation certificate from a merchant banker for regulatory filings. So the number this calculator produces is a negotiation range, not a filed figure. Indian founders should treat it as the opening of a discussion, then let a chartered accountant check the final number before any term sheet is signed.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a listed company's multiple for a tiny startup without any size discount.
  • Choosing a single method and presenting its answer as the valuation instead of giving a range.
  • Leaving the ESOP pool out of the model, so the founder's real stake looks larger than it will be.
