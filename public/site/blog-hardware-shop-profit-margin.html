Hardware Shop Profit Margin: What You Keep

A ₹500 sale, the rent it has to cover, and the dead stock that quietly eats the rest.

Profit & MarginsNovember 13, 2026

A hardware shop sells paint, pipes, fittings, tools and the hundred small things a house or a contractor needs. It is a steady business in almost every Indian town, and it is also a business that looks more profitable than it is. The owner counts the margin on a tin of paint, but the rent, the dead stock at the back and the contractor who pays after two months all come out of the same pocket.

This guide works one small hardware shop through the two numbers that matter: the margin on a single sale, and what is left of the month after the fixed costs. Then it shows the number of sales that covers the shop, and the quiet costs a grocery does not carry.

A hardware shop earns two margins, not one

The gap between what a customer pays and what the goods cost is the gross margin, and it is the number owners quote. What survives the rent and the bills is the net margin, and it is the only one you can spend. A shop can look healthy on gross margin and still make nothing, because the fixed costs are paid in full whether the counter is busy or empty.

A ₹500 sale, worked through

Say one typical sale — a tin of paint, a few fittings and a roll of tape — sells for ₹500. The goods inside it were bought for ₹400. So the sale earns ₹100 before the shop's own costs.

  • Selling price: ₹500
  • Cost of the goods: ₹400
  • Gross profit: ₹500 − ₹400 = ₹100
  • Gross margin: ₹100 ÷ ₹500 = 20%

Twenty percent is normal for hardware, and it is not the owner's money yet.

The fixed costs that arrive every month

Fixed costs come whether you sell one tin or a hundred:

  • Shop rent: ₹6,000
  • Electricity and water: ₹1,000
  • Helper's salary: ₹2,000
  • Total fixed cost per month: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 a month is the hurdle. Until the sales cover it, every rupee of gross profit is only paying the shop back, not the owner.

The break-even: how many sales a month

Break-even is the number of sales whose gross profit exactly equals the fixed costs. Divide one by the other:

  • Fixed cost per month: ₹9,000
  • Gross profit per sale: ₹100
  • Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹100 = 90 sales a month

90 sales a month is about 3 a day. Sell fewer than that and the shop is still short; the first 90 sales of the month were never profit, they were the rent.

A slow month, and what it costs

Sell 110 sales and the month works: 20 sales above break-even, which is ₹2,000 clear. Sell 70 instead — a shut market week, a festival lull — and the same shop brings ₹7,000 of gross profit against ₹9,000 of fixed costs. That is a loss of ₹2,000, and the rent was still paid in full.

Two costs a hardware shop carries that a grocery does not

Hardware is not grocery. Two costs sit quietly on this business:

  • Dead stock. Fittings, sizes and shades go out of demand while the box sits on the shelf. That cash is already spent, and a shelf full of slow stock is money the shop cannot use.
  • Contractor credit. A painter or a plumber buys on account and pays after the job. The sale is booked, the goods are gone, and the cash is still with the customer for a month or two.

What actually lifts the margin

None of these needs more customers; each one lifts the gross profit on a sale you already make:

  1. Stock the fast-moving sizes and shades, and clear the slow ones at cost rather than let them age.
  2. Sell the fitting, the tape and the brush with the tin. The add-ons carry a higher margin than the paint.
  3. Put a date on contractor credit, and stop supplying an account that never clears.
  4. Charge for delivery and cutting. It is work, and it is not free.

The one number to watch each week

At the end of each week, add up the gross profit and hold it against the month's fixed cost. If the first three weeks have not yet covered ₹9,000, the last week is not profit, it is the shop paying itself back. Knowing that on the twenty-first day, not the thirtieth, is what separates a shop that survives a slow month from one that does not.

Haardaveyar ki dukaan pent, paaip, phiting, auzaar aur ghar ya mistre ko chahiye wali sau chhoti cheezein bechti hai. Bhaarat ke lagabhag har kasbe mein yeh ek sthir karobar hai, aur yeh woh karobar bhi hai jo dikhane mein jitna munaafedaar lagta hai usse kam hai. Maalik pent ke ek tin par munafa ginata hai, lekin kiraya, peechhe pada maal aur do mahine baad paisa dene wala mistre — sab usi ek jeb se nikalte hain.

Yeh guide ek chhoti haardaveyar dukaan ke do aankade nikaalate hai: ek bikri par hua munafa, aur mahine ke aakhir mein tay kharch kaatane ke baad kya bacha. Phir woh sankhya dikhati hai jo dukaan ka kharch nikaal dete hai, aur ve chupchaap pade kharch jo kiraana dukaan par nahi hote.

Haardaveyar dukaan ka munafa do hote hain, ek nahi

Graahak jo deta hai aur maal jis daam par aaya uska farq gross margin hai, aur yahi sankhya maalik batata hai. Kiraya aur bil kaatane ke baad jo bachta hai woh net margin hai, aur sirf wahi kharch kiya ja sakta hai. Dukaan gross margin par swasth dikh sakate hai aur phir bhi kuch na kamaye, kyunki tay kharch pura dena padta hai, chaahe kaauntar bhara ho ya khaale.

₹500 ki ek bikri, pura hisaab

Maan lijiye ek aam bikri — ek tin pent, kuch phiting aur ek rol tep — ₹500 ki hoti hai. Usamen rakha maal ₹400 mein aaya tha. Yani dukaan ke kharch se pehle us bikri par ₹100 banate hain.

  • Bikri keemat: ₹500
  • Maal ki lagat: ₹400
  • Gross profit: ₹500 − ₹400 = ₹100
  • Gross margin: ₹100 ÷ ₹500 = 20%

Haardaveyar mein bees pratishat samanya hai, aur yeh bhi abhe maalik ka paisa nahi hai.

Har mahine aane wale tay kharch

Tay kharch ve hain jo tab bhi aate hain jab aap ek tin bechen ya sau:

  • Dukaan ka kiraya: ₹6,000
  • Bijli aur paani: ₹1,000
  • Sahaayak ka vetan: ₹2,000
  • Mahine ka kul tay kharch: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 + ₹2,000 = ₹9,000

₹9,000 mahine ki rukaavat hai. Jab tak bikri ise nahi nikaalate, har rupaya dukaan ko hi vaapas kar raha hai, maalik ko nahi.

Break-even: mahine mein kitni bikri

Break-even woh sankhya hai jisake gross profit theek tay kharch ke baraabar ho. Ek ko doosare se baantie:

  • Mahine ka tay kharch: ₹9,000
  • Har bikri par gross profit: ₹100
  • Break-even: ₹9,000 ÷ ₹100 = mahine mein 90 bikri

Mahine mein 90 bikri yani roz kareeb 3. Isase kam bechen to dukaan ab bhi kam hai; mahine ki pehli 90 bikri kabhi munafa theen hi nahi, ve kiraya theen.

Sust mahina, aur uski keemat

110 bikri bechen to mahina chal jaata hai: break-even se 20 bikri upar, yani ₹2,000 saaf. Iski jagah 70 bechen — bazaar band ka hafta, tyohaar ki suste — to wahi dukaan ₹9,000 ke tay kharch ke saamane ₹7,000 ki gross profit laate hai. Yani ₹2,000 ka nuksan, aur kiraya phir bhi pura dena pada.

Do kharch jo haardaveyar dukaan par hote hain, kiraana par nahi

Haardaveyar kiraana nahi hai. Do kharch is karobar par chupchaap baithe rehte hain:

  • Mrit maal. Phiting, saaiz aur rang ka chalan badal jaata hai aur dibba shelf par pada rehta hai. Woh paisa kharch ho chuka hai, aur dheeme maal se bhare shelf woh paisa hai jo dukaan istemal nahi kar sakate.
  • Mistre ka udhaar. Pentar ya plambar udhaar leta hai aur kaam pura hone par paisa deta hai. Bikri darj ho chuke hai, maal ja chuka hai, aur paisa ek-do mahine tak graahak ke paas hi hai.

Munafa asal mein kaise badhta hai

Inmein se kisi ke liye zyada graahak nahi chahiye; har ek us bikri par gross profit badhaata hai jo aap pehle se kar rahe hain:

  1. Tez chalane wale saaiz aur rang rakhein, aur dheeme maal ko lagat par nikalein, sadane dein nahi.
  2. Tin ke saath phiting, tep aur brash bhi bechen. In jodon par pent se zyada margin hota hai.
  3. Mistre ke udhaar par taareekh likhen, aur jo khaata kabhi saaf nahi hota use maal dena band karein.
  4. Dileevare aur kataaee ka paisa len. Woh kaam hai, aur woh muft nahi hai.

Har hafte dekhane wali ek sankhya

Har hafte ke aakhir mein gross profit jodein aur use mahine ke tay kharch ke saamane rakhein. Agar teen hafte mein ₹9,000 ab tak nahi nikale, to aakhire hafta munafa nahi hai, woh dukaan ka khud ko vaapas karna hai. Yeh ikkeesaven din pata hona, teesaven din nahi — yahi tay karta hai ki sust mahina dukaan jhel paaege ya nahi.
