Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate COGS in rupees?

Use COGS = Opening Inventory + Purchases - Closing Inventory. With ₹2 lakh opening, ₹8 lakh purchases and ₹3 lakh closing, the COGS is ₹7 lakh.

Why is closing stock subtracted from COGS?

Because those goods were not sold in the period. Subtracting them leaves only the cost of goods that actually went out the door.

How does COGS affect gross margin?

Gross profit is revenue minus COGS, so a higher COGS means a lower gross margin. COGS is the single biggest driver of your margin.

Skippi Ice Pops pitched on Shark Tank India with a cost of goods sold of about ₹4 a pop, a wholesale price of ₹7 and an MRP of ₹20, which left a healthy gross margin on every unit. The sharks read that COGS line before they read anything else, because it decides how much money is left to actually run the company. Get COGS wrong and every margin downstream is wrong too. What is cogs calculator india?

A COGS calculator for India finds the true cost of the goods you actually sold in a period, not the cost of everything you bought. The formula is opening inventory plus purchases minus closing inventory. The closing stock is subtracted because those goods were not sold yet, so charging them to this period would overstate your cost and understate your profit.

Cost of goods sold is the first line of any honest profit calculation. Revenue minus COGS gives gross profit, and gross profit divided by revenue gives gross margin. If COGS is inflated, the gross margin looks worse and you may wrongly raise prices. If COGS is understated, the margin looks flattering and you may scale a product that never really made money.

Why Indian Calculation is Different (US vs India)

In India, inventory is often valued with GST on top, and founders sometimes record the tax-inclusive purchase value as their cost. That inflates COGS and drags the gross margin down. Use GST-exclusive purchase values so the cost of goods sold matches the revenue you book.

Indian trading and manufacturing businesses also deal with stock that is damaged, expired or returned. Those write-offs are a real cost and belong in the period, but many founders leave them out of closing stock and quietly overstate profit. A clean stock count is the foundation of a clean COGS number.

For a small company, the tax department and a chartered accountant both look closely at the closing stock figure, because it directly changes taxable profit. Keeping opening stock, purchases and closing stock consistent across months is what keeps a GST filing and a profit and loss statement in agreement.

Common Mistakes

  • Entering GST-inclusive purchase values, which inflates COGS and makes the gross margin look worse than it really is.
  • Guessing the closing stock instead of counting it. A rough closing figure changes COGS and therefore changes taxable profit.
  • Forgetting damaged, expired or returned stock. If it cannot be sold, it should not sit in closing inventory as if it still had full value.
